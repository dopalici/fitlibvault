### B14. Powerlifting

## Quick Takeaway
Powerlifting is a strength sport focused on maximizing total weight lifted across three competition lifts: squat, bench press, and deadlift, requiring specialized training emphasizing technique mastery, progressive overload, and strategic periodization.

## Key Points
- Train the "big three" lifts (squat, bench, deadlift) as primary movements with assistance exercises targeting weak points
- Focus heavily on technique refinement - competition standards are strict and proper form maximizes weight lifted safely
- Use periodized programming to manage high training loads and peak for competitions
- Allow adequate recovery between intense sessions due to high neural and systemic demands
- Consider hiring a qualified coach for technique development and programming guidance

## Deep Dive

### Competition Structure
Powerlifting competitions center on three barbell lifts performed in sequence: back squat, bench press, and deadlift (conventional or sumo stance). Each lifter gets three attempts per lift, with the highest successful attempt counting toward their total. Specific rules govern technique requirements including squat depth, bench press pauses, and lockout standards.

### Training Principles

#### Specificity and Skill Development
Powerlifting training must prioritize the competition lifts themselves, following the [[A20. Principle of Specificity (SAID)]] principle. Unlike general strength training, powerlifting requires mastering highly technical movement patterns under maximal loads. Training should emphasize competition commands, timing, and precise technique execution based on [[B4. Strength Training Principles]].

#### Progressive Overload Application
Systematic load progression on the competition lifts forms the foundation of powerlifting training using [[B1. Progressive Overload]]. This involves gradually increasing weight, volume, or intensity over time while maintaining technical proficiency. Advanced lifters may need more nuanced approaches due to diminishing returns at higher strength levels.

#### Periodization Strategies
Competition powerlifting demands sophisticated periodization to manage training stress and peak performance through [[A25. Principle of Periodization]] and [[K2. Advanced Periodization Strategies]]. Common models include:
- **Linear periodization:** Gradual intensity increase with volume decrease
- **Daily undulating periodization:** Varying intensity/volume within weeks
- **Block periodization:** Focused training phases with specific adaptations
- **Conjugate method:** Simultaneous development of multiple strength qualities

#### Assistance Exercise Selection
Supplementary exercises target weak points in the competition lifts and build supporting muscle mass. Effective choices include overhead press for bench press lockout, Romanian deadlifts for hip hinge strength, and pause squats for bottom position strength. Exercise selection should address individual biomechanical limitations and strength imbalances.

### Technical Considerations
Powerlifting technique differs significantly from general fitness training. Competition standards require specific depths, pauses, and lockout positions that must be practiced consistently following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]. Technique refinement often yields greater performance gains than pure strength increases, especially for intermediate lifters.

## Research Evidence

**Swinton PA, et al. (2011).** A biomechanical analysis of straight and hexagonal barbell deadlifts using submaximal loads. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 25(7), 2000-2009. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181e73f87
- Key finding: Straight barbell deadlifts produce greater knee flexion moments and quadriceps activation compared to hexagonal barbells
- Study type: Observational biomechanical analysis
- Evidence quality: Medium (controlled laboratory conditions, small sample size)

**Ferland PM, Comtois AS. (2019).** Classic Powerlifting Performance: A Systematic Review. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 33 Suppl 1, S194-S201. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002312
- Key finding: Competition performance correlates with training volume, technique proficiency, and periodization strategies
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive analysis of multiple studies)

**Hales ME. (2010).** Improving the deadlift: Understanding biomechanical constraints and physiological adaptations to resistance exercise. *Strength & Conditioning Journal*, 32(4), 44-51. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0b013e3181e5e6d5
- Key finding: Deadlift performance depends on hip extensor strength, posterior chain coordination, and optimal bar path mechanics
- Study type: Technical review
- Evidence quality: Medium (expert analysis, limited empirical data)

## Practical Applications
- **Training frequency:** Train each competition lift 1-3 times per week based on experience level and recovery capacity
- **Rep ranges:** Emphasize 1-5 reps for strength, 6-12 reps for hypertrophy assistance work
- **Competition preparation:** Begin 12-16 week peaking cycles for major competitions
- **Equipment familiarity:** Train with competition-standard equipment including proper barbells, benches, and platforms using [[B8. Free Weights (Barbells, Dumbbells)]]
- **Opener selection:** Choose competition openers at 90-95% of current max to ensure successful first attempts
- **Meet simulation:** Practice competition timing, commands, and warm-up protocols during training

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Neglecting technique for load:** Prioritizing weight on the bar over movement quality leads to competition failures and injuries
⚠️ **Inadequate recovery:** Training too frequently at high intensities without sufficient rest periods per [[B6. Rest and Recovery for Optimal Adaptation (Resistance Training Focus)]]
⚠️ **Poor competition lift ratios:** Focusing disproportionately on one lift while neglecting others creates imbalanced totals
⚠️ **Ignoring mobility work:** Insufficient attention to movement quality and joint health limits performance potential
⚠️ **Inconsistent competition simulation:** Failing to practice meet conditions, timing, and commands during training
