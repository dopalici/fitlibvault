### **EXL-35: Dumbbell Overhead Triceps Extension (Single & Double Arm)**

## Quick Takeaway
An isolation exercise that targets the triceps with emphasis on the long head through overhead positioning, performed by extending the elbow(s) while holding a dumbbell behind the head.

## Key Points
- Overhead position places the long head of the triceps under greater stretch and activation
- Can be performed single or double arm, seated or standing
- Requires adequate shoulder flexion mobility for safe execution
- Upper arm position must remain stable throughout the movement
- Seated variation provides better stability for beginners

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Description
The dumbbell overhead triceps extension is an isolation exercise targeting the triceps brachii muscle group. The overhead position creates a unique training stimulus by placing the long head of the triceps under significant stretch due to the shoulder flexion position. This exercise can be performed with one or both arms and in seated or standing positions.

### Muscle Activation
**Primary Movers:** The triceps brachii serves as the primary mover, with the long head receiving enhanced activation due to the overhead arm position. The long head originates from the scapula and crosses both the shoulder and elbow joints, making it particularly responsive to exercises performed in shoulder flexion.

**Secondary Movers:** The anconeus assists with elbow extension.

**Stabilizers:** Core musculature provides stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]], while scapular stabilizers and rotator cuff muscles maintain proper shoulder position throughout the movement.

### Setup and Execution
**Double Arm Version:** Begin seated or standing with core engaged. Hold one dumbbell vertically with both hands cupped under the top weight plate (diamond grip). Raise the dumbbell directly overhead with arms fully extended.

**Single Arm Version:** Hold one dumbbell in one hand and raise it directly overhead. The non-working hand can provide elbow support if needed for stability.

**Movement Pattern:** Maintain vertical upper arm position close to the head while lowering the dumbbell behind the head through elbow flexion. Control the descent to achieve a full stretch in the triceps, then extend the elbow(s) to return to the starting position while focusing on triceps contraction.

## Practical Applications

### Programming Guidelines
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 repetitions with light weight
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 8-12 repetitions with moderate weight
- **Advanced:** 4-5 sets of 6-10 repetitions with heavier loads

### Load Progression
Start with a weight that allows perfect form throughout the full range of motion. Progress by increasing weight in 2.5-5 lb increments when you can complete all sets with 2+ reps in reserve.

### Tempo Recommendations
- 2-3 second eccentric (lowering) phase to maximize stretch
- Brief pause at bottom position
- 1-2 second concentric (lifting) phase

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Excessive elbow flare:** Allowing elbows to drift outward reduces triceps activation and increases shoulder stress

⚠️ **Moving upper arms:** Using shoulder movement instead of isolated elbow extension reduces exercise effectiveness

⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion:** Not achieving full stretch at bottom or complete extension at top limits muscle development

⚠️ **Lower back arch:** Compensating with spinal extension when core stability is inadequate

⚠️ **Dropping weight too quickly:** Losing control during the eccentric phase increases injury risk
