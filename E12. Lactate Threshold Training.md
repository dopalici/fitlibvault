### E12. Lactate Threshold Training

## Quick Takeaway
Lactate threshold training improves your ability to sustain higher intensities aerobically by training at the point where lactate begins to accumulate faster than your body can clear it.

## Key Points
- Lactate thresholds mark critical transitions in energy system dominance during exercise
- LT1 (aerobic threshold) represents initial lactate rise, while LT2 (anaerobic threshold/MLSS) marks maximal lactate steady state
- Training at threshold intensities enhances mitochondrial function, lactate clearance, and metabolic efficiency
- Threshold training typically occurs at 80-90% max heart rate with RPE described as "comfortably hard"
- Optimal programming includes 1-2 threshold sessions per week combined with high-volume easy training

## Deep Dive

### Understanding Lactate Thresholds
Lactate threshold training targets two key physiological markers that determine sustainable exercise intensity. The first lactate threshold (LT1/Aerobic Threshold) represents the point where lactate begins to rise above baseline levels, typically occurring around 65-75% of VO2max. The second threshold (LT2/Anaerobic Threshold or MLSS - Maximal Lactate Steady State) marks the highest intensity where lactate production equals clearance, usually at 80-90% of VO2max.

These thresholds are closely tied to the body's [[A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]], representing the balance between lactate production through glycolysis and lactate clearance through oxidation and gluconeogenesis. The physiological basis involves the interaction between oxygen delivery, mitochondrial oxidative capacity, and the rate of anaerobic glycolysis.

### Physiological Adaptations
Training at threshold intensities stimulates several key adaptations:

**Mitochondrial Improvements:** Enhanced mitochondrial density, enzyme activity, and respiratory capacity improve the muscle's ability to utilize oxygen and clear lactate. These adaptations are fundamental to shifting the lactate thresholds to higher absolute intensities.

**Lactate Transport and Clearance:** Increased monocarboxylate transporter (MCT) density improves lactate shuttling between muscle fibers and tissues. Enhanced lactate oxidation capacity in cardiac and skeletal muscle, along with improved hepatic gluconeogenesis, increases overall lactate clearance.

**Metabolic Efficiency:** Improved fat oxidation capacity at higher intensities reduces reliance on anaerobic glycolysis, delaying lactate accumulation. Enhanced buffering capacity helps tolerate higher lactate concentrations when they do occur.

### Training Methods

#### Tempo/Threshold Training
The cornerstone approach involves sustained efforts at or slightly below LT2/MLSS intensity. This typically corresponds to 80-90% of maximum heart rate, Zone 3-4 training, or an RPE described as "comfortably hard." Sessions can be structured as:
- Continuous efforts: 20-60 minutes at threshold intensity
- Long intervals: 2-4 repetitions of 10-20 minutes with 2-5 minute recovery periods

#### Sweet Spot Training
Popular in cycling, this method targets intensities slightly below functional threshold power (FTP) or LT2, typically 88-94% of FTP. This approach allows substantial training volume accumulation at a challenging but sustainable intensity, maximizing adaptations while managing fatigue accumulation.

#### Polarized Integration
Threshold training fits within a polarized training model, where approximately 10-20% of training time occurs in the threshold/tempo zone, with the majority at easy intensities below LT1. This distribution optimizes adaptation while preventing overreaching.

## Research Evidence

**Faude, O., Kindermann, W., & Meyer, T. (2009).** Lactate threshold concepts: how valid are they? *Sports Medicine*, 39(6), 469-490. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-009-0043-9
- Key finding: Lactate thresholds are valid markers of metabolic transitions and correlate strongly with endurance performance
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive analysis of threshold concepts)

**Seiler, S. (2010).** What is best practice for training intensity and duration distribution in endurance athletes? *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance*, 5(3), 276-291. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.5.3.276
- Key finding: Polarized training distribution with ~80% easy, ~20% moderate-to-hard produces superior adaptations compared to threshold-heavy approaches
- Study type: Review and analysis
- Evidence quality: High (extensive performance data analysis)

**Laursen, P. B., & Jenkins, D. G. (2002).** The scientific basis for high-intensity interval training: optimising training programmes and maximising performance in highly trained endurance athletes. *Sports Medicine*, 32(1), 53-73. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2165/00007256-200232010-00003
- Key finding: Threshold and high-intensity training induce distinct but complementary adaptations in mitochondrial function and lactate kinetics
- Study type: Comprehensive review
- Evidence quality: High (foundational training theory review)

## Practical Applications

**Weekly Programming:**
- Include 1-2 threshold sessions per week during build phases
- Allow 48-72 hours recovery between high-intensity sessions
- Combine with 3-4 easy aerobic sessions for optimal adaptation
- Limit consecutive weeks of intensive threshold training to 3-4 to prevent overreaching

**Intensity Determination:**
- Laboratory testing: Direct lactate measurement during incremental protocol (gold standard)
- Field testing: 30-60 minute time trials or functional threshold power tests
- Heart rate estimation: 80-90% of maximum heart rate or 85-95% of heart rate reserve
- RPE guidance: 6-7 on 1-10 scale, "comfortably hard" - sustainable but challenging pace

**Sample Sessions:**
- Continuous tempo: 30-40 minute run/ride at threshold pace
- Cruise intervals: 3 x 15 minutes at threshold with 3-minute easy recovery
- Sweet spot: 2 x 20 minutes at 90% threshold with 5-minute recovery
- Progression: 20-30-40 minute build-up at increasing intensities ending at threshold

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Training too hard** - exceeding threshold intensity reduces sustainable duration and shifts training stress to anaerobic systems
⚠️ **Insufficient recovery** - not allowing adequate time between sessions leads to accumulated fatigue and poor adaptation
⚠️ **Poor intensity estimation** - training without proper testing often results in intensities that are too hard or too easy
⚠️ **Neglecting aerobic base** - focusing only on threshold work without supporting easy volume limits adaptation potential
⚠️ **Excessive frequency** - performing threshold sessions more than 2-3 times per week increases injury and overtraining risk
⚠️ **Ignoring environmental factors** - heat, altitude, and fatigue state all affect threshold intensities and should be considered
