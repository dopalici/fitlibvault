### P9. Anti-Doping Ethics and Education
**ID:** P9-ANTI-DOPING-ETHICS **Created:** 2025-06-05

Anti-doping ethics and education encompasses the moral obligations and educational responsibilities of fitness professionals to promote clean sport, discourage performance-enhancing drug use, and provide accurate information about legal performance optimization methods. This includes understanding prohibited substances, health risks, and ethical alternatives.

**Core Components:**
- **Substance Education:** Comprehensive knowledge of prohibited substances, supplement contamination risks, and legal performance enhancers to provide accurate guidance to clients ([[C21. Evidence-Based Supplements (Group A)]], [[C22. Supplements with Limited/Mixed Evidence (Group B)]], [[C23. Supplement Safety, Regulation, and Quality Control]])
- **Health Risk Communication:** Clear explanation of physical and psychological risks associated with performance-enhancing drugs, including long-term health consequences and dependency issues ([[P2. Performance Enhancement Ethics]], [[I1. Training and Hormonal Responses]], [[A10. Hormonal Responses to Exercise]])
- **Legal Alternative Promotion:** Emphasis on evidence-based training methods, nutrition strategies, and legal ergogenic aids that can enhance performance safely and ethically ([[C24. Ergogenic Aids]], [[B4. Strength Training Principles]], [[E11. VO2 Max Training]])
- **Detection and Reporting:** Understanding testing procedures, detection windows, and professional obligations regarding suspected doping while maintaining appropriate boundaries ([[P1. Ethical Training and Coaching]], [[P7. Confidentiality and Privacy Protection]], [[P6. Professional Boundaries and Relationships]])
- **Competitive Integrity:** Promoting fair play, honest competition, and the intrinsic values of sport and fitness achievement through natural means ([[K1. Genetic Factors in Athletic Performance]], [[P4. Evidence-Based Coaching Methodologies]], [[K3. Psychological Aspects of Elite Performance]])
