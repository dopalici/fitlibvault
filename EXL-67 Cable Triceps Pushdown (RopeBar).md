### **EXL-67: Cable Triceps Pushdown (Rope/Bar)**

## Quick Takeaway
A beginner-friendly cable isolation exercise that effectively targets all three heads of the triceps through controlled elbow extension with various attachment options.

## Key Points
- Targets all three heads of the triceps brachii (long, lateral, medial) through elbow extension
- Cable system provides constant tension throughout the full range of motion
- Different attachments (rope, straight bar, V-bar) offer varying grip positions and muscle emphasis
- Requires minimal stabilization demands, making it accessible for beginners
- Upper arms remain stationary while elbows act as the primary hinge joint

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable triceps pushdown is an isolation exercise that primarily involves elbow extension against resistance. The cable system provides consistent tension throughout the movement arc, unlike free weights where resistance varies with gravity. The triceps brachii, consisting of three heads (long, lateral, and medial), works as the primary mover to extend the elbow joint.

### Attachment Variations
Different attachments provide unique benefits:
- **Rope attachment**: Allows for external rotation at the bottom position, potentially enhancing peak contraction
- **Straight bar**: Enables heavier loading and provides a stable grip
- **V-bar**: Offers a neutral grip position that may be more comfortable for some individuals

### Setup and Execution
Standing facing a high cable pulley, the exerciser grasps the chosen attachment with appropriate grip. Body positioning involves feet shoulder-width apart with slight knee bend and engaged core. The movement begins with elbows positioned at approximately 90 degrees, tucked close to the sides. Extension occurs through controlled triceps contraction while maintaining stationary upper arms.

### Progression Strategies
Progression follows standard [[B1. Progressive Overload]] principles through increased load, volume, or complexity. Advanced variations include tempo manipulations, single-arm execution, and progression to more challenging free-weight triceps exercises.

## Practical Applications

### Training Implementation
- **Beginners**: Start with 3 sets of 12-15 repetitions using moderate weight
- **Intermediate**: Progress to 3-4 sets of 8-12 repetitions with heavier loads
- **Advanced**: Incorporate tempo variations (3-second eccentric) or single-arm execution

### Programming Considerations
Position the exercise toward the end of upper body or arm-focused training sessions. The isolation nature makes it ideal for targeting triceps after compound movements like [[EXL-02 Barbell Bench Press (Flat)]] or [[EXL-82 Dip (Parallel Bar)]].

### Load Selection
Choose a weight that allows complete range of motion while maintaining proper form. The last 2-3 repetitions should be challenging but achievable with good technique.

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Allowing elbows to drift forward or flare out during the movement
⚠️ Using momentum by leaning forward excessively or swinging the torso
⚠️ Incomplete range of motion, particularly failing to achieve full elbow extension
⚠️ Shrugging shoulders or allowing upper traps to become involved
⚠️ Moving too quickly through the eccentric (lowering) phase
