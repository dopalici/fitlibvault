### Cancer Exercise Rehabilitation

Cancer exercise rehabilitation encompasses evidence-based exercise interventions designed to support individuals during and after cancer treatment, addressing treatment-related side effects, improving quality of life, and supporting recovery. This approach considers the unique challenges of cancer survivors including fatigue, neuropathy, lymphedema, and treatment-induced physical deconditioning.

**Core Components:**
- **Treatment Phase Considerations:** Adapting exercise protocols for pre-treatment, during active treatment, and post-treatment phases with varying energy levels and capabilities, connecting to [[../foundation/A23. Principle of Individuality]] and [[../foundation/A26. Principle of Recovery]]
- **Fatigue Management:** Structured exercise interventions to combat cancer-related fatigue and improve energy levels, linking to [[../recovery/M3. Stress Management and Performance]] and [[../foundation/A35. Metabolic Adaptations to Training]]
- **Functional Capacity Restoration:** Progressive exercise programs to rebuild strength, endurance, and daily living capabilities after treatment, relating to [[../cross_training/L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]] and [[../foundation/A21. Principle of Progression]]
- **Side Effect Mitigation:** Exercise strategies to address common treatment side effects including neuropathy, bone loss, and cardiovascular deconditioning, connecting to [[../foundation/A41. Skeletal Adaptations to Training]] and [[../foundation/A39. Cardiovascular Adaptations to Training]]
- **Psychological and Social Support:** Understanding exercise's role in mental health recovery and social reintegration post-treatment, linking to [[../performance_factors/F4. Behavioral Psychology in Fitness]] and [[../foundation/A65. Exercise-Induced Neuroplasticity]]
