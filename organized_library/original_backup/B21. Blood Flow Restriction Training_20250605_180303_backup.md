### B21. Blood Flow Restriction Training
**ID:** B21-BFR-TRAINING
**Created:** 2025-06-05

Blood Flow Restriction (BFR) training involves the application of external pressure to limbs during exercise to partially restrict venous return while maintaining arterial inflow, allowing for strength and hypertrophy gains at lower training loads.

**Key Components:**
- **Mechanism:** Partial occlusion increases metabolic stress and muscle fiber recruitment
- **Pressure:** Typically 40-80% of arterial occlusion pressure 
- **Load:** 20-40% 1RM vs traditional 70%+ for similar adaptations
- **Application:** Proximal limb occlusion during resistance or aerobic exercise
- **Safety:** Requires proper equipment and monitoring protocols

**Training Applications:**
- Low-load strength training (20-40% 1RM)
- Rehabilitation settings with load restrictions
- Endurance training enhancement
- Hypertrophy stimulus with reduced joint stress
- Metabolic conditioning protocols

**Key Research:**
- Loenneke JP, et al. (2012). Low intensity blood flow restriction training: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Applied Physiology, 112(5), 1849-1859.
- Slysz J, et al. (2016). The efficacy of blood flow restricted exercise: A systematic review & meta-analysis. Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport, 19(8), 669-675.
