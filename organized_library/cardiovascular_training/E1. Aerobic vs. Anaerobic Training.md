### E1. Aerobic vs. Anaerobic Training

## Quick Takeaway
Aerobic training uses oxygen for sustained, moderate-intensity exercise that builds endurance, while anaerobic training relies on stored energy for short, high-intensity bursts that develop power and speed.

## Key Points
- **Aerobic training**: Sustainable 3+ minutes, improves heart health and fat burning, examples include jogging and cycling
- **Anaerobic training**: High-intensity bursts under 3 minutes, builds power and speed, examples include sprints and heavy lifting
- **Energy systems**: Aerobic uses oxygen, anaerobic uses stored ATP and muscle glucose without oxygen
- **Training zones**: Aerobic typically under lactate threshold, anaerobic above lactate threshold
- **Most real activities**: Use both systems - training programs should include both for optimal results

## Deep Dive

Training methods are fundamentally distinguished by which energy system provides the majority of ATP during exercise, as detailed in [[../foundation/A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]].

### Aerobic Training Characteristics

**Physiological Definition**: Exercise performed at intensities where the oxidative system can meet ATP demands using oxygen as the primary substrate. This typically corresponds to efforts sustainable for more than 2-3 minutes continuously.

**Intensity Parameters**:
- Below lactate threshold 2 (LT2/MLSS)
- Heart rate zones 1-4
- RPE typically 3-6 on a 1-10 scale
- Can maintain steady conversation

**Primary Adaptations**: Research demonstrates significant cardiovascular improvements including increased maximum cardiac output (Qmax), stroke volume, and blood volume (Bassett & Howley, 2000). Cellular adaptations include enhanced mitochondrial density and function, increased capillary density around muscle fibers, and improved fat oxidation capacity. These adaptations are closely tied to principles in [[../foundation/A8. Cardiovascular Physiology Fundamentals]].

### Anaerobic Training Characteristics

**Physiological Definition**: High-intensity exercise where ATP demand exceeds oxidative system capacity, requiring primary reliance on ATP-PCr (phosphocreatine) and glycolytic energy systems. Duration is typically limited to under 2-3 minutes due to metabolite accumulation.

**Intensity Parameters**:
- Above lactate threshold 2 (LT2/MLSS)
- Heart rate zone 5+
- RPE 7-10 on a 1-10 scale
- Cannot maintain conversation

**Primary Adaptations**: Enhanced capacity of immediate and short-term energy systems through increased enzyme activity (creatine kinase, phosphofructokinase), elevated substrate stores (phosphocreatine, muscle glycogen), and improved buffering capacity to handle lactate and hydrogen ion accumulation.

### Training Integration

Most real-world activities and sports involve contributions from multiple energy systems simultaneously. The relative contribution depends on exercise intensity and duration. Effective training programs strategically incorporate both modalities based on specific performance goals and individual needs, as outlined in [[E4. Interval Training (General)]].

## Research Evidence

**Hawley JA, et al. (2014)**. Molecular responses to strength and endurance training: are they incompatible? Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism, 39(3), 312-321.
- Demonstrates distinct molecular signaling pathways for aerobic vs anaerobic adaptations
- Shows potential interference effects when combining training types

**Laursen PB. (2010)**. Training for intense exercise performance: high-intensity or high-volume training? Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports, 20 Suppl 2, 1-10.
- Systematic review comparing training approaches
- Evidence for polarized training distribution (80% low intensity, 20% high intensity)

**Bassett DR, Howley ET. (2000)**. Limiting factors for maximum oxygen uptake and determinants of endurance performance. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 32(1), 70-84.
- Comprehensive review of cardiovascular adaptations to aerobic training

## Practical Applications

**Aerobic Training Implementation**:
- Begin with 20-30 minutes at conversational pace
- Progress duration before intensity (10% rule)
- Examples: [[E2. Steady-State Cardio (LISS - Low-Intensity Steady State)]], zone 2 cycling, easy running
- Frequency: 3-5 sessions per week for general fitness

**Anaerobic Training Implementation**:
- Start with 1-2 sessions per week
- Work-to-rest ratios of 1:2 to 1:4 for recovery
- Examples: [[E3. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)]], 30-second sprints, heavy resistance training
- Allow 48-72 hours between high-intensity sessions

**Periodization Strategy**:
- Build aerobic base first (4-6 weeks)
- Add anaerobic components gradually
- Use 80/20 intensity distribution for endurance goals
- Adjust ratios based on sport-specific demands

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Training too hard too often**: Most aerobic training should feel "easy" - if you can't hold a conversation, you're likely in the anaerobic zone

⚠️ **Insufficient recovery**: Anaerobic training requires complete recovery between intervals and sessions - incomplete rest reduces training quality

⚠️ **Ignoring energy system specificity**: Training at moderate intensities (gray zone) often fails to optimally stress either system

⚠️ **Poor progression**: Jumping to high-intensity work without adequate aerobic base increases injury risk and limits adaptation
