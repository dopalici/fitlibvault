### A1. Energy Balance

## Quick Takeaway
Energy balance—the relationship between calories consumed and calories burned—determines whether you gain, lose, or maintain weight according to fundamental physics.

## Key Points
- Eat more calories than you burn = weight gain (surplus)
- Burn more calories than you eat = weight loss (deficit)
- Match calories in with calories out = weight maintenance
- Athletes need sufficient energy availability (30-35+ kcal/kg fat-free mass daily) to maintain health and performance
- Body composition changes depend on training stimulus and protein intake, not just energy balance

## Deep Dive

Energy balance describes the relationship between energy intake (EI - calories consumed) and energy expenditure (EE - calories burned). This relationship dictates changes in body mass according to the First Law of Thermodynamics, which states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed.

### The Three States of Energy Balance

**Caloric Surplus (EI > EE):** Results in weight gain through storage of excess energy. While this is primarily stored as fat, lean tissue can also be gained when combined with appropriate stimulus like resistance training and adequate protein intake.

**Caloric Deficit (EI < EE):** Leads to weight loss through mobilization of stored energy. The body preferentially uses fat stores, but lean tissue loss can occur without adequate protein intake and resistance training stimulus.

**Energy Balance (EI = EE):** Results in weight maintenance, with energy intake matching expenditure over time.

### Energy Availability for Athletes

Energy Availability (EA) represents a more sophisticated concept than simple energy balance, particularly relevant for athletes. EA describes the dietary energy remaining for physiological functions after accounting for exercise energy expenditure, normalized to fat-free mass:

**EA = (Energy Intake - Exercise Energy Expenditure) / Fat-Free Mass**

Low energy availability (<30-35 kcal/kg FFM/day) significantly impairs both health and performance markers.

## Research Evidence

**Hall KD, et al. (2012).** Energy balance and its components: implications for body weight regulation. *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 95(4), 989-994. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.112.036350
- Key finding: Energy balance follows thermodynamic principles with body weight changes determined by the difference between energy intake and expenditure
- Study type: Review
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive review of energy balance fundamentals)

**Loucks AB, et al. (2011).** Low energy availability in athletes: health, performance, and physiology. *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 29(S1), S7-S15. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2011.588958
- Key finding: Energy availability below 30 kcal/kg FFM/day disrupts reproductive, metabolic, and bone health functions
- Study type: Review
- Evidence quality: High (foundational work on energy availability concept)

**Garthe I, et al. (2011).** Effect of two different weight-loss rates on body composition and strength and power-related performance in elite athletes. *International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism*, 21(2), 97-104. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97
- Key finding: Slow weight loss (0.7% body weight/week) preserved lean mass and performance better than fast weight loss (1.4%/week)
- Study type: RCT
- Evidence quality: High (well-controlled study in elite athletes)

## Practical Applications

- **Weight Loss:** Create a deficit of 300-500 calories daily for sustainable fat loss of 0.5-1 lb per week
- **Weight Gain:** Maintain a surplus of 200-500 calories daily, combined with resistance training for lean mass gains
- **Athlete Monitoring:** Calculate energy availability weekly, ensuring intake stays above 35 kcal/kg FFM/day
- **Body Recomposition:** Use small deficits (200-300 calories) with high protein intake and resistance training

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ Assuming all weight gain/loss is fat—body composition depends on training and nutrition quality
⚠️ Ignoring energy availability in favor of simple calorie counting, especially for active individuals
⚠️ Expecting linear weight changes—water retention, hormones, and adaptive metabolism affect daily fluctuations
⚠️ Focusing solely on scale weight rather than body composition changes
