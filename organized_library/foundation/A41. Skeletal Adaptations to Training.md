### A41. Reactive Strength Index
**ID:** A41-REACTIVE-STRENGTH-INDEX
**Created:** 2025-04-26 (Updated: 2025-01-14)

## Quick Takeaway
Reactive Strength Index (RSI) measures stretch-shortening cycle efficiency by calculating the ratio of jump height to ground contact time, providing a key metric for plyometric performance and neuromuscular function.

## Key Points
- RSI quantifies the efficiency of the stretch-shortening cycle during plyometric movements
- Calculated as jump height divided by ground contact time (RSI = Jump Height ÷ Contact Time)
- Higher RSI values indicate better reactive strength and neuromuscular efficiency
- Drop jump tests at 30cm height are the gold standard for RSI assessment
- RSI provides objective feedback for monitoring plyometric training adaptations

## Deep Dive

### Understanding RSI Calculation
RSI is calculated using the formula: **RSI = Jump Height (m) ÷ Ground Contact Time (s)**

This simple ratio provides insight into an athlete's ability to rapidly transition from eccentric to concentric muscle actions. A higher RSI indicates more efficient use of the stretch-shortening cycle, suggesting better neuromuscular coordination and reactive strength capabilities.

### Physiological Mechanisms
The stretch-shortening cycle involves three phases: eccentric (muscle lengthening), amortization (transition), and concentric (muscle shortening). RSI captures the efficiency of this entire cycle, reflecting:
- Elastic energy storage and utilization
- Neuromuscular coordination
- Muscle-tendon unit stiffness
- Motor unit recruitment patterns

### Assessment Protocols
**Standard Drop Jump Test:**
- Drop height: 30cm (most commonly used)
- Landing: Both feet simultaneously
- Jump: Maximum effort vertical jump immediately after landing
- Measurement: Force plates or jump mats record contact time and jump height
- Trials: 3-5 attempts with adequate rest between trials

### Performance Benchmarks
RSI values vary by population and sport:
- Recreational athletes: 0.5-1.0
- Trained athletes: 1.0-2.0
- Elite power athletes: 2.0-3.0+

## Research Evidence

**Flanagan, E.P. & Comyns, T.M. (2008).** The use of contact time and the reactive strength index to optimize fast stretch-shortening cycle training. *Strength and Conditioning Journal*, 30(5), 32-38. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0b013e318187e25b
- Key finding: RSI provides reliable assessment of stretch-shortening cycle function and training adaptations
- Study type: Review/Technical article
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive analysis of RSI methodology)

**Ebben, W.P. & Petushek, E.J. (2010).** Using the reactive strength index modified to evaluate plyometric performance. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 24(8), 1983-1987. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181e72466
- Key finding: Modified RSI (using flight time instead of jump height) showed strong correlations with traditional RSI measurements
- Study type: Observational study
- Evidence quality: Medium (validation of alternative measurement method)

**Lloyd, R.S. et al. (2012).** The effects of 4-weeks of plyometric training on reactive strength index and leg stiffness in male youths. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 26(10), 2812-2819. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e318242d2ec
- Key finding: Four weeks of plyometric training significantly improved RSI by 13.8% in youth athletes
- Study type: Randomized controlled trial
- Evidence quality: High (well-controlled intervention study)

## Practical Applications

**Assessment Implementation:**
1. **Equipment Setup:** Use force plates or validated jump mats for accurate measurements
2. **Standardized Protocol:** 30cm drop height, maximum effort rebound jump
3. **Data Collection:** Record both contact time (milliseconds) and jump height (centimeters)
4. **Calculation:** RSI = Jump Height (m) ÷ Contact Time (s)

**Training Applications:**
- **Baseline Testing:** Establish individual RSI values before training programs
- **Progress Monitoring:** Track RSI changes every 4-6 weeks during plyometric phases
- **Load Management:** Higher RSI may indicate readiness for increased training loads
- **Return to Sport:** RSI can help assess neuromuscular function recovery post-injury

**Programming Guidelines:**
- Target RSI improvements of 10-20% over 6-8 week training blocks
- Use RSI to optimize drop jump training heights (maximize RSI rather than jump height alone)
- Monitor for RSI decreases as potential indicators of fatigue or overreaching

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Using inconsistent drop heights** - Standardize at 30cm for reliable comparisons and normative data application

⚠️ **Focusing only on jump height** - RSI emphasizes efficiency; a lower jump with much shorter contact time may yield better RSI

⚠️ **Inadequate rest between trials** - Fatigue significantly affects RSI measurements; allow 60-90 seconds between attempts

⚠️ **Ignoring contact time quality** - Excessive ground contact time (>250ms) may indicate technical issues rather than true reactive strength
