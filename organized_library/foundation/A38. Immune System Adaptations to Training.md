### A38. Fatigue-Induced Movement Changes

## Quick Takeaway
Fatigue progressively alters movement patterns, reducing coordination and efficiency while increasing injury risk through compensatory movement strategies.

## Key Points
- Fatigue disrupts neuromuscular coordination, leading to movement pattern degradation
- Motor unit recruitment becomes less efficient as fatigue accumulates
- Compensatory movement strategies emerge to maintain performance output
- Movement variability increases under fatigue conditions
- Recovery of movement quality often lags behind force recovery

## Deep Dive

### Neuromuscular Changes Under Fatigue

**Central Fatigue Effects:**
Central fatigue reduces the nervous system's ability to fully activate muscles, leading to altered motor unit recruitment patterns. The brain's motor cortex shows decreased excitability, while inhibitory mechanisms become more prominent. This results in reduced voluntary activation and changes in movement timing and coordination.

**Peripheral Fatigue Effects:**
At the muscle level, fatigue impairs excitation-contraction coupling and reduces force-generating capacity. Metabolic byproducts accumulate, pH decreases, and calcium handling becomes compromised. These changes force the neuromuscular system to adopt alternative movement strategies.

### Movement Pattern Adaptations

**Kinematic Changes:**
Fatigued individuals typically demonstrate reduced range of motion, altered joint angles, and modified movement velocities. The body adopts energy-conserving strategies that may compromise optimal biomechanics. Trunk stability often decreases, leading to increased spinal motion during previously stable movements.

**Kinetic Alterations:**
Force production patterns shift under fatigue, with reduced peak forces and altered force-time characteristics. Ground reaction forces may change direction and magnitude, affecting movement efficiency and potentially increasing injury risk.

### Assessment Methods

**Real-Time Movement Analysis:**
Modern motion capture systems and wearable sensors can detect subtle changes in movement patterns before obvious performance decrements occur. Key metrics include joint angle deviations, movement smoothness, and symmetry indices.

**Functional Movement Screening:**
Standardized movement assessments can identify fatigue-induced compensations. These screenings should be performed both in fresh and fatigued states to establish individual baseline patterns and fatigue responses.

## Research Evidence

**Gandevia, S.C. (2001).** Spinal and supraspinal factors in human muscle fatigue. *Physiological Reviews*, 81(4), 1725-1789. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.2001.81.4.1725
- Key finding: Central fatigue contributes significantly to reduced voluntary activation and altered movement patterns
- Study type: Comprehensive review
- Evidence quality: High (landmark review establishing central fatigue mechanisms)

**Gribble, P.A. & Hertel, J. (2004).** Effect of lower-extremity muscle fatigue on postural control. *Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation*, 85(4), 589-592. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2003.06.031
- Key finding: Lower extremity fatigue significantly impaired single-limb postural control and increased center of pressure excursion
- Study type: Controlled experimental study
- Evidence quality: High (well-controlled laboratory study)

**McLean, S.G. et al. (2007).** Fatigue-induced ACL injury risk stems from a degradation in central control. *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 39(8), 1341-1347. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31804c274b
- Key finding: Neuromuscular fatigue altered lower extremity kinematics and kinetics in ways that increase ACL injury risk
- Study type: Controlled biomechanical analysis
- Evidence quality: High (direct measurement of injury-relevant biomechanics)

## Practical Applications

**Fatigue Monitoring Protocols:**
- Implement movement screens before and after training sessions
- Use wearable technology to track movement quality metrics in real-time
- Establish individual baseline movement patterns for comparison
- Monitor asymmetries that may indicate compensatory patterns

**Training Modifications:**
- Reduce training intensity when movement quality degrades significantly
- Incorporate movement quality cues during fatigued states
- Practice maintaining proper form under progressive fatigue
- Use fatigue-resistant movement patterns in sport-specific training

**Injury Prevention Strategies:**
- Educate athletes on recognizing their own movement quality changes
- Implement mandatory rest periods when movement screens indicate high risk
- Focus on core stability and proprioceptive training to maintain movement quality under fatigue
- Develop sport-specific movement protocols that account for fatigue patterns

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Ignoring early movement changes** - Waiting for obvious performance decrements misses critical intervention windows

⚠️ **Focusing only on force output** - Movement quality often degrades before measurable strength losses occur

⚠️ **Using generic fatigue protocols** - Individual and sport-specific fatigue responses vary significantly

⚠️ **Inadequate recovery assessment** - Movement quality recovery often lags behind subjective recovery feelings
