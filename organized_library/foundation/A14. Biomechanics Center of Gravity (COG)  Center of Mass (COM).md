### A14. Biomechanics: Center of Gravity (COG) / Center of Mass (COM)

## Quick Takeaway
The center of gravity (COG) is the point where your body's weight is concentrated, and keeping it positioned over your base of support is essential for balance, stability, and safe movement.

## Key Points
- COG location changes constantly with body position and limb movement
- Stability occurs when COG remains vertically above the base of support (BOS)
- Lower COG height and wider BOS increase stability
- Movement requires shifting COG outside the BOS to create controlled instability
- COG can be located outside the physical body during certain movements

## Deep Dive

### Understanding Center of Gravity vs Center of Mass
The Center of Gravity (COG) and Center of Mass (COM) are often used interchangeably in human movement analysis. Technically, COM refers to the point where mass is concentrated, while COG refers to the point where gravitational forces act. For practical exercise applications, these terms represent the same concept.

### COG Location and Variability
In humans standing in anatomical position, the COG typically sits around the S2 vertebra level, approximately 55-57% of total body height from the ground. However, this location is highly dynamic:

- **Postural changes**: Raising arms overhead shifts COG upward and forward
- **Limb positioning**: Single-leg stance moves COG toward the supporting leg
- **External loads**: Carrying weight alters COG position based on load placement
- **Body composition**: Higher muscle mass in upper body raises COG; more mass in hips/legs lowers it

### Base of Support (BOS) Relationship
The BOS encompasses all points of contact between the body and supporting surface. The COG-BOS relationship determines stability:

**Stable equilibrium**: COG positioned vertically above BOS center
**Unstable equilibrium**: COG near BOS edges
**Loss of balance**: COG moves outside BOS boundaries

### Factors Affecting Stability
Multiple variables influence the COG-BOS stability relationship:

1. **COG height**: Lower positions increase stability (why athletes crouch in ready positions)
2. **BOS size**: Wider stance provides larger margin for COG movement
3. **COG positioning**: Central placement within BOS maximizes stability margins
4. **Body mass**: Greater mass increases momentum but also provides stability
5. **Surface conditions**: Uneven or compliant surfaces reduce effective BOS

### Movement Initiation Mechanics
All voluntary movement begins with COG displacement outside the BOS. This creates controlled instability that the nervous system must address through:

- **Anticipatory postural adjustments**: Muscle activation before movement begins
- **Compensatory responses**: Reactive balance corrections during movement
- **Step strategies**: Expanding BOS through foot placement

## Practical Applications

### Exercise Programming
- **Stability training**: Progress from wide to narrow BOS (bilateral to unilateral stance)
- **Strength training**: Lower COG through wider stance in squats and deadlifts
- **Balance challenges**: Elevate COG (overhead reaches) or reduce BOS (single-leg exercises)
- **Sport-specific training**: Practice COG control relevant to sport demands

### Technique Optimization
- **Lifting mechanics**: Keep load close to body to minimize COG displacement
- **Athletic positions**: Lower COG in ready positions for quick movement initiation
- **Fall prevention**: Maintain COG within BOS during daily activities
- **Rehabilitation**: Progress COG challenges as balance improves

### Training Progressions
1. **Beginner**: Wide BOS, low COG (wide-stance exercises)
2. **Intermediate**: Normal BOS with COG challenges (overhead movements)
3. **Advanced**: Narrow BOS or elevated COG (single-leg with reaches)
4. **Expert**: Dynamic COG control (sport-specific movement patterns)

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Assuming COG is always located within the physical body boundaries
⚠️ Ignoring COG changes when adding external loads or changing body position
⚠️ Progressing balance challenges too quickly without adequate COG control
⚠️ Focusing only on BOS width while neglecting COG height considerations
