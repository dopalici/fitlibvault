### E7. Cycling (Technique, Programming)
**ID:** E7-CYCLING
**Created:** 2025-04-23 (Updated: 2025-01-07)

## Quick Takeaway
Cycling is a highly efficient, low-impact cardiovascular exercise that requires proper bike fit and structured training zones to maximize performance while minimizing injury risk.

## Key Points
- Professional bike fit prevents injury and improves efficiency through optimal positioning
- Maintain 80-100 RPM cadence for optimal power transfer and reduced joint stress
- Structure training around power zones (FTP-based) or heart rate zones for progressive overload
- Combine base endurance rides with targeted interval sessions for comprehensive fitness
- Indoor trainers enable precise, weather-independent structured workouts

## Deep Dive

### Technique Fundamentals

**Bike Fit Essentials**
Proper bike fit is the foundation of safe, efficient cycling. Research shows that incorrect positioning can reduce power output and significantly increase injury risk. Key measurements include saddle height (typically 25-35mm below inseam), fore-aft saddle position, and handlebar reach. Professional fitting addresses individual anatomy and flexibility limitations.

**Pedaling Mechanics and Cadence**
Efficient pedaling involves a smooth, circular motion rather than just pushing down. The concept of "ankling" - pointing toes down during the power phase and up during recovery - optimizes force application throughout the pedal stroke. Cadence of 80-100 RPM is generally most efficient for endurance cycling, though this varies with fitness level, terrain, and training goals.

**Posture and Positioning**
Cycling posture varies significantly between bike types (road, mountain, hybrid). Generally, maintain a neutral spine, relaxed shoulders, and slight bend in elbows. Hand position should allow comfortable reach without overstretching or cramping the torso.

### Programming Components

**Base Building Phase**
Foundation training emphasizes longer rides at low-moderate intensity, typically 60-80% of maximum heart rate. This develops aerobic capacity and fat oxidation efficiency through steady-state cardio [[E2. Steady-State Cardio (LISS - Low-Intensity Steady State)]]. Base phases typically last 8-16 weeks depending on experience level.

**Intensity Training Structure**
- **Endurance Rides:** 2-6 hours at conversational pace
- **Tempo/Threshold Work:** Sustained efforts at lactate threshold [[E12. Lactate Threshold Training]]
- **VO2 Max Intervals:** 3-8 minute high-intensity efforts [[E11. VO2 Max Training]]
- **Anaerobic Capacity:** 30 seconds to 2 minutes above threshold
- **Sprint Training:** 10-15 second maximal efforts with full recovery

**Power and Heart Rate Zone Training**
Modern cycling training utilizes power zones based on Functional Threshold Power (FTP) - the maximum sustainable power for one hour. Seven-zone systems are common, with Zone 1 (active recovery) to Zone 7 (neuromuscular power). Heart rate zone training [[E13. Heart Rate Zone Training]] provides an accessible alternative when power meters aren't available.

### Critical Power vs Lactate Threshold
Recent research has refined our understanding of the relationship between critical power and maximal lactate steady state in cycling. Critical power represents the theoretical power output that can be sustained indefinitely without fatigue, while FTP serves as a practical approximation for training purposes.

## Practical Applications

**Weekly Training Structure (Intermediate Level):**
- 3-4 rides per week, 6-10 hours total
- 1-2 easy endurance rides (60-70% FTP)
- 1 tempo/threshold session (85-95% FTP)
- 1 high-intensity interval session
- Include 1-2 rest days for adaptation

**Indoor Training Setup:**
- Smart trainer with power measurement
- Training apps (Zwift, TrainerRoad, Sufferfest)
- Fan for cooling, towel for sweat management
- Screen/device at eye level to maintain posture

**Progression Guidelines:**
- Increase weekly volume by 10% maximum
- Follow 3:1 work-to-recovery ratio (3 weeks building, 1 week easy)
- Test FTP every 6-8 weeks to adjust training zones

**Nutritional Considerations:**
- Pre-ride carbohydrate loading for sessions >90 minutes
- Consider caffeine supplementation 30-60 minutes before high-intensity sessions
- Maintain hydration with electrolyte replacement during longer rides

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Riding too hard on easy days** - Compromises recovery and adaptation quality
⚠️ **Ignoring bike fit** - Leads to discomfort, injury, and reduced efficiency
⚠️ **Training without structure** - Random intensity fails to optimize specific adaptations
⚠️ **Inadequate recovery** - More isn't always better; adaptation occurs during rest
⚠️ **Neglecting strength training** - Off-bike strength work enhances power and injury prevention
⚠️ **Confusing critical power with FTP** - These represent different physiological concepts requiring distinct training approaches
