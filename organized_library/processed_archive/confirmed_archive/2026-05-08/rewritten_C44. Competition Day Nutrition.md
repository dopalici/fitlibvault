### C44. Competition Day Nutrition
**ID:** C44-COMPETITION-DAY-NUTRITION
**Created:** 2025-06-05 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Competition day nutrition requires precise timing of carbohydrates, fluids, and electrolytes to optimize performance while minimizing gastrointestinal distress through practiced protocols.

## Key Points
- Pre-competition nutrition focuses on maximizing glycogen stores and optimizing hydration 1-4 hours before events
- During-event fueling maintains blood glucose and electrolyte balance through sport-specific protocols
- Carbohydrate intake of 30-90g per hour during prolonged events (>60 minutes) supports sustained performance
- Gastrointestinal tolerance must be trained during practice to prevent competition day issues
- Recovery nutrition should begin within 30 minutes post-competition to initiate adaptation processes

## Deep Dive

Competition day nutrition represents the culmination of systematic nutritional preparation, requiring precise implementation of evidence-based strategies to optimize athletic performance while managing physiological stress and gastrointestinal function.

### Pre-Competition Nutrition Strategy

The pre-competition phase involves strategic nutrient timing and selection to optimize energy substrate availability. Carbohydrate loading protocols should be completed 1-3 days prior, with the final pre-competition meal consumed 1-4 hours before event start to allow adequate digestion while maintaining glycogen availability ([[C27. Nutrition for Endurance Performance (Carb Loading)]]).

Hydration optimization begins 24 hours before competition, with systematic fluid intake to achieve euhydration without overhydration. Pre-competition meals should emphasize familiar, easily digestible carbohydrates while minimizing fiber, fat, and protein to reduce gastrointestinal burden ([[C4. Hydration]]).

### During-Event Fueling Protocols

During-event nutrition varies significantly based on exercise duration, intensity, and environmental conditions. Events lasting less than 60 minutes typically require only fluid replacement, while longer events necessitate systematic carbohydrate and electrolyte replacement ([[C5. Pre, During, and Post-Exercise Nutrition]]).

For endurance events exceeding 60 minutes, carbohydrate intake of 30-60g per hour supports performance, with higher rates (60-90g/hour) beneficial for ultra-endurance activities when using multiple transportable carbohydrates. Electrolyte replacement becomes critical in events exceeding 2 hours or in hot environments ([[C5. Electrolytes]], [[C47. Heat Stress Nutrition]]).

### Sport-Specific Considerations

Different sports require tailored nutritional approaches based on their unique physiological demands. Team sports with intermittent high-intensity efforts benefit from strategic halftime refueling, while endurance sports require systematic hourly intake protocols ([[X. Sports-Specific Training]]).

Environmental factors significantly influence nutritional needs, with heat stress increasing fluid and electrolyte requirements while cold exposure may alter substrate utilization patterns. Multi-day competitions require additional considerations for glycogen resynthesis and cumulative stress management ([[C49. Multi-Day Event Nutrition]]).

### Gastrointestinal Management

Gastrointestinal distress represents a primary limitation to optimal competition nutrition. Strategic gut training during practice sessions improves tolerance to competition day fueling protocols. This involves gradually increasing carbohydrate intake rates and practicing with competition day products and timing ([[C32. Gut Health and Microbiome]]).

Food selection should prioritize low-fiber, low-fat options with moderate protein content. Liquid carbohydrate sources often provide better tolerance than solid foods during high-intensity exercise, though individual preferences and tolerances vary significantly.

- Study type: Systematic review

- Key finding: Evidence-based recommendations for competition day nutrition across different sports and durations
- Study type: Position statement/Meta-analysis

## Practical Applications

**Pre-Competition Timeline:**
- 3-4 hours before: 1-4g carbohydrate per kg body weight with familiar foods
- 1-2 hours before: 0.5-1g carbohydrate per kg body weight, primarily liquid
- 15-30 minutes before: Small amounts of easily absorbed carbohydrates if desired

**During-Event Protocols:**
- Events <60 minutes: Water or sports drink as needed
- Events 1-2 hours: 30-60g carbohydrate per hour plus electrolytes
- Events >2 hours: 60-90g carbohydrate per hour using multiple carbohydrate types

**Environmental Modifications:**
- Hot conditions: Increase fluid intake by 150-250ml per hour, emphasize sodium replacement
- Cold conditions: Maintain carbohydrate intake despite reduced thirst sensation
- Altitude: Increase carbohydrate emphasis due to altered substrate utilization

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Trying new foods or supplements on competition day without prior testing
⚠️ Overhydrating in attempts to prevent dehydration, leading to hyponatremia risk
⚠️ Ignoring individual tolerance differences and following generic recommendations
⚠️ Focusing only on during-event nutrition while neglecting pre-competition preparation
⚠️ Underestimating the importance of post-competition recovery nutrition timing
