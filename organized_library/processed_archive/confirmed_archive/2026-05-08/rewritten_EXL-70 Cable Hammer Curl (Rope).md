### **EXL-70: Cable Hammer Curl (Rope)**

**ID:** EXL-70-CEX-HAMMERCURL **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-14)

## Quick Takeaway
Cable hammer curls with rope provide constant tension throughout the movement while emphasizing the brachialis and brachioradialis muscles more than traditional bicep curls.

## Key Points
- Uses neutral grip positioning which targets brachialis and brachioradialis muscles primarily
- Cable system provides constant tension throughout entire range of motion
- Beginner-friendly exercise with multiple progression and regression options
- Requires strict upper arm positioning to isolate elbow flexor muscles effectively
- Can be performed unilaterally or bilaterally for training variation

## Deep Dive

### Movement Pattern and Muscle Activation
The cable hammer curl with rope is an isolated elbow flexion exercise that utilizes a neutral grip position. This grip orientation shifts emphasis from the biceps brachii to the deeper brachialis muscle and the brachioradialis of the forearm. The constant tension provided by the cable system maintains muscle activation throughout both concentric and eccentric phases [[B20. Eccentric Training]].

### Equipment Setup and Positioning
The exercise requires a low pulley cable machine [[B10. Machine Training]] with a rope attachment. Proper positioning involves standing directly in front of the machine with feet stable and core engaged [[L4. Core Stability Training]]. The rope ends are grasped with palms facing each other, maintaining this neutral grip throughout the movement.

### Execution Technique
Starting from a fully extended arm position, the movement involves curling the rope upward while keeping the upper arms stationary against the torso. The rope ends can remain together or be slightly separated at the top of the movement. Control is maintained throughout both lifting and lowering phases, with particular attention to resisting the weight during the eccentric portion.

## Practical Applications

### Programming Guidelines
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 repetitions with moderate weight
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 8-12 repetitions with progressive loading [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- **Advanced:** Incorporate tempo variations, single-arm variations, or higher volume protocols [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]

### Progression Strategies
1. Increase load incrementally using weight stack
2. Add repetitions or sets for volume progression
3. Implement tempo modifications (2-second pause at top, 3-second eccentric)
4. Progress to single-arm variations using D-handle
5. Advance to free-weight dumbbell hammer curls [[EXL-31 Dumbbell Hammer Curl]]

### Regression Options
- Reduce load to focus on movement quality
- Use resistance bands for variable resistance [[B11. Resistance Bands]]
- Emphasize compound pulling movements before isolated arm work

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Body swing or momentum:** Using torso movement to assist the lift reduces target muscle activation
⚠️ **Forward elbow drift:** Allowing elbows to move forward shifts emphasis away from intended muscles
⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion:** Not fully extending or flexing limits muscle development potential
⚠️ **Grip rotation:** Changing from neutral to supinated grip alters muscle recruitment patterns
