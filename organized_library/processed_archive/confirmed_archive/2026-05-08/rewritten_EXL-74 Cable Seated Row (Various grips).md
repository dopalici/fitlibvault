### **EXL-74: Cable Seated Row (Various grips)**

**ID:** EXL-74-CEX-ROW-SEATEDVARIOUS **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
A versatile cable machine exercise that targets the entire posterior chain with different grip options to emphasize specific back muscle groups while providing constant tension throughout the movement.

## Key Points
- Different grip attachments target different areas of the back musculature
- Close neutral grip emphasizes lats and mid-back thickness
- Wide pronated grip targets upper back, rear delts, and traps more effectively
- Constant cable tension provides consistent resistance throughout the full range of motion
- Scapular retraction initiation is crucial for proper muscle activation and shoulder health

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable seated row is a horizontal pulling movement performed on a low pulley cable station. The seated position with foot bracing provides stability while allowing focus on the pulling motion. The exercise involves pulling a handle attachment toward the torso while maintaining proper spinal alignment.

### Grip Variations and Muscle Emphasis
**Close Neutral Grip (V-bar):** This grip position allows for the greatest range of motion and emphasizes the latissimus dorsi and mid-back muscles, particularly the rhomboids and middle trapezius. The neutral hand position reduces stress on the wrists and allows for stronger pulling mechanics.

**Wide Pronated Grip (Lat bar):** This variation shifts emphasis to the upper back, posterior deltoids, and upper trapezius fibers. The wider grip reduces the contribution of the lats while increasing activation of the posterior deltoid and upper back stabilizers.

**Single D-Handles:** Using individual handles allows for unilateral training and the greatest range of motion. This variation can help address strength imbalances between sides and allows for natural rotation during the movement.

### Biomechanical Considerations
The movement should initiate with scapular retraction before elbow flexion occurs. This sequencing ensures proper muscle activation patterns and reduces the risk of shoulder impingement. The torso should remain relatively upright throughout the movement, with only slight forward lean at the start position to allow full arm extension.

## Practical Applications

**Programming Guidelines:**
- Beginners: 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps with moderate weight
- Intermediate: 3-4 sets of 6-10 reps with progressive loading
- Advanced: Vary rep ranges and grip positions within training cycles

**Load Selection:**
- Start with a weight that allows full range of motion with proper form
- Progress load by 2.5-5% when all sets can be completed with perfect technique
- Focus on controlled eccentric (2-3 seconds) and brief pause at peak contraction

**Grip Rotation Protocol:**
- Week 1-2: Close neutral grip focus
- Week 3-4: Wide pronated grip emphasis  
- Week 5-6: Single handle unilateral work
- Repeat cycle with progressive loading

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Excessive torso swing:** Using momentum reduces muscle activation and increases injury risk
⚠️ **Arm-dominant pulling:** Failing to initiate with scapular retraction reduces back muscle engagement
⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion:** Not allowing full arm extension or complete scapular retraction limits effectiveness
⚠️ **Forward head posture:** Allowing the head to drift forward places unnecessary stress on the cervical spine
⚠️ **Rounded lower back:** Loss of neutral spine position increases risk of lumbar injury
