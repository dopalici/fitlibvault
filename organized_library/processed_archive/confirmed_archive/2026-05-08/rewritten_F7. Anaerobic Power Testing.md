### F7. Anaerobic Power Testing
**ID:** F7
**Created:** 2025-06-05 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Anaerobic power testing measures maximum power output during brief, high-intensity efforts to assess phosphocreatine and glycolytic energy system capacity for training design and performance monitoring.

## Key Points
- Peak power assessment measures highest instantaneous power output during maximal efforts
- Mean power evaluation analyzes sustained power over 10-60 seconds to assess anaerobic capacity
- Fatigue index quantifies power decline during sustained efforts to evaluate neuromuscular fatigue resistance
- Protocol standardization ensures reliable results through proper warm-up, resistance settings, and recovery periods
- Sport-specific adaptations match testing protocols to movement patterns and energy system demands

## Deep Dive

### Assessment Components

**Peak Power Measurement**
Peak power represents the highest instantaneous power output achievable during maximal effort. This metric primarily reflects the phosphocreatine system's capacity and neuromuscular coordination during explosive movements. Testing typically uses cycle ergometers, force plates, or sport-specific equipment to capture maximum power generation within the first 5-10 seconds of effort, when the [[A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]] phosphocreatine system dominates energy production.

**Mean Power Analysis**
Mean power evaluation assesses sustained power output over specific durations, typically ranging from 10-60 seconds. This measurement reflects both anaerobic capacity and power endurance, providing insights into glycolytic system efficiency and the ability to maintain high power outputs as the phosphocreatine system depletes. The duration selected should align with the specific demands of the sport or training goal being assessed, connecting to [[B13. Strength Training Goals (Max Strength, Power, Hypertrophy, Endurance)]].

**Fatigue Index Calculation**
The fatigue index quantifies the rate of power decline during sustained maximal efforts, calculated as the percentage decrease from peak to minimum power output. This metric evaluates neuromuscular fatigue resistance and provides insights into [[A7. Muscle Physiology Fundamentals]] fiber type distribution and metabolic efficiency during high-intensity exercise.

### Testing Protocols

**Standardization Requirements**
Proper protocol standardization includes specific warm-up procedures (typically 5-10 minutes of progressive intensity), appropriate resistance settings based on body mass or predetermined loads, standardized duration parameters, and controlled recovery periods between trials. Environmental conditions, equipment calibration, and testing time of day should remain consistent to ensure reliable results, as outlined in [[F2. Performance Optimization Factors]].

**Sport-Specific Adaptations**
Testing protocols should be adapted to match the specific demands of target sports through appropriate movement patterns and energy system emphasis. For example, team sport athletes may benefit from protocols that mirror the intermittent nature of their competition demands, connecting to [[X4. Team Sports - General Considerations]] and the principles of [[E3. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)]].

## Practical Applications

**Testing Frequency**
Conduct anaerobic power testing every 4-6 weeks during training phases to monitor adaptations and adjust program intensity. Pre-season testing establishes baseline values, while in-season testing (every 6-8 weeks) tracks maintenance or changes in power capacity.

**Equipment Selection**
- Cycle ergometers: Wingate test protocol (30 seconds at 0.075 kg/kg body weight)
- Force plates: Vertical jump testing with power calculations
- Sport-specific equipment: Sport-specific movements with power measurement capabilities

**Data Interpretation**
Compare results to sport-specific normative data and individual baseline values. Focus on trends over time rather than single test results, and consider the relationship between peak power, mean power, and fatigue index when designing training interventions.

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ Inadequate warm-up leading to underestimation of true anaerobic power capacity
⚠️ Using generic protocols that don't match sport-specific movement patterns or energy system demands
⚠️ Inconsistent testing conditions (time of day, environmental factors, equipment settings) affecting reliability
⚠️ Focusing solely on peak power while ignoring mean power and fatigue index, missing important performance insights
⚠️ Testing too frequently (weekly) without allowing adequate recovery, leading to accumulated fatigue affecting results
