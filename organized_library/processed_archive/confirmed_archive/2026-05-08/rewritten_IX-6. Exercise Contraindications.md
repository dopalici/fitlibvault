### IX-6. Exercise Contraindications
**ID:** IX-6-CONTRAINDICATIONS
**Created:** 2025-04-24 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Exercise contraindications are medical conditions where exercise risks outweigh benefits - absolute contraindications require medical clearance before any exercise, while relative contraindications allow modified exercise with proper supervision.

## Key Points
- Pre-exercise screening identifies conditions that make exercise unsafe or require medical supervision
- Absolute contraindications require complete exercise cessation until medical clearance is obtained
- Relative contraindications may allow modified exercise with appropriate medical oversight and monitoring
- Fitness professionals must recognize their scope of practice limitations and refer to healthcare providers
- Proper documentation of screening and medical clearances protects both client safety and professional liability

## Deep Dive

Exercise contraindications represent clinical situations where the cardiovascular, metabolic, or physiological stress of exercise could potentially cause harm that outweighs therapeutic benefits. Understanding these contraindications is essential for safe exercise prescription and helps determine when medical evaluation is necessary before beginning or continuing an exercise program.

### Classification System

Contraindications are classified into two primary categories based on risk severity and the potential for safe exercise modification. This classification system helps fitness professionals make appropriate decisions about exercise safety and medical referral needs.

### Absolute Contraindications

These conditions require complete exercise cessation until the underlying condition is stabilized or treated by medical professionals. Exercise is prohibited regardless of intensity or supervision level:

**Acute Cardiovascular Events:**
- Recent significant change in resting ECG suggesting ischemia or recent myocardial infarction (within 2 days)
- Unstable angina pectoris
- Uncontrolled cardiac dysrhythmias causing symptoms or hemodynamic compromise
- Symptomatic severe aortic stenosis
- Acute myocarditis or pericarditis
- Suspected or known dissecting aneurysm

**Acute Systemic Conditions:**
- Uncontrolled symptomatic heart failure
- Acute pulmonary embolus or pulmonary infarction
- Acute systemic infection with fever, body aches, or swollen lymph glands

### Relative Contraindications

These conditions may allow for carefully modified exercise programs under appropriate medical supervision. The decision to exercise depends on individual risk-benefit analysis and medical oversight:

**Cardiovascular Conditions:**
- Left main coronary artery stenosis
- Moderate stenotic valvular heart disease
- Severe arterial hypertension (systolic BP >200 mmHg or diastolic BP >110 mmHg at rest)
- Tachyarrhythmias or bradyarrhythmias
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and other outflow tract obstructions
- High-degree atrioventricular block
- Ventricular aneurysm

**Metabolic and Systemic Conditions:**
- Electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia)
- Uncontrolled metabolic diseases (diabetes, thyrotoxicosis)
- Chronic infectious diseases
- Mental or physical impairments affecting exercise capacity
- Advanced pregnancy complications

### Screening and Assessment Process

Proper identification of contraindications requires systematic screening using validated tools and comprehensive health history assessment. The screening process should identify both obvious and subtle risk factors that might contraindicate exercise participation.

## Practical Applications

**Pre-Exercise Screening Protocol:**
1. Administer standardized screening questionnaires (PAR-Q+, ACSM Health/Fitness Facility Pre-participation Screening Questionnaire)
2. Conduct comprehensive health history interview including medications, symptoms, and family history
3. Document all identified risk factors and contraindications
4. Obtain written medical clearance for any identified contraindications before exercise participation
5. Establish facility emergency action plans and staff training protocols

**Managing Relative Contraindications:**
- Require physician evaluation with specific exercise prescription guidelines
- Implement continuous physiological monitoring (heart rate, blood pressure, symptoms)
- Begin with very low-intensity activities (40-50% heart rate reserve) with gradual progression
- Maintain direct supervision by qualified exercise professionals with emergency training
- Establish clear criteria for exercise termination (chest pain, excessive fatigue, dizziness)

**Documentation and Legal Requirements:**
- Maintain detailed records of all screening results and medical clearances
- Update health status assessments regularly (annually minimum, more frequently for high-risk individuals)
- Document any exercise program modifications and rationale
- Keep emergency contact information and current medication lists readily accessible

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Assuming stable conditions remain stable** - Medical conditions can deteriorate rapidly, requiring ongoing reassessment and communication with healthcare providers

⚠️ **Exercising clients with absolute contraindications** - Believing that "light exercise won't hurt" can be life-threatening with acute cardiac conditions

⚠️ **Inadequate or superficial screening** - Relying only on brief verbal questions without proper documentation, follow-up, or use of validated screening tools

⚠️ **Exceeding scope of practice boundaries** - Making independent medical decisions about exercise safety instead of appropriately referring to qualified healthcare providers

⚠️ **Ignoring medication effects on exercise response** - Not considering how cardiac medications, blood pressure medications, or other drugs affect exercise capacity and safety parameters

⚠️ **Failing to establish emergency protocols** - Not having clear procedures for medical emergencies or contraindication identification during exercise sessions
