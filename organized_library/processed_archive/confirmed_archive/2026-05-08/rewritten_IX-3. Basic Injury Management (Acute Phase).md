### IX-3. Basic Injury Management (Acute Phase)
**ID:** IX-3-INJURY-MANAGEMENT
**Created:** 2025-04-24 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Modern injury management follows PEACE (immediate care) and LOVE (ongoing recovery) protocols, emphasizing protection initially followed by gradual loading and movement rather than extended rest.

## Key Points
- Protect the injury for 1-3 days, then gradually introduce movement and loading
- Ice is primarily for pain relief, not healing - inflammation serves important biological functions
- Early return to pain-free activity promotes better healing than complete rest
- Seek professional help for severe pain, inability to bear weight, or significant swelling/deformity
- Patient education and positive expectations significantly impact recovery outcomes

## Deep Dive

### The Evolution from RICE to PEACE & LOVE
Traditional RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) protocol has evolved based on updated understanding of tissue healing. The new PEACE & LOVE approach recognizes that inflammation plays crucial roles in healing and that early, appropriate loading accelerates recovery.

### Immediate Post-Injury Management (PEACE)
The first 24-72 hours focus on protecting tissues while supporting natural healing processes.

**Protection (P):** Unload or restrict movement for 1-3 days to minimize further tissue damage and bleeding. This doesn't mean complete immobilization - protect from aggravating movements while allowing pain-free motion.

**Elevation (E):** Raise the injured limb above heart level when possible to promote fluid drainage and reduce swelling through gravity assistance.

**Avoid Anti-inflammatory Modalities (A):** Current evidence suggests avoiding NSAIDs and excessive ice application immediately post-injury. Inflammation serves important biological functions including debris clearance and tissue repair initiation.

**Compression (C):** Apply elastic bandaging or compression garments to limit swelling and provide mechanical support. Compression should be firm but not restrict circulation.

**Education (E):** Understanding the injury, expected recovery timeline, and benefits of active recovery improves outcomes and reduces anxiety.

### Ongoing Recovery Management (LOVE)
After the initial protective phase, focus shifts to promoting healing through controlled activity.

**Load (L):** Gradual mechanical stress within pain tolerance stimulates tissue healing and remodeling. Complete rest often delays recovery - tissues adapt to imposed demands, requiring progressive loading for optimal repair.

**Optimism (O):** Positive patient expectations and addressing fear-avoidance behaviors significantly influence recovery outcomes, as explored in behavioral approaches [[F4. Behavioral Psychology in Fitness]].

**Vascularisation (V):** Early return to pain-free cardiovascular activity increases blood flow to injured tissues, delivering nutrients and removing metabolic waste products.

**Exercise (E):** Active rehabilitation addressing mobility, strength, and proprioception should begin early in the recovery process, progressing systematically.

## Practical Applications

**First 24-48 Hours:**
- Apply compression bandage (not tight enough to restrict circulation)
- Elevate limb above heart level during rest periods
- Use ice for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours for pain relief only
- Avoid anti-inflammatory medications unless prescribed for specific conditions
- Begin gentle, pain-free range of motion within comfort limits

**Days 3-7:**
- Gradually increase weight-bearing and movement as tolerated
- Start light cardiovascular exercise that doesn't aggravate injury
- Progress range of motion exercises
- Begin basic strengthening exercises within pain limits

**Week 2 and Beyond:**
- Systematically progress loading and exercise complexity
- Address movement patterns and functional activities
- Include proprioception and balance training
- Gradually return to sport-specific or activity-specific movements

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Extended complete rest:** Prolonged immobilization leads to tissue weakness and delayed recovery

⚠️ **Excessive ice application:** Using ice for extended periods or for "healing" rather than pain relief can impair natural inflammatory processes

⚠️ **Ignoring pain signals:** Pushing through significant pain can worsen injury and delay healing

⚠️ **Premature return to full activity:** Returning to pre-injury activity levels without adequate rehabilitation increases re-injury risk

⚠️ **Over-relying on passive treatments:** Depending solely on rest, ice, and medication without active rehabilitation slows recovery
