### A4. Micronutrients
**ID:** A4-MICRONUTRIENTS
**Created:** 2025-04-23 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Vitamins and minerals are essential for energy production, immune function, and exercise recovery - while whole foods should be your primary source, athletes may need targeted supplementation for iron, vitamin D, and electrolytes based on individual testing.

## Key Points
- Food-first approach meets most micronutrient needs through varied, nutrient-dense eating
- Athletes have elevated requirements for iron, calcium, vitamin D, zinc, magnesium, and B vitamins
- Fat-soluble vitamins require dietary fat for absorption while some minerals compete for uptake
- Blood testing identifies true deficiencies more accurately than guessing with supplements
- Sweat losses during training significantly increase electrolyte replacement needs

## Deep Dive

### Vitamin Categories and Functions

**Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)**
These vitamins are stored in body fat and require dietary fat for optimal absorption. Vitamin D is crucial for calcium absorption, bone health, immune function, and muscle contraction. Vitamin E serves as a key antioxidant protecting cell membranes from oxidative damage. Vitamin K plays essential roles in blood clotting and bone mineralization.

**Water-Soluble Vitamins (B Complex, C)**
Not stored significantly in the body (except B12), requiring regular intake. B vitamins are crucial cofactors in energy metabolism pathways, with increased needs during high training volumes. Vitamin C functions as an antioxidant, supports collagen synthesis for connective tissue health, and enhances immune function.

### Mineral Requirements

**Major Minerals (>100mg/day needed)**
Include calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, sodium, potassium, and chloride. These electrolytes are critical for muscle contraction, nerve transmission, and fluid balance.

**Trace Minerals (<100mg/day needed)**
Iron enables oxygen transport through hemoglobin and supports cellular energy production. Zinc supports immune function and serves as a cofactor for over 300 enzymes. Iodine is essential for thyroid hormone production, while selenium functions as an antioxidant enzyme cofactor.

### Athletic Considerations

Athletes face increased micronutrient needs due to higher metabolic demands, increased turnover of red blood cells, and losses through sweat. Iron deficiency is particularly common in endurance athletes, especially females, due to increased needs and losses. Magnesium requirements increase with exercise volume, as it's involved in over 300 enzymatic reactions including energy metabolism.

- Study type: Narrative review
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive analysis of iron status in athletes)

## Practical Applications

**Daily Implementation:**
- Include colorful fruits and vegetables at each meal to cover vitamin and antioxidant needs
- Consume fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) with dietary fat sources for optimal absorption
- Time iron-rich foods away from calcium-rich foods and coffee/tea to maximize absorption
- Consider vitamin D3 supplementation (1000-4000 IU daily) if blood levels are below 30 ng/mL

**For Athletes:**
- Monitor iron status through annual blood testing (ferritin, hemoglobin, transferrin saturation)
- Increase electrolyte intake during heavy training periods or hot weather conditions
- Focus on magnesium-rich foods (leafy greens, nuts, seeds) or consider 200-400mg supplementation
- Prioritize B-vitamin rich foods during high training volumes (whole grains, lean meats, dairy)

**Absorption Optimization:**
- Take iron supplements on empty stomach with vitamin C source
- Consume calcium and magnesium separately from iron and zinc
- Space multivitamins away from high-fiber meals to improve mineral absorption

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Megadosing antioxidants**: High-dose vitamin C and E supplements may blunt training adaptations by interfering with beneficial oxidative stress signals

⚠️ **Ignoring absorption interactions**: Taking iron with calcium, coffee, or tea significantly reduces iron uptake

⚠️ **Supplementing without testing**: Many athletes supplement unnecessarily while missing actual deficiencies that could be identified through blood work

⚠️ **Neglecting food sources**: Whole foods provide micronutrients in bioavailable forms with complementary compounds that supplements often lack

⚠️ **Timing errors**: Taking all supplements together reduces absorption efficiency of competing minerals
