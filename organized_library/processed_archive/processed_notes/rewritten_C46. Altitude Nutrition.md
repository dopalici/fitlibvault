### C46. Altitude Nutrition

**ID:** C46-ALTITUDE-NUTRITION **Created:** 2025-06-05 (Updated: 2025-01-11)

## Quick Takeaway
Altitude nutrition focuses on supporting physiological adaptation to reduced oxygen availability through targeted hydration, iron optimization, carbohydrate emphasis, and strategic supplementation to minimize altitude sickness and maintain performance.

## Key Points
- Altitude exposure increases fluid losses and metabolic demands requiring adjusted nutrition strategies
- Iron status becomes critical for supporting increased red blood cell production during acclimatization
- Carbohydrate metabolism becomes more efficient at altitude, making it the preferred fuel source
- Antioxidant needs increase due to elevated oxidative stress from hypoxic conditions
- Strategic timing of nutrition interventions can minimize altitude sickness symptoms and support adaptation

## Deep Dive

### Physiological Challenges at Altitude
Altitude exposure creates a hypoxic environment that triggers multiple physiological adaptations. The reduced partial pressure of oxygen stimulates erythropoietin production, leading to increased red blood cell synthesis. This process places significant demands on iron stores and protein synthesis pathways ([[A64. Oxygen Sensing Pathways]], [[A39. Cardiovascular Adaptations to Training]]).

Metabolic efficiency shifts toward carbohydrate utilization as the body adapts to reduced oxygen availability. The respiratory quotient increases, indicating greater reliance on glucose metabolism over fat oxidation ([[A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]], [[A35. Metabolic Adaptations to Training]]).

### Hydration and Electrolyte Management
Altitude exposure significantly increases fluid losses through enhanced respiratory water loss and diuresis. The dry air and increased ventilation rate can lead to dehydration rates 1.5-2 times higher than at sea level. Strategic hydration protocols must account for these increased losses while maintaining electrolyte balance ([[C4. Hydration]], [[C5. Electrolytes]]).

### Iron and Hematological Support
Iron requirements increase substantially during altitude adaptation due to enhanced erythropoiesis. Athletes with marginal iron status may struggle with acclimatization and experience prolonged performance decrements. Pre-altitude iron assessment and optimization becomes crucial for successful adaptation ([[C6. Micronutrients (Detailed Roles & Considerations)]).

### Antioxidant Defense Systems
Hypoxic conditions paradoxically increase oxidative stress through altered mitochondrial function and inflammatory responses. Enhanced antioxidant support through both dietary sources and targeted supplementation may help manage this oxidative burden ([[C34. Antioxidants]], [[C41. Adaptogens in Sports]]).

## Research Evidence

**Fulco et al. (2007).** Rock climbing at altitude: energy requirements during a multi-day climb and effect of acute mountain sickness. *Journal of Applied Physiology*, 103(2), 428-438. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.01393.2006
- Key finding: Energy expenditure increased 300-500 kcal/day at altitude with carbohydrate becoming the preferred fuel source
- Study type: Observational field study
- Evidence quality: Medium (controlled field conditions, small sample size)

**Butterfield et al. (1992).** Increased energy intake minimizes weight loss in men at high altitude. *Journal of Applied Physiology*, 72(5), 1741-1748. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/jappl.1992.72.5.1741
- Key finding: Adequate energy intake (4000+ kcal/day) prevented the typical 1-1.5 kg weight loss during altitude exposure
- Study type: Controlled intervention study
- Evidence quality: High (controlled conditions, adequate sample size)

**Koehle et al. (2006).** Antioxidant supplementation and exercise-induced oxidative stress at altitude. *International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism*, 16(1), 35-54. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.16.1.35
- Key finding: Combined antioxidant supplementation (vitamins C, E, and α-lipoic acid) reduced markers of oxidative stress at 4300m
- Study type: Randomized controlled trial
- Evidence quality: High (double-blind, placebo-controlled design)

## Practical Applications

**Pre-Altitude Preparation (2-4 weeks before):**
- Assess and optimize iron status (ferritin >30 ng/mL for women, >40 ng/mL for men)
- Begin antioxidant supplementation (vitamin C 500mg, vitamin E 400 IU daily)
- Practice high-carbohydrate diet patterns (60-70% of total calories)

**During Altitude Exposure:**
- Increase fluid intake by 1.5-2 liters above sea level requirements
- Consume 8-12g carbohydrate per kg body weight daily
- Maintain regular meal timing to support metabolic adaptation
- Monitor urine color and body weight for hydration status

**Specific Supplementation Protocols:**
- Iron: 18-27mg daily if deficient (with vitamin C for absorption)
- Carbohydrate: 30-60g per hour during prolonged activities
- Electrolytes: 200-300mg sodium per hour of activity
- Antioxidants: Vitamin C (500mg), Vitamin E (400 IU), Alpha-lipoic acid (200mg)

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Ignoring pre-altitude iron status assessment - iron deficiency severely impairs acclimatization
⚠️ Reducing food intake due to altitude-induced appetite suppression - inadequate energy accelerates performance decline
⚠️ Over-hydrating without electrolyte replacement - can lead to hyponatremia at altitude
⚠️ Relying solely on supplementation without optimizing whole food nutrition patterns
