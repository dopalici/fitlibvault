### **EXL-83: Push-up (Standard & Variations)**

**ID:** EXL-83-BW-PUSHUP **Created:** 2025-05-01 (Updated: 2025-01-16)

## Quick Takeaway
The push-up is a versatile bodyweight exercise that builds upper body strength and core stability, with variations available for every fitness level from beginner to advanced.

## Key Points
- Targets chest, shoulders, and triceps while engaging core muscles for stability
- Progress from incline push-ups to standard, then advanced variations like decline or weighted
- Keep body in straight line, lower chest to floor level, and control both up and down phases
- Can be performed anywhere with no equipment needed
- Form quality matters more than quantity - maintain proper alignment throughout

## Deep Dive

### Movement Pattern and Muscle Activation
A push-up is a horizontal pushing movement [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]] that primarily targets the pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, and triceps brachii. Research shows push-ups activate the chest muscles at 64% of maximum voluntary contraction, making them highly effective for strength development (Kikuchi & Nakazato, 2017).

The exercise also demands significant core stabilization [[L4. Core Stability Training]], with EMG studies demonstrating continuous activation of the rectus abdominis, obliques, and transverse abdominis throughout the movement to maintain proper body alignment.

### Proper Form and Technique
Following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] principles:

**Setup Phase:**
- Position hands slightly wider than shoulder-width apart
- Extend legs back, supporting weight on hands and toes
- Create straight line from head to heels
- Engage core and glutes to prevent hip sagging

**Execution Phase:**
- Lower chest toward floor by bending elbows at 45-60 degrees from torso
- Descend until chest nearly touches floor or upper arms parallel to ground
- Press through hands to return to starting position
- Maintain rigid body line throughout entire movement

### Breathing Pattern
Implement proper breathing to enhance performance and stability:
- Inhale during the lowering (eccentric) phase
- Exhale during the pushing (concentric) phase
- Maintain core tension throughout breathing cycle

## Practical Applications

### Progression Pathway
**Beginner Level:**
- Wall push-ups: Stand arm's length from wall
- Incline push-ups: Hands on bench or step (12-24 inches high)
- Knee push-ups: Perform on knees instead of toes

**Intermediate Level:**
- Standard push-ups: 3 sets of 8-15 reps
- Wide-grip variations for chest emphasis
- Close-grip for triceps focus

**Advanced Level:**
- Decline push-ups: Feet elevated 12+ inches
- Plyometric push-ups: Explosive pressing with hand clap
- Weighted push-ups: 10-45 lbs additional load
- Single-arm progressions

### Training Integration
Incorporate push-ups into routines using [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]] guidelines:
- Beginners: 2-3 times per week, 2-3 sets
- Intermediate: 3-4 times per week, 3-4 sets
- Advanced: Daily practice possible with varied intensities

Apply [[B1. Progressive Overload]] through:
- Increased repetitions
- Advanced variations
- Tempo manipulation ([[B20. Eccentric Training]] - 3-5 second lowering phase)
- Added external resistance

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Hip sagging or piking** - Indicates weak core strength; regress to easier variation or focus on [[EXL-84 Plank (Standard & Variations)]]

⚠️ **Excessive elbow flaring** - Creates shoulder impingement risk; keep elbows at 45-60 degrees from torso

⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion** - Reduces effectiveness; lower chest to within 1-2 inches of floor

⚠️ **Forward head posture** - Strains neck; maintain neutral spine alignment throughout

⚠️ **Bouncing at bottom** - Eliminates muscle tension; control the movement through full range
