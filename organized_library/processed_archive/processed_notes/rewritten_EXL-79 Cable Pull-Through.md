### **EXL-79: Cable Pull-Through**

**ID:** EXL-79-CEX-PULLTHROUGH **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-03)

## Quick Takeaway
A beginner-friendly hip hinge exercise using a cable machine that teaches proper glute and hamstring activation while building strength in the posterior chain.

## Key Points
- Perfect for learning the hip hinge movement pattern before progressing to deadlifts
- Focus on driving hips forward and squeezing glutes hard at the top
- Keep arms straight - they're just holding the rope, not pulling it
- Start light to master the movement, then progressively add weight
- Excellent for glute activation and hamstring flexibility

## Deep Dive

### Movement Description
The cable pull-through is a hip hinge exercise using a low pulley cable machine with rope attachment. You pull the cable between your legs while extending your hips, primarily targeting the glutes and hamstrings. This exercise serves as an excellent teaching tool for the fundamental hip hinge pattern used in deadlifts and kettlebell swings.

### Target Muscles
- **Primary Movers (Agonists):** Gluteus Maximus, Hamstrings
- **Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Adductor Magnus, Erector Spinae (isometric stabilization)
- **Stabilizers:** Core musculature for spinal stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]], feet and ankles for ground contact

### Setup and Execution
**Setup:** Attach a rope handle to a low pulley cable. Stand facing away from the machine, straddling the cable. Reach down between your legs and grasp both rope ends with a neutral grip. Step forward until there's tension on the cable. Position feet shoulder-width apart with knees slightly bent.

**Execution:** 
1. Hinge forward at the hips while maintaining a straight back (neutral spine), allowing the rope to travel back between your legs
2. Initiate the return movement by driving your hips forward forcefully
3. Extend hips and knees to stand tall, squeezing glutes hard at the top
4. Keep arms relatively straight throughout - they act as levers, not primary movers
5. Control the eccentric phase, slowly hinging back at the hips while maintaining neutral spine
6. Feel the stretch in your hamstrings at the bottom position

**Breathing Pattern:** Exhale during the forward hip thrust (concentric phase), inhale during the backward hinge (eccentric phase).

## Research Evidence
While specific research on cable pull-throughs is limited, studies on hip hinge movements demonstrate their effectiveness for posterior chain development:

- Hip hinge exercises activate gluteus maximus significantly more than squatting movements (Contreras et al., 2013)
- Cable-based resistance training provides consistent tension throughout the range of motion, enhancing muscle activation (McMahon et al., 2014)
- Hip extension exercises improve athletic performance and reduce injury risk (Comfort et al., 2012)

## Practical Applications

### Progression Pathways
- **Load Progression:** Increase weight stack following progressive overload principles [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- **Volume Progression:** Increase reps/sets systematically [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- **Tempo Variations:** Add pauses at top for stronger glute squeeze, or slow eccentric phase [[B20. Eccentric Training]]
- **Exercise Progression:** Advance to kettlebell swings [[B9. Kettlebell Training]], Romanian deadlifts [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]] or [[EXL-37 Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]], or hip thrusts [[EXL-07 Barbell Hip Thrust]]

### Regression Options
- Decrease load to focus on movement quality
- Practice bodyweight glute bridges [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]
- Use resistance band pull-throughs [[B11. Resistance Bands]] for lighter resistance
- Focus on bodyweight hip hinge practice to master the movement pattern [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]]

### Programming Recommendations
- **Beginners:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps with light weight
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 8-12 reps with moderate to heavy weight
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times per week as part of lower body or full-body routines

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Rounding the lower back** - Maintain neutral spine throughout the movement
⚠️ **Pulling with arms** - The arms are passive; hip extension drives the movement
⚠️ **Squatting instead of hinging** - Movement should occur primarily at the hips, not knees
⚠️ **Incomplete hip extension** - Stand fully tall and squeeze glutes hard at the top
⚠️ **Using too much weight too soon** - Master the movement pattern before adding significant load

## Contraindications & Safety
- **Avoid with:** Acute low back pain, recent hamstring injuries, or inability to maintain neutral spine
- **Safety Priority:** Focus on proper hip hinge mechanics and spinal alignment following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]
- **Assessment:** Ensure adequate hip mobility and core stability before loading the movement
