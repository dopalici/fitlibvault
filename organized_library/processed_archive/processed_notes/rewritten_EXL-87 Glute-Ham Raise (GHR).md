### **EXL-87: Glute-Ham Raise (GHR)**

**ID:** EXL-87-BW-GHR **Created:** 2025-05-01 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The Glute-Ham Raise is an advanced bodyweight exercise that builds exceptional hamstring and glute strength through a unique knee-dominant movement pattern, requiring significant posterior chain strength to perform correctly.

## Key Points
- **Advanced exercise**: Requires existing hamstring strength - start with regressions if you can't perform 5+ controlled reps
- **Unique movement**: Combines knee flexion and hip extension in one fluid motion, unlike most hamstring exercises
- **Progression priority**: Master the eccentric (lowering) phase first, using hands for assistance on the way up
- **Setup critical**: Proper pad and foot plate adjustment determines exercise effectiveness and safety
- **Exceptional strength builder**: Develops functional posterior chain strength that transfers to sprinting, jumping, and deadlifting

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics and Setup
The Glute-Ham Raise is performed on a dedicated GHR apparatus [[VIII-2. Commercial Gym Equipment.md]], making it distinct from other bodyweight exercises [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]. The exercise involves lowering the torso by extending the knees while maintaining a rigid body position, then pulling back up using primarily the hamstrings and glutes.

**Proper Setup Protocol:**
- Adjust knee pad so knees rest comfortably without pressure points
- Position foot plate to secure feet firmly against it
- Start in upright position with torso perpendicular to floor
- Maintain neutral spine throughout the movement
- Cross arms over chest or place hands behind head

### Execution Technique
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]], the movement has two distinct phases:

**Eccentric Phase (Lowering):**
- Initiate by slowly extending knees while keeping body rigid from knees to head
- Maintain hip extension and neutral spine position
- Lower until torso reaches parallel to floor or maximum controllable range
- Inhale during this phase

**Concentric Phase (Rising):**
- Forcefully flex knees by pulling with hamstrings
- Simultaneously extend hips by squeezing glutes
- Return to upright starting position
- Exhale during this phase
- Use minimal hand push-off assistance only as needed

### Training Applications
The GHR fits into progressive overload [[B1. Progressive Overload]] through multiple advancement strategies. Research shows that exercises emphasizing knee flexion while maintaining hip extension create unique hamstring activation patterns not replicated by other movements (Bourne et al., 2017).

## Research Evidence

**Hamstring Activation Studies:**
- Bourne et al. (2017) demonstrated that GHRs produce higher hamstring EMG activity compared to traditional leg curls, particularly in the biceps femoris. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 31*(2), 341-349. DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001489

**Injury Prevention Research:**
- Petersen et al. (2011) showed that eccentric hamstring exercises (including GHR-type movements) reduced hamstring injury rates by 51% in soccer players. *American Journal of Sports Medicine, 39*(11), 2296-2303. DOI: 10.1177/0363546511419277

**Strength Development:**
- Delahunt et al. (2016) found that GHR training produced greater improvements in eccentric hamstring strength compared to traditional concentric exercises. *Journal of Sports Sciences, 34*(24), 2282-2288.

## Practical Applications

**Beginner Protocol (Regression Phase):**
- Start with 3 sets of 3-5 eccentric-only repetitions
- Use hands to push back to starting position
- Focus on 3-4 second controlled lowering
- Progress when you can perform 8+ controlled eccentrics

**Intermediate Protocol:**
- 3-4 sets of 5-8 full repetitions
- Minimal hand assistance on concentric phase
- 2-3 times per week with 48-72 hours recovery

**Advanced Progressions:**
- Add external load (5-25 lb weight plate held to chest)
- Increase tempo variations (5-second eccentrics)
- Perform 3-5 sets of 8-15 repetitions
- Integrate with comprehensive training volume [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Hip flexion during descent** - Bending at hips instead of maintaining rigid body position from knees to head

⚠️ **Excessive back arching** - Hyperextending lumbar spine to compensate for weak posterior chain

⚠️ **Rushing the eccentric** - Dropping down quickly instead of controlling the 3-4 second lowering phase

⚠️ **Over-reliance on hands** - Using excessive push-off assistance instead of building actual strength

⚠️ **Inadequate setup** - Improper pad/foot plate positioning leading to discomfort or ineffective movement
