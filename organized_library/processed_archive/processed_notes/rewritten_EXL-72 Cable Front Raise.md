### **EXL-72: Cable Front Raise**

**ID:** EXL-72-CEX-FRONTRAISE
**Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
An isolation exercise using cable machines to target the front deltoids with constant tension throughout the full range of motion, making it excellent for shoulder development and muscle activation.

## Key Points
- Use controlled movement - raise arms to shoulder height, pause briefly, then lower slowly
- Keep slight bend in elbows throughout to protect joints and maintain tension
- Start with lighter weight than you'd use for dumbbells due to constant cable tension
- Avoid leaning back or using momentum - let your front delts do the work
- Progress by adding weight, reps, or tempo variations before moving to compound pressing movements

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable front raise is an isolation exercise performed using a low pulley [[B10. Machine Training]] that specifically targets the anterior deltoids. Unlike free weights, cables provide constant tension throughout the entire range of motion, which can enhance muscle activation and time under tension for hypertrophy [[B3. Hypertrophy Training Principles]].

### Setup and Execution
Stand facing away from the cable machine with the handle attached to the low pulley. Maintain an upright posture with core engaged [[L4. Core Stability Training]] and arms positioned in front of your thighs. The key is maintaining proper body alignment throughout the movement while following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]].

Execute the movement by raising your arms forward and upward to shoulder height while maintaining a slight elbow bend. The constant tension from the cable system requires greater stabilization from supporting muscles compared to free weight variations.

### Breathing and Tempo
Exhale during the lifting phase (concentric) and inhale during the lowering phase (eccentric). Consider tempo variations including pauses at the top position or slower eccentric phases [[B20. Eccentric Training]] to increase training stimulus.

## Research Evidence
Research on shoulder isolation exercises demonstrates that front raises effectively activate the anterior deltoid while minimizing compensation from other muscle groups when performed with proper form. Cable variations may provide superior muscle activation compared to free weights due to the accommodating resistance curve and constant tension throughout the range of motion.

Studies on resistance training progression show that isolation exercises like cable front raises can effectively contribute to overall shoulder development when incorporated into a comprehensive program focused on [[B1. Progressive Overload]].

## Practical Applications

### Programming Recommendations
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps with light weight focusing on form
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 10-12 reps with moderate weight
- **Advanced:** Include tempo variations, pauses, or single-arm variations

### Load Selection
Start with approximately 20-30% less weight than you would use for dumbbell front raises due to the constant tension provided by cables. Progress according to [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]] guidelines.

### Progression Strategies
1. Increase load on weight stack
2. Add repetitions or sets
3. Implement tempo variations (2-second pause at top, 3-second eccentric)
4. Try different attachments (straight bar, rope, single handle)
5. Progress to single-arm variations for unilateral training
6. Advance to compound movements like overhead pressing [[EXL-05 Barbell Overhead Press (Standing)]], [[EXL-23 Dumbbell Shoulder Press (Seated)]]

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Using excessive weight leading to momentum and body swing
⚠️ Leaning backward to compensate for heavy loads
⚠️ Allowing elbows to bend significantly during the raising phase
⚠️ Raising arms above shoulder height, which can cause shoulder impingement
⚠️ Uncontrolled eccentric phase - missing the muscle-building benefits of the lowering portion

## Related Concepts
This exercise targets the anterior deltoid as primary mover with assistance from the pectoralis major (clavicular head), medial deltoid, serratus anterior, and biceps brachii (short head) as described in [[G-VII-1. Musculoskeletal Anatomy Overview]]. 

For those with shoulder issues, consider regression to resistance band variations [[B11. Resistance Bands]] or alternative exercises. The cable front raise can progress to free weight variations like the [[EXL-25 Dumbbell Front Raise]] or compound pressing movements.

Monitor progress using appropriate metrics [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]] while being mindful of contraindications [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]] related to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff issues.
