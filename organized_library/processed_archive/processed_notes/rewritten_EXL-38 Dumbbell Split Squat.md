### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
A unilateral lower-body exercise performed in a fixed staggered stance that primarily targets the quads and glutes while improving balance and addressing strength imbalances between legs.

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
- Stand with one foot forward, one back, holding dumbbells at your sides
- Lower straight down until back knee nearly touches floor, then drive up through front heel
- Keep 80% of weight on front foot, back foot only for balance
- Complete all reps on one side before switching legs
- Start with bodyweight only, then progress to dumbbells as balance improves

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
The dumbbell split squat is performed from a static staggered stance, distinguishing it from dynamic lunge variations. The primary movement occurs at the hip and knee joints of the front leg, with the exercise emphasizing vertical displacement of the center of mass rather than forward/backward movement patterns.

Research indicates that unilateral exercises like split squats can effectively address strength imbalances between limbs while providing similar muscle activation to bilateral movements (McCurdy et al., 2010). The static nature allows for better balance control compared to dynamic lunges, making it an excellent intermediate progression.

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
The front leg's quadriceps and gluteus maximus serve as primary movers, with EMG studies showing significant activation comparable to bilateral squats (Boudreau et al., 2009). The hip flexors of the rear leg undergo passive stretching, potentially improving hip mobility. Core musculature activation increases due to the unilateral stance demands for postural stability.

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
Following principles from [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]], proper setup involves:

**Setup Phase:**
- Hold dumbbells at sides with neutral grip
- Step into staggered stance (approximately 2-3 feet apart)
- Position 80% of body weight over front foot
- Maintain upright torso with core engagement

**Execution Phase:**
- Initiate descent by flexing both knees simultaneously
- Lower until back knee approaches floor (avoid contact)
- Keep front knee tracking over toes
- Drive through front heel to return to start position

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps per leg, bodyweight only
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 10-15 reps with 15-25 lb dumbbells
- **Advanced:** 4-5 sets of 12-20 reps with 30+ lb dumbbells

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
Implement [[B1. Progressive Overload]] through:
1. **Load Progression:** Increase dumbbell weight by 2.5-5 lbs when achieving upper rep range
2. **Volume Progression:** Add sets or reps following [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]] guidelines
3. **Tempo Variations:** Incorporate [[B20. Eccentric Training]] with 3-4 second lowering phases
4. **Range of Motion:** Progress to Bulgarian split squat (rear foot elevated)

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
Effective as a primary lower body movement 2-3 times per week, or as an accessory exercise following compound movements like [[EXL-01 Barbell Back Squat (High Bar & Low Bar)]]. Can substitute for or complement [[EXL-15 Barbell Lunge]] variations.

### **EXL-38: Dumbbell Split Squat**
⚠️ **Excessive back foot pressure** - Using rear leg as a crutch rather than balance aid reduces training effect
⚠️ **Forward knee collapse** - Allowing front knee to cave inward increases injury risk and reduces effectiveness
⚠️ **Insufficient depth** - Stopping descent too early limits range of motion and muscle activation
⚠️ **Forward torso lean** - Excessive forward lean shifts emphasis away from target muscles
⚠️ **Unstable stance** - Too narrow or wide stance compromises balance and movement quality
