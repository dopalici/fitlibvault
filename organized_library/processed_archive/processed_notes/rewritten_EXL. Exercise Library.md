## Research Evidence

The exercise selection in this library is based on biomechanical analysis and training effectiveness research. Compound movements like squats, deadlifts, and presses have been shown to produce greater strength gains and muscle activation compared to isolation exercises alone (Gentil et al., 2017).

Multi-joint exercises demonstrate superior transfer to functional activities and sports performance (Kritz et al., 2009). The inclusion of unilateral exercises addresses strength imbalances and improves stability (Speirs et al., 2016).

## Practical Applications

**Program Design Guidelines:**
- Select 1-2 exercises per major movement pattern
- Progress from bodyweight to loaded variations
- Use compound movements as primary exercises
- Add isolation work for specific muscle groups
- Include both bilateral and unilateral variations

**Equipment Availability Adaptations:**
- Home gym: Focus on bodyweight and dumbbell exercises
- Commercial gym: Utilize full range of equipment
- Travel: Emphasize bodyweight movements
- Rehabilitation: Start with machine-based exercises

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ Attempting advanced exercises without mastering basic movement patterns
⚠️ Focusing only on isolation exercises while neglecting compound movements
⚠️ Not progressing exercise difficulty as strength improves
⚠️ Ignoring unilateral training variations
⚠️ Selecting exercises based on preference rather than program goals
