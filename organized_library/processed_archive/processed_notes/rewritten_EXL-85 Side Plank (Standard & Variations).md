### **EXL-85: Side Plank (Standard & Variations)**

**ID:** EXL-85-BW-SIDEPLANK **Created:** 2025-05-01 (Updated: 2025-01-15)

## Quick Takeaway
The side plank is an isometric exercise that strengthens the obliques and lateral core stabilizers by holding the body in a straight line while lying on one side, supporting weight on the forearm and feet.

## Key Points
- Hold body in straight line from head to feet, avoiding hip sag or rotation
- Start with 15-30 second holds and progress to 60+ seconds with good form
- Focus on breathing steadily while maintaining core tension throughout
- Modify by dropping to knees or progress by adding leg lifts or arm movements
- Essential for lateral core stability and spine health

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Mechanics
The side plank is an isometric core stability exercise [[B19. Isometrics]] performed lying on one side, supporting the body on the forearm/hand and feet. It primarily targets the obliques and other lateral core stabilizers [[L4. Core Stability Training]], resisting lateral flexion of the spine.

### Primary Target Muscles
- **Primary Stabilizers:** Obliques (Internal & External - especially bottom side), Quadratus Lumborum (bottom side), Gluteus Medius/Minimus (bottom leg)
- **Secondary Stabilizers:** Transverse Abdominis, Rectus Abdominis, Erector Spinae, Latissimus Dorsi, Deltoids (supporting shoulder), Adductors (top leg)

### Proper Form and Technique
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]:

**Setup:** Lie on side with legs straight. Place bottom forearm on floor with elbow directly under shoulder. Place top hand on hip or extend towards ceiling. Engage core before lifting.

**Execution:** Lift hips off floor until body forms a straight line from head to feet. Avoid letting hips sag towards floor or pike upwards. Keep head and neck aligned with spine. Hold isometrically while maintaining tension in lateral core.

**Breathing:** Maintain steady, controlled breathing throughout the hold - avoid breath holding which can increase blood pressure unnecessarily.

### Progression Strategies
**Difficulty Level:** Beginner to Advanced (depending on variation and duration)

**Progression Path:**
- Increase hold duration following isometric principles [[B19. Isometrics]]
- Stack feet (reduces base of support)
- Lift top leg (increases challenge on supporting hip abductors/core)
- Add arm movements (reach through/up)
- Add hip dips (dynamic variation)
- Side plank on hand (straight arm, increases lever length)
- Add external load (hold dumbbell on top hip)
- Star side plank (top arm and leg extended)

**Regression Path:**
- Knee side plank (support on bottom knee bent at 90 degrees)
- Shorten hold durations to 10-15 seconds
- Perform with wider foot stance for more stability
- Master standard plank [[EXL-84 Plank (Standard & Variations)]] first

## Research Evidence
Research demonstrates the effectiveness of side planks for core stability and injury prevention:

- Side planks activate the quadratus lumborum and obliques significantly more than traditional crunches (McGill et al., 2003)
- Isometric side planks show high activation of the external oblique (>40% MVC) and moderate activation of internal oblique (Ekstrom et al., 2007)
- Progressive side plank training improves lateral core endurance and reduces low back pain risk (McGill, 2007)

## Practical Applications
- **Beginner Protocol:** Start with 15-30 second holds, 2-3 sets per side
- **Intermediate Protocol:** Progress to 45-60 second holds, 3 sets per side
- **Advanced Protocol:** 60+ second holds or add challenging variations
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times per week as part of core stability training [[L4. Core Stability Training]]
- **Integration:** Use as warm-up activation or as part of bodyweight training [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Hip sagging:** Most common error - maintain straight body line throughout
⚠️ **Hip rotation:** Avoid letting hips roll forward or backward
⚠️ **Shoulder creep:** Keep supporting shoulder away from ear
⚠️ **Head position:** Don't crane neck up or let it drop down
⚠️ **Breath holding:** Maintain steady breathing pattern throughout hold

## Contraindications & Safety
- **Conditions:** Acute shoulder pain/injury (on supporting side), acute hip or back pain
- **Safety Guidelines:** Ensure elbow/hand is directly under shoulder, maintain straight body line, stop if form breaks down or pain occurs
- **Modifications:** Adjust based on individual limitations following exercise contraindication guidelines [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]]
