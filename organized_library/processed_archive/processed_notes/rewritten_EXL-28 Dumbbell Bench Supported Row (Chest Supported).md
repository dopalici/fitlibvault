### **EXL-28: Dumbbell Bench Supported Row (Chest Supported)**

**ID:** EXL-28-DB-ROW-CHESTSUP **Created:** 2025-04-29 (Updated: 2025-01-14)

## Quick Takeaway
A beginner-friendly dumbbell rowing exercise performed on an incline bench that eliminates momentum and effectively targets the upper back muscles with reduced risk of lower back strain.

## Key Points
- Chest support eliminates momentum and lower back stress compared to bent-over rows
- Pull dumbbells toward your armpits, not your shoulders, leading with your elbows
- Focus on squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of each rep
- Start with lighter weights to master the scapular retraction pattern
- Excellent stepping stone to more advanced unsupported rowing variations

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The chest-supported dumbbell row is performed lying prone on an incline bench set at 30-45 degrees. This positioning provides several mechanical advantages over unsupported rowing variations by eliminating the need for spinal stabilization and reducing the tendency to use momentum.

The movement begins with scapular retraction (pulling shoulder blades together), followed by elbow flexion to bring the dumbbells toward the torso. This sequence ensures proper activation of the target musculature while maintaining joint safety.

### Setup and Execution
**Setup:** Position an incline bench at 30-45 degrees. Lie face down with your chest supported, feet planted firmly on the floor or bench supports. Grasp dumbbells with a neutral grip (palms facing each other), allowing arms to hang straight down.

**Execution:** Initiate the movement by retracting your scapulae, then pull the dumbbells up toward your torso/armpits, leading with your elbows. Keep elbows relatively close to your body and squeeze your back muscles at the top. Lower the dumbbells under control, allowing your shoulder blades to protract at the bottom for a full stretch.

**Breathing Pattern:** Exhale during the pulling phase (concentric), inhale during the lowering phase (eccentric).

### Target Muscle Groups
**Primary Movers:** Latissimus Dorsi, Middle Trapezius, Rhomboids, Teres Major - these muscles are responsible for scapular retraction and shoulder adduction.

**Secondary Movers:** Posterior Deltoid, Biceps Brachii, Brachialis, Brachioradialis - assist with the pulling motion and elbow flexion.

**Stabilizers:** Erector Spinae (minimal demand due to chest support), Rotator Cuff muscles for shoulder stability.

## Research Evidence
Research on chest-supported rowing exercises demonstrates superior muscle activation patterns compared to unsupported variations for beginners learning proper scapular mechanics. The stable position allows for greater focus on target muscle recruitment without compensatory patterns (Fenwick et al., 2009. An EMG analysis of muscle activation during pulling exercises. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 23(7), 2126-2135).

Studies indicate that supported rowing variations produce similar latissimus dorsi activation to unsupported rows while significantly reducing erector spinae activation, making them suitable for individuals with lower back concerns (Lehman et al., 2004. Variations in muscle activation during seated rows. Sports Biomechanics, 3(2), 151-165).

## Practical Applications
- **Beginner Programs:** Use 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps with moderate weight
- **Hypertrophy Focus:** 3-4 sets of 8-15 reps with 60-90 second rest periods
- **Strength Development:** 3-5 sets of 5-8 reps with heavier loads
- **Rehabilitation:** Light loads focusing on scapular movement quality
- **Bench Angle Adjustment:** Lower angles (30°) emphasize lats more, higher angles (45°) target middle traps

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Using momentum:** Lifting chest off the pad or swinging the weights reduces muscle activation
⚠️ **Pulling toward shoulders:** This shifts emphasis to upper traps instead of target muscles
⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion:** Failing to allow full scapular protraction at the bottom limits muscle stretch
⚠️ **Shoulder shrugging:** Elevating shoulders reduces middle trap and rhomboid activation
⚠️ **Excessive bench incline:** Angles above 45° can create uncomfortable shoulder positioning

## Safety Considerations
**Appropriate for:** Most fitness levels, particularly beneficial for those with lower back issues due to reduced spinal loading demands.

**Contraindications:** Acute shoulder injuries may require modification or avoidance. Always ensure proper [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] and bench stability before beginning.

**Assessment Metrics:** Track load progression per [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]], monitor form quality, and assess scapular movement patterns.
