### Mediterranean Diet
**ID:** C10-MEDITERRANEAN-DIET **Created:** 2025-04-26 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The Mediterranean Diet is a research-backed eating pattern emphasizing olive oil, fish, vegetables, and whole grains that significantly reduces cardiovascular disease risk and supports overall health and longevity.

## Key Points
- Prioritizes olive oil as primary cooking fat, fish twice weekly, and abundant vegetables and fruits
- Emphasizes whole grains over refined grains with daily inclusion of legumes, nuts, and seeds
- Limits red meat to monthly consumption while minimizing processed foods and added sugars
- Reduces inflammation markers and improves endothelial function through antioxidant-rich foods
- Provides optimal nutrition for active individuals with adequate carbohydrates for fuel and anti-inflammatory compounds for recovery

## Deep Dive

The Mediterranean Diet (MedDiet) reflects the traditional dietary patterns of populations in Mediterranean regions (e.g., Greece, Southern Italy, Spain). It is not a single prescribed diet but rather a pattern characterized by high consumption of plant-based foods and healthy fats. It is one of the most researched dietary patterns and consistently linked to significant health benefits, particularly cardiovascular health.

### Core Components

**High Intake Foods (Daily):**
- Fruits and Vegetables (diverse, abundant - aim for 5-9 servings daily)
- Whole Grains (breads, pasta, rice - 3-6 servings daily)
- Legumes (beans, lentils, peas - 3+ servings weekly)
- Nuts and Seeds (1 ounce daily)
- Olive Oil (especially Extra Virgin) as the principal source of added fat for balanced [[A3. Macronutrients]]
- Fish and Seafood (especially fatty fish rich in Omega-3s, consumed ≥2 times/week)

**Moderate Intake Foods (Weekly):**
- Poultry (2-3 servings)
- Dairy products (primarily yogurt and cheese - 1-2 servings daily)
- Eggs (2-4 per week)
- Wine (moderate consumption, typically with meals - optional, 1 glass for women, 2 for men)

**Low Intake Foods (Monthly or less):**
- Red Meat and Processed Meats (2-3 servings monthly)
- Sweets, sugary drinks, pastries
- Refined Grains
- Butter and other saturated fats

### Health Benefits and Mechanisms

**Cardiovascular Health:** The Mediterranean Diet demonstrates strong evidence for reducing cardiovascular events through multiple mechanisms. It improves blood pressure, enhances lipid profiles, supports endothelial function, and reduces inflammatory markers through mechanisms related to [[C33. Anti-Inflammatory FoodsDiets]]. The landmark PREDIMED trial provided definitive evidence with over 7,400 participants showing significant cardiovascular protection.

**Metabolic Health:** Associated with reduced Type 2 Diabetes risk and improved glycemic control through high fiber intake and stable blood sugar patterns from whole food carbohydrates.

**Cognitive Function:** Linked to slower rates of cognitive decline and reduced risk of neurodegenerative diseases, likely due to anti-inflammatory compounds and omega-3 fatty acids from fish consumption.

**Weight Management:** Supports healthy weight management through high fiber content (25-35g daily), nutrient density, and satiety value from whole foods, though not primarily designed as a weight-loss diet.

## Research Evidence

**Estruch, R., et al. (2018).** Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease with a Mediterranean Diet Supplemented with Extra-Virgin Olive Oil or Nuts. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 378(25), e34. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389
- Key finding: 30% reduction in major cardiovascular events in high-risk individuals
- Study type: RCT (PREDIMED Study with 7,447 participants)
- Evidence quality: High (Large-scale randomized controlled trial)

**Martínez-González, M.A., et al. (2019).** The Mediterranean Diet and Cardiovascular Health. *Circulation Research*, 124(5), 779-798. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.118.313348
- Key finding: Comprehensive review confirming cardiovascular benefits through multiple mechanisms
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (Comprehensive analysis of multiple high-quality studies)

**Sofi, F., et al. (2010).** Adherence to Mediterranean diet and health status: meta-analysis. *BMJ*, 341, c4671. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c4671
- Key finding: 9% reduction in overall mortality and 9% reduction in cardiovascular mortality
- Study type: Meta-analysis (1.5+ million participants)
- Evidence quality: High (Large meta-analysis with consistent findings)

## Practical Applications

**Daily Implementation:**
- Use 2-3 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil for cooking and salad dressing
- Consume 2-3 pieces of fruit and 3-4 servings of vegetables daily
- Choose whole grain bread, brown rice, or whole wheat pasta over refined versions
- Include 1 ounce (small handful) of unsalted nuts as snacks

**Weekly Planning:**
- Include fish/seafood in 2-3 meals (prioritize fatty fish like salmon, sardines, mackerel)
- Prepare 2-3 legume-based meals (lentil soup, chickpea salad, bean stew)
- Limit red meat to 1-2 small servings maximum

**Fitness & Performance Considerations:**
- Provides 45-65% calories from carbohydrates to fuel training through [[C27. Nutrition for Endurance Performance (Carb Loading)]]
- Rich in essential [[C6. Micronutrients (Detailed Roles & Considerations)]] and [[C34. Antioxidants]] supporting recovery
- Anti-inflammatory components from [[C33. Anti-Inflammatory FoodsDiets]] may enhance recovery between training sessions
- For muscle gain goals [[C25. Nutrition for Muscle Gain (Hypertrophy)]], increase fish, Greek yogurt, or legume portions to meet higher protein requirements

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Using regular olive oil instead of extra virgin** - Extra virgin contains significantly more beneficial polyphenols and antioxidants

⚠️ **Focusing only on individual "superfoods"** - The pattern's benefits emerge from the overall combination and synergy of foods, not isolated components

⚠️ **Assuming all fats provide equal benefits** - The diet specifically emphasizes monounsaturated fats from olive oil and omega-3s from fish, not indiscriminate fat intake

⚠️ **Ignoring portion control principles** - While emphasizing nutrient-dense foods, appropriate portions remain important for weight management goals
