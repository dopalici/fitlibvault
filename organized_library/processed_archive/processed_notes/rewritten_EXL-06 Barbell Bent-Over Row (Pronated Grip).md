### **EXL-06: Barbell Bent-Over Row (Pronated Grip)**
- Targets entire posterior chain with emphasis on lats, rhomboids, and middle traps
- Requires solid hip hinge mechanics and core stability before loading heavily
- Pull bar to lower chest/upper abdomen with elbows at 45-60 degrees from torso
- Start with lighter weights to master form - poor technique leads to back injury
- Progress systematically through load, reps, or tempo variations

### **EXL-06: Barbell Bent-Over Row (Pronated Grip)**
The barbell bent-over row is a compound, multi-joint pulling exercise that serves as a cornerstone movement for posterior chain development. Research demonstrates that rowing movements are essential for balanced shoulder health and posture correction in our forward-head, rounded-shoulder society (Konstantinidou et al., 2017).

### **EXL-06: Barbell Bent-Over Row (Pronated Grip)**
The pronated grip barbell row preferentially targets the latissimus dorsi, middle and lower trapezius, and rhomboids as primary movers. Surface EMG studies show significant activation of the posterior deltoid and biceps complex as secondary movers, while the erector spinae provides crucial isometric stabilization (Fenwick et al., 2009). Understanding proper muscle activation patterns from [[G-VII-1. Musculoskeletal Anatomy Overview]] helps optimize technique.

### **EXL-06: Barbell Bent-Over Row (Pronated Grip)**
Proper setup requires mastering the hip hinge pattern from [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]]. The torso should reach approximately 15-45 degrees above parallel to maximize lat recruitment while maintaining spinal safety. Core bracing techniques from [[L4. Core Stability Training]] are essential for spinal protection under load.

The pulling motion should initiate with scapular retraction, followed by elbow drive toward the hip. Bar contact point at the lower chest/upper abdomen optimizes mechanical advantage and muscle activation patterns. Maintaining the pronated grip width at 1.5x shoulder width provides optimal leverage.

### **EXL-06: Barbell Bent-Over Row (Pronated Grip)**
⚠️ **Lower back rounding** - Maintain neutral spine throughout entire range of motion
⚠️ **Excessive hip heave** - Keep torso angle consistent; don't use momentum to lift weight
⚠️ **Pulling too high** - Bar should contact lower chest/upper abdomen, not neck or upper chest  
⚠️ **Insufficient range of motion** - Full scapular protraction at bottom, full retraction at top
⚠️ **Ego lifting** - Master technique with lighter loads before progressing weight
