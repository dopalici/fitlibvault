### **EXL-64: Hip Adduction (Machine)**

**ID:** EXL-64-MEX-HIPADD **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
A beginner-friendly isolation exercise that targets the inner thigh muscles using a machine to squeeze legs together against resistance pads.

## Key Points
- Targets all five adductor muscles of the inner thigh effectively
- Beginner-friendly with controlled movement pattern on stable machine
- Progress by increasing weight, reps, or adding tempo variations
- Essential for hip stability and injury prevention in athletes
- Complements compound movements like squats and lunges

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
Hip adduction involves bringing the legs toward the midline of the body against resistance. This seated machine exercise provides a stable platform to isolate the adductor muscle group while maintaining proper spinal alignment throughout the movement.

### Primary Muscle Activation
The exercise targets five key muscles of the inner thigh:
- **Adductor Magnus** (largest, provides most force)
- **Adductor Longus** (contributes to hip flexion)
- **Adductor Brevis** (shortest of the group)
- **Pectineus** (also assists hip flexion)
- **Gracilis** (crosses both hip and knee joints)

### Execution Protocol
**Setup Phase:**
- Adjust machine so legs start in comfortable abducted position
- Sit upright with back supported against pad
- Position inner thighs/knees against outside of resistance pads
- Engage core musculature for stability
- Grip handles to maintain upper body position

**Movement Phase:**
- Exhale while squeezing legs together through full range of motion
- Focus on contracting inner thigh muscles rather than using momentum
- Pause briefly at peak adduction for maximum muscle activation
- Inhale while slowly controlling return to starting position
- Maintain tension throughout eccentric (lengthening) phase

## Research Evidence

Research on hip adductor strengthening demonstrates significant benefits for athletic performance and injury prevention:

- Delahunt et al. (2011) found that adductor strengthening reduced groin injury risk in soccer players by 41% over one season. Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports, 21(3), 493-500.

- Thorborg et al. (2014) demonstrated that eccentric adductor strengthening was more effective than stretching for treating long-standing groin pain in athletes. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 48(14), 1089-1094.

- Tyler et al. (2002) showed that athletes with adductor strength ratios below 80% of abductor strength had 17 times higher risk of groin strain. The American Journal of Sports Medicine, 30(5), 680-683.

## Practical Applications

### Programming Recommendations
- **Beginners:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps, 2x per week
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 10-12 reps with heavier load
- **Advanced:** Include tempo variations (3-second eccentric, 2-second pause)

### Load Selection
Start with weight that allows controlled movement through full range of motion. Increase load by 5-10% when you can complete all sets with perfect form and 2+ extra reps in reserve.

### Integration with Training
- Perform after compound lower body exercises
- Use as activation exercise before sports requiring lateral movement
- Include in injury prevention protocols for groin strains

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Using momentum instead of controlled muscle contraction** - Focus on smooth, deliberate movement patterns

⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion** - Ensure full adduction and complete return to starting position

⚠️ **Uncontrolled eccentric phase** - Maintain tension while slowly returning to start position

⚠️ **Improper machine setup** - Adjust starting position to allow comfortable but challenging range of motion

⚠️ **Lifting hips or shifting position** - Maintain seated posture with back against pad throughout movement
