### C44. Competition Day Nutrition

**ID:** C44-COMPETITION-DAY-NUTRITION 
**Created:** 2025-06-05 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Competition day nutrition requires precise timing and selection of carbohydrates, fluids, and electrolytes to maintain energy availability and optimize performance while preventing gastrointestinal distress.

## Key Points
- Pre-competition meals should be consumed 3-4 hours before events to optimize glycogen stores and minimize GI issues
- During-event fueling becomes critical for activities lasting longer than 60-90 minutes
- Carbohydrate intake of 30-60g per hour maintains blood glucose during endurance events
- Hydration strategies must account for sweat rate, environmental conditions, and event duration
- Post-competition nutrition should begin within 30 minutes to initiate recovery processes

## Deep Dive

Competition day nutrition represents the culmination of training adaptations and nutritional periodization, requiring precise implementation of evidence-based strategies to optimize athletic performance while managing physiological stress.

### Pre-Competition Nutrition Strategy

The pre-competition phase focuses on optimizing substrate availability through strategic carbohydrate loading protocols [[C27. Nutrition for Endurance Performance (Carb Loading)]] and maintaining optimal hydration status [[C4. Hydration]]. Athletes should consume familiar foods 3-4 hours before competition to allow adequate digestion and gastric emptying while maximizing glycogen stores.

Pre-competition meals should emphasize moderate to high glycemic index carbohydrates (1-4g/kg body weight) with minimal fiber, fat, and protein to reduce gastrointestinal burden. Caffeine intake of 3-6mg/kg body weight can enhance performance when consumed 30-60 minutes before competition, though individual tolerance varies significantly.

### During-Event Fueling Protocols

For events exceeding 60-90 minutes, during-event nutrition becomes performance-limiting. Carbohydrate intake recommendations range from 30-60g per hour for events lasting 1-2.5 hours, increasing to 60-90g per hour for ultra-endurance events lasting over 2.5 hours.

Multiple transportable carbohydrates (glucose:fructose ratios of 2:1) optimize absorption rates and minimize gastrointestinal distress [[C5. Pre, During, and Post-Exercise Nutrition]]. Electrolyte replacement becomes critical in hot environments or during prolonged sweating, with sodium intake of 200-700mg per hour maintaining fluid balance [[C5. Electrolytes]].

### Sport-Specific Considerations

Different sports require tailored approaches based on exercise intensity, duration, and logistical constraints. Intermittent sports may benefit from strategic fueling during breaks, while endurance sports require continuous intake strategies [[X. Sports-Specific Training]].

Environmental factors significantly influence nutrition needs, with heat stress requiring enhanced fluid and electrolyte strategies [[C47. Heat Stress Nutrition]]. Multi-day competitions necessitate comprehensive recovery nutrition between events [[C49. Multi-Day Event Nutrition]].

### Gastrointestinal Management

Gastrointestinal distress represents a primary cause of performance decrements during competition. Strategic gut training during training phases improves tolerance to competition nutrition protocols [[C32. Gut Health and Microbiome]]. Athletes should practice race-day nutrition strategies during training to identify optimal foods, timing, and quantities.

Low-FODMAP approaches may benefit athletes prone to GI issues, though individual responses vary considerably. Probiotic supplementation may support gut health during high-stress competition periods.

## Research Evidence

- Key finding: Carbohydrate intake of 30-60g/hour optimizes performance in events lasting 1-2.5 hours
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive analysis of controlled trials)

- Key finding: Pre-competition meals consumed 3-4 hours before events optimize glycogen availability while minimizing GI distress
- Study type: Position statement with systematic evidence review
- Evidence quality: High (expert consensus based on extensive research)

**Jeukendrup, A. (2014).** A step towards personalized sports nutrition: carbohydrate intake during exercise. *Sports Medicine*, 44(1), 25-33. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-014-0148-z
- Key finding: Multiple transportable carbohydrates increase oxidation rates up to 1.75g/min compared to single carbohydrate sources
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (meta-analysis of controlled trials)

## Practical Applications

**3-4 Hours Before Competition:**
- Consume 1-4g carbohydrates per kg body weight
- Include 150-300ml fluid every 15-20 minutes
- Choose familiar, low-fiber foods (oatmeal, banana, toast)
- Avoid high-fat, high-protein, or novel foods

**1 Hour Before Competition:**
- Small carbohydrate snack (30-60g) if needed
- Final hydration check (pale yellow urine)
- Caffeine intake (3-6mg/kg) for enhanced performance

**During Competition (>60 minutes):**
- 30-60g carbohydrates per hour (events 1-2.5 hours)
- 60-90g carbohydrates per hour (events >2.5 hours)
- 150-250ml fluid every 15-20 minutes
- 200-700mg sodium per hour in hot conditions

**Immediately Post-Competition:**
- Begin carbohydrate intake within 30 minutes (1-1.2g/kg)
- Include high-quality protein (20-25g)
- Prioritize fluid replacement (150% of fluid losses)

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Trying new foods or supplements on competition day** - Always practice nutrition strategies during training
⚠️ **Over-consuming fluids leading to hyponatremia** - Match fluid intake to sweat losses and include electrolytes
⚠️ **Ignoring individual tolerance** - Gastrointestinal responses vary significantly between athletes
⚠️ **Focusing only on during-event nutrition** - Pre-competition preparation is equally important
⚠️ **Neglecting post-competition recovery** - Early nutrition intervention optimizes subsequent performance
