The seated row with close/neutral grip is a fundamental [[B10. Machine Training]] exercise that teaches proper pulling mechanics while providing chest support for stability. This makes it ideal for beginners learning to activate their posterior chain muscles effectively.

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
**Primary Movers (Agonists):** Rhomboids, Trapezius (Middle), Latissimus Dorsi - these muscles are responsible for the pulling motion and scapular retraction that defines good posture.

**Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Teres Major, Posterior Deltoid, Biceps Brachii, Brachialis, Brachioradialis - these assist in the pulling motion and arm flexion.

**Stabilizers:** Erector Spinae, [[L4. Core Stability Training]] musculature, Rotator Cuff. The chest support significantly reduces stabilizer demand compared to free weight alternatives, allowing focus on learning the movement pattern.

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
Following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] principles:

**Setup Phase:**
- Adjust seat height so chest sits firmly against pad when arms are fully extended
- Grasp close or neutral grip handle with both hands
- Plant feet firmly on floor or footrests
- Allow shoulders to protract (move forward) slightly at start position

**Movement Phase:**
- Initiate by retracting scapulae (pinch shoulder blades together)
- Exhale and pull handle toward lower chest/upper abdomen
- Keep elbows close to body throughout the pull
- Focus on squeezing mid-back muscles at peak contraction
- Pause briefly at full contraction
- Inhale and slowly return handle to starting position
- Allow controlled protraction of shoulder blades for full stretch

**Breathing Pattern:** Exhale during pull (concentric phase), inhale during return (eccentric phase).

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
Machine-based rowing exercises have been shown to effectively activate the rhomboids and middle trapezius, which are critical for scapular stability and postural control (Fenwick et al., 2009). The chest-supported position allows for greater focus on target muscles while reducing compensatory movement patterns commonly seen in unsupported rowing variations.

Electromyographic studies demonstrate that close-grip rowing positions emphasize rhomboid and middle trapezius activation more than wide-grip variations, making this exercise particularly effective for addressing postural imbalances (Lehman et al., 2004).

Following [[B1. Progressive Overload]] principles:
- **Load Progression:** Increase weight stack by 5-10 lbs when current weight allows 12+ reps with perfect form
- **Volume Progression:** Add sets or reps within [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]] guidelines
- **Tempo Variations:** 2-second pause at peak contraction, or 3-second [[B20. Eccentric Training]] lowering phase
- **Handle Variations:** Progress to wider grips or single handles for different muscle emphasis
- **Equipment Progression:** Advance to [[EXL-74 Cable Seated Row (Various grips)]], then to free weight options like [[EXL-06 Barbell Bent-Over Row (Pronated Grip)]] or [[EXL-27 Dumbbell Row (Single Arm)]]

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
- **Beginners:** 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps, 2-3x per week
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 6-15 reps depending on training phase
- **Focus on form over load for first 4-6 weeks of training**

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
⚠️ **Shrugging shoulders** - leads to neck tension and reduces target muscle activation
⚠️ **Using momentum** - reduces muscle activation and increases injury risk
⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion** - especially failing to fully retract and protract shoulder blades
⚠️ **Pulling primarily with arms** - biceps take over instead of back muscles doing the work
⚠️ **Poor chest pad position** - compromises stability and proper movement mechanics

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
Following [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]]:
- Acute shoulder impingement or rotator cuff injuries
- Recent elbow injuries (tennis elbow, golfer's elbow)
- Chest support makes this generally safe for those with lower back issues

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
- Ensure proper seat and chest pad adjustment before beginning
- Start with lighter weight to master movement pattern
- Maintain controlled tempo throughout entire range of motion
- Stop if sharp pain occurs in shoulders or elbows

### **EXL-57: Seated Row (Machine - Close Grip/Neutral)**
Track progress using [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]] methods:
- **Load Progression:** Weight used for target rep ranges
- **Volume Capacity:** Maximum reps at submaximal loads
- **Form Quality:** Assess scapular movement, control, and range of motion
- **Postural Improvements:** Measure forward head posture and rounded shoulder corrections over time
