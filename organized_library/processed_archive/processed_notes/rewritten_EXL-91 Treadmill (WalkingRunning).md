### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**
Treadmill walking/running provides controlled cardiovascular training with adjustable speed and incline, suitable for all fitness levels from beginner walking to advanced interval protocols.

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**
- Start with comfortable walking pace and gradually increase speed or incline over weeks
- Avoid gripping handrails excessively - this reduces calorie burn and alters natural movement
- Use the safety clip and know emergency stop procedures before starting
- Focus on maintaining natural gait with upright posture and forward gaze
- Track heart rate zones rather than machine calorie estimates for better workout monitoring

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**
The treadmill is among the most common cardiovascular exercise machines [[VIII-2. Commercial Gym Equipment.md]], featuring a motorized belt that allows controlled indoor walking, jogging, or running. The ability to precisely adjust speed (typically 0.1-15+ mph) and incline (0-15%+ grade) makes it versatile for various fitness levels and training goals.

**Primary Movers:**
- Quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes, and calves work dynamically during locomotion

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**
Detailed running mechanics are covered in [[E6. Running (Technique, Programming)]] and [[A16. Biomechanics Gait Analysis]].

**Setup:**
- Attach safety clip to clothing before starting
- Begin standing on side rails, not the belt
- Start belt at slow walking speed (1-2 mph) before stepping on

**Execution:**
- Step onto moving belt carefully, maintaining natural gait pattern
- Keep posture upright with slight forward lean from ankles, not waist
- Engage core and look forward, not down at feet
- Use handrails minimally - only light touch for balance if absolutely necessary
- Land with feet under your center of gravity to avoid overstriding

**Breathing:**
- Establish rhythmic breathing pattern appropriate for intensity
- For steady-state work, aim for conversational pace breathing

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**

**Progression Paths** following [[B1. Progressive Overload]]:
- Increase speed incrementally (0.1-0.2 mph weekly)
- Increase incline gradually (1-2% weekly)
- Extend duration (10-15% weekly increases)
- Incorporate [[E4. Interval Training (General)]] or [[E3. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)]]
- Reduce handrail dependence

**Regression Paths:**
- Start with slower speeds (1-2 mph walking)
- Use zero incline initially
- Begin with shorter sessions (10-15 minutes)
- Allow light handrail use for balance while learning
- Progress walking → power walking → jogging → running

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**

Treadmill training effectiveness is well-established across multiple populations:

- Systematic reviews demonstrate treadmill training significantly improves VO₂max, with effect sizes of 0.5-1.2 depending on protocol intensity and duration (Garber et al., 2011)
- Incline walking at 5-15% grade increases energy expenditure by 30-60% compared to level walking at the same speed (Minetti et al., 2002)
- Handrail support reduces energy expenditure by 15-25% and alters gait mechanics (Berling et al., 2006)

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**

**For Beginners:**
- Start with 15-20 minutes at 2.5-3.5 mph, 0% incline
- Focus on consistent daily walking before increasing intensity
- Use 1% incline minimum to better simulate outdoor conditions

**For Weight Loss:**
- Combine steady-state sessions (45-60 minutes) with interval work
- Target heart rate zones using [[E13. Heart Rate Zone Training]]
- Include incline intervals (5-10% grade for 2-5 minutes)

**For Athletic Training:**
- Use for controlled speed work and hill training
- Implement specific interval protocols matching sport demands
- Monitor with objective metrics rather than machine estimates

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**

⚠️ **Excessive handrail gripping** - Reduces energy expenditure and creates poor movement patterns

⚠️ **Overstriding** - Landing with feet too far forward increases impact and injury risk

⚠️ **Looking down constantly** - Compromises posture and can cause neck strain

⚠️ **Starting too fast** - Begin conservatively and progress gradually to avoid injury

⚠️ **Ignoring safety features** - Always use safety clip and know emergency stop location

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**

**Medical Conditions** requiring clearance [[IX-6 Exercise Contraindications.md]], [[Q5. Exercise Considerations for Health Conditions Overview]]:
- Acute lower extremity injuries (knee, ankle, hip)
- Severe balance disorders or vertigo
- Uncontrolled cardiovascular conditions
- Recent orthopedic surgery

**Safety Protocols:**
- Always attach safety clip before starting
- Start belt slowly before stepping on
- Maintain proper hydration [[C4. Hydration]] during longer sessions
- Know emergency stop procedures
- Step off carefully onto side rails when finished

### **EXL-91: Treadmill (Walking/Running)**

**Objective Measures** [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]]:
- Time, distance, speed, and incline settings
- Heart rate response using [[E13. Heart Rate Zone Training]]
- Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE 6-20 scale)

**Note on Calorie Estimates:**
Machine calorie displays are often inaccurate (±20-40% error) as noted in [[VIII-3. Wearable Technology (Fitness Trackers, Smartwatches, HRMs)]]. Use heart rate zones and RPE for better intensity monitoring.
