### **EXL-89: Abdominal Crunch / Sit-up (Floor or Bench)**

**ID:** EXL-89-BW-CRUNCH **Created:** 2025-05-01 (Updated: 2025-01-11)

## Quick Takeaway
Crunches and sit-ups are beginner-friendly spinal flexion exercises that primarily target the rectus abdominis, though modern research suggests core stability exercises may be more beneficial for functional strength and spine health.

## Key Points
- Crunches lift only head/shoulders (safer), while sit-ups lift entire torso (higher injury risk)
- Focus on controlled movement and breathing - avoid pulling on neck or using momentum
- Consider progressing to stability-based core exercises for better functional strength
- Limit if you have neck/back issues or prefer spine-neutral training approaches
- Start with 2-3 sets of 10-15 reps, focusing on form over quantity

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Classification and Mechanics
This common [[B7. Bodyweight Training]] exercise involves spinal flexion to target the anterior core muscles. The crunch variant lifts only the head and shoulders, while the full sit-up brings the entire torso to an upright position. Both exercises primarily recruit the rectus abdominis through concentric contraction during the lifting phase.

**Difficulty Level:** Beginner

### Target Muscles
- **Primary Movers (Agonists):** Rectus Abdominis
- **Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Obliques (Internal & External), Hip Flexors (especially active during sit-ups)
- **Stabilizers:** Transverse Abdominis

### Proper Form and Technique
Following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] principles:

**Setup (Crunch):** Lie supine on floor or mat, knees bent, feet flat on floor hip-width apart. Place hands lightly behind head (don't pull on neck) or across chest.

**Setup (Sit-up):** Same as crunch, may need feet anchored for full sit-up.

**Execution (Crunch):** Exhale and contract abdominals to lift head, shoulders, and upper back off the floor through spinal flexion. Focus on bringing rib cage towards pelvis while keeping lower back pressed into floor. Pause briefly at peak contraction, then inhale and slowly lower back to starting position under control.

**Execution (Sit-up):** Continue lifting torso off floor until nearly upright. This variation involves significant hip flexor activation and should be controlled throughout the full range of motion.

**Breathing Pattern:** Exhale during upward phase (concentric), inhale during lowering phase (eccentric).

### Progression Pathways
- Increase reps/sets following [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]] principles
- Add external load (weight plate on chest/behind head)
- Decline crunches/sit-ups (increases difficulty)
- Advanced variations: [[EXL-101 Cable Crunch]], [[EXL-88 Hanging Leg Raise  Knee Raise (Captain's Chair or Pull-up Bar)]], V-ups, tuck crunches

### Regression Options
- Reduce range of motion
- Focus on abdominal bracing/hollow body holds from [[X9. Gymnastics  Calisthenics]]
- Transition to [[EXL-84 Plank (Standard & Variations)]]
- Dead bug exercises from [[L4. Core Stability Training]]

## Research Evidence
Current research suggests that while crunches and sit-ups effectively activate the rectus abdominis, repeated spinal flexion under load may contribute to disc degeneration over time (McGill, 2007). Studies indicate that isometric core stability exercises may provide superior functional benefits with lower injury risk (Hibbs et al., 2008).

## Practical Applications
- **Beginner Protocol:** Start with 2-3 sets of 10-15 repetitions
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times per week as part of comprehensive core training
- **Assessment:** Track maximum reps with proper form using [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]] methods
- **Integration:** Best used as part of varied core routine rather than sole abdominal exercise

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Neck pulling:** Yanking on head/neck instead of using abdominal muscles
⚠️ **Momentum use:** Fast, uncontrolled movements that reduce muscle activation
⚠️ **Hip flexor dominance:** Relying on hip flexors rather than abdominals (especially in sit-ups)
⚠️ **Lower back lifting:** Allowing lumbar spine to come off floor during crunches
⚠️ **Breath holding:** Forgetting to breathe throughout the movement

## Contraindications & Safety
Following [[IX-6 Exercise Contraindications.md]] guidelines:

**Avoid with:**
- Acute neck or low back pain
- Disc issues aggravated by spinal flexion  
- Diastasis recti (abdominal separation)

**Considerations:** Modern spine research questions the long-term safety of repeated spinal flexion exercises. [[L4. Core Stability Training]] approaches may be preferable for many individuals seeking functional core strength.
