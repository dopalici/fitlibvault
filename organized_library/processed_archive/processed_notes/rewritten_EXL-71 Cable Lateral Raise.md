### **EXL-71: Cable Lateral Raise**

**ID:** EXL-71-CEX-LATRAISE **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-16)

## Quick Takeaway
Cable lateral raises provide constant tension throughout the movement, making them highly effective for building medial deltoid strength and size with better muscle activation than free weights.

## Key Points
- Use constant cable tension to target side delts more effectively than dumbbells
- Keep elbow slightly bent and lead with elbow, not hand, during the raise
- Control the eccentric (lowering) phase for 2-3 seconds to maximize muscle growth
- Start with lighter weight than dumbbell version due to constant tension curve
- Avoid momentum and shoulder shrugging to maintain deltoid focus

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Mechanics and Setup
The cable lateral raise is an isolation exercise using a low pulley cable machine [[B10. Machine Training]] that targets the medial (side) deltoids through arm abduction. The constant tension provided by the cable system creates a unique resistance profile compared to free weights, maintaining muscle activation throughout the entire range of motion.

Setup involves attaching a D-handle to a low pulley cable and standing sideways to the machine. The working hand (furthest from machine) grasps the handle, allowing the cable to pass across the body. This positioning creates optimal resistance angles for deltoid activation.

### Muscle Activation Patterns
**Primary Movers:** Medial Deltoid serves as the primary mover, responsible for arm abduction from 15-90 degrees of shoulder elevation.

**Secondary Contributors:** Anterior and posterior deltoids provide stability, while the supraspinatus initiates the first 15 degrees of abduction. Upper trapezius activates significantly at the top portion of the range of motion.

**Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]], particularly the contralateral obliques, work to maintain upright posture against the cable's lateral pull. Scapular stabilizers and rotator cuff muscles maintain shoulder joint integrity throughout the movement.

### Execution Technique
Proper form requires maintaining an upright posture with engaged core, slight elbow bend (15-20 degrees), and controlled movement patterns. The arm raises in a wide arc, leading with the elbow rather than the hand, until reaching approximately parallel to the floor. The pause at the top position maximizes peak contraction before the controlled eccentric phase.

## Research Evidence

Research on lateral raise variations demonstrates that cable-based movements provide superior muscle activation compared to free weights due to constant tension characteristics (Schoenfeld et al., 2014). EMG studies show increased medial deltoid activation with cable resistance compared to dumbbell variations throughout the full range of motion.

Studies on shoulder abduction mechanics indicate that the supraspinatus is most active during the first 30 degrees of abduction, while the deltoids become increasingly dominant beyond this point (Escamilla et al., 2009). This supports the exercise's effectiveness for deltoid-focused training.

## Practical Applications

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Beginner: 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps at moderate weight
- Intermediate: 3-4 sets of 10-15 reps with progressive overload [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Advanced: Include tempo variations (3-second eccentrics) or pause reps

**Load Selection:** Start with 20-30% less weight than your dumbbell lateral raise due to the constant tension curve. Most individuals can progress by 2.5-5 lbs every 1-2 weeks with consistent training.

**Frequency:** Can be performed 2-3 times per week as part of shoulder or upper body training, allowing 48-72 hours between sessions for recovery.

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Using excessive momentum or body lean** - This shifts work away from the deltoids and reduces exercise effectiveness

⚠️ **Allowing upper traps to dominate** - Excessive shoulder shrugging reduces medial deltoid activation and can cause neck strain

⚠️ **Bending elbow excessively** - Too much elbow flexion turns the movement into a rowing motion rather than pure abduction

⚠️ **Uncontrolled eccentric phase** - Letting the weight drop quickly misses half the muscle-building stimulus

⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion** - Stopping short of parallel reduces peak contraction and limits strength gains

## Practical Applications

**Progression Strategies:**
- Increase load through weight stack progression [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Advance volume via increased reps/sets [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Add tempo variations with pause at top or slow eccentrics [[B20. Eccentric Training]]
- Progress to leaning cable lateral raise for altered resistance profile

**Regression Options:**
- Reduce load on weight stack
- Transition to dumbbell lateral raise [[EXL-24 Dumbbell Lateral Raise]]
- Use resistance band lateral raise [[B11. Resistance Bands]]
