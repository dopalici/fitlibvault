### C12. Paleo Diet
**ID:** C12-PALEO-DIET
**Created:** 2025-04-26 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The Paleo diet emphasizes whole foods like meats, fish, fruits, and vegetables while excluding grains, legumes, and dairy - it can support health goals but requires careful planning to avoid nutrient gaps and meet athletic performance needs.

## Key Points
- Focus on lean meats, fish, fruits, vegetables, nuts, and seeds while avoiding grains, legumes, dairy, and processed foods
- May improve short-term weight management and blood sugar control due to emphasis on whole foods
- Can be challenging for athletes due to limited carbohydrate-dense foods for performance
- Requires attention to calcium, vitamin D, and fiber intake from non-excluded sources
- Long-term adherence is difficult due to restrictive nature

## Deep Dive

The Paleolithic (Paleo) diet aims to emulate the eating patterns of humans during the Paleolithic era (before agriculture), focusing on whole, unprocessed foods thought to be available to hunter-gatherers.

### Diet Composition

**Foods Typically Included:**
- Lean meats, fish, seafood
- Fruits and non-starchy vegetables
- Nuts, seeds, eggs
- Some versions include sweet potatoes and other tubers

**Foods Typically Excluded:**
- Cereal grains (wheat, rice, corn, oats)
- Legumes (beans, lentils, peas, peanuts)
- Dairy products
- Refined sugars and processed foods [[C30. Food Quality & Health (Whole vs Processed)]]
- Added salt and refined vegetable oils

The diet typically results in higher protein intake and lower-to-moderate carbohydrates compared to standard diets, with fat intake varying based on food choices.

### Proposed Rationale and Benefits

The diet is based on the premise that human genetics are best adapted to pre-agricultural foods. Potential benefits are attributed to high intake of whole foods and exclusion of processed items, including improved weight management, glycemic control, and cardiovascular risk factors, though short-term evidence exists while long-term data remains limited (Manheimer et al., 2015).

### Nutritional Concerns

**Restrictiveness Issues:**
The diet excludes major food groups (dairy, grains, legumes) known to provide valuable nutrients and have associated health benefits, contrasting with less restrictive approaches like the [[C10. Mediterranean Diet]] or well-planned [[C11. Vegetarian & Vegan Diets]].

**Specific Nutrient Considerations:**
- **Calcium & Vitamin D:** Exclusion of dairy requires careful attention to non-dairy sources like leafy greens, bony fish, and potentially supplements [[C6. Micronutrients (Detailed Roles & Considerations)]]
- **Fiber:** While intake can be high from fruits and vegetables, it lacks the diversity provided by whole grains and legumes [[A3. Macronutrients]]
- **Carbohydrates:** Can be challenging to consume sufficient carbohydrates for high-intensity or endurance exercise performance without denser sources

## Research Evidence

**Meta-Analysis on Metabolic Effects:**
Manheimer, E.W., et al. (2015). Paleolithic nutrition for metabolic syndrome: systematic review and meta-analysis. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 102(4), 922-932.
- Shows potential short-term benefits for some metabolic markers
- Limited by data quality and study duration
- Evidence quality: Moderate (systematic review with limitations)

**Critical Perspective:**
Pitt, C.E. (2016). Cutting through the Paleo hype: The evidence for the Paleolithic diet. Australian Family Physician, 45(1), 35-38.
- Questions historical accuracy of dietary assumptions
- Notes lack of long-term safety and efficacy data

**Research on Excluded Foods:**
Otten, J., et al. (2019). Benefits of regular consumption of legumes on metabolic syndrome features. Nutrients, 11(11), 2693. DOI: 10.3390/nu11112693
- Demonstrates health benefits of legumes (excluded in Paleo)
- Suggests potential missed benefits from restriction

## Practical Applications

**Implementation Guidelines:**
- Plan meals around a palm-sized portion of protein, 2-3 cups of vegetables, and appropriate amounts of nuts/seeds
- Include variety in vegetable colors and types to maximize nutrient diversity
- For active individuals, emphasize starchy vegetables like sweet potatoes and increase fruit intake
- Consider timing carbohydrate-rich foods around workouts for performance

**Sample Daily Structure:**
- Breakfast: Eggs with vegetables cooked in olive oil, berries
- Lunch: Large salad with grilled chicken, avocado, nuts
- Dinner: Salmon with roasted vegetables and sweet potato
- Snacks: Nuts, seeds, or fruit as needed

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Inadequate carbohydrate intake for active individuals** - can impair performance and recovery
⚠️ **Neglecting calcium sources** - focusing only on meat while ignoring leafy greens and fish with bones
⚠️ **All-or-nothing mentality** - strict adherence often leads to diet failure rather than sustainable habits
⚠️ **Assuming "Paleo-approved" processed foods are healthy** - many commercial Paleo products are still highly processed
⚠️ **Ignoring individual tolerance** - some people digest grains and legumes well and benefit from including them
