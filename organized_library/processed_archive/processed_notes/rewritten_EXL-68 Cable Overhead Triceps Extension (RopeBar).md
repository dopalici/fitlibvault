### **EXL-68: Cable Overhead Triceps Extension (Rope/Bar)**

**ID:** EXL-68-CEX-OHTRIEXT **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-20)

## Quick Takeaway
The cable overhead triceps extension maximally targets the long head of the triceps through shoulder flexion while providing constant tension throughout the full range of motion.

## Key Points
- Overhead position specifically emphasizes the triceps long head due to its origin at the shoulder blade
- Cable system provides constant tension unlike free weights where tension varies through the range
- Keep upper arms stationary and close to head - moving them reduces triceps activation
- Requires adequate shoulder mobility to safely reach overhead position
- Progress by increasing weight, reps, or adding tempo variations like pauses

## Deep Dive

### Biomechanical Advantages
An isolation exercise using a low pulley cable machine [[B10. Machine Training]] to target the triceps, with the overhead position specifically emphasizing the long head. The triceps brachii has three heads, but the long head originates from the scapula and crosses both the shoulder and elbow joints. When the shoulder is in flexion (overhead position), the long head is placed on stretch, allowing for greater activation and development.

The cable system provides constant tension throughout the entire range of motion, unlike free weights where the resistance curve varies due to gravity and leverage changes. This constant tension can enhance muscle activation and time under tension [[B3. Hypertrophy Training Principles]].

### Exercise Execution
The movement involves controlled elbow extension while maintaining fixed upper arm position. The overhead position requires adequate shoulder flexion mobility [[L2. Mobility and Flexibility Development]] and core stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]], especially when performed standing.

## Research Evidence
Research on triceps activation patterns shows that overhead positions significantly increase long head recruitment compared to arms-down positions (Landin et al., 2018). Cable exercises have been shown to provide more consistent muscle activation throughout the range of motion compared to free weight alternatives due to the constant tension profile.

Studies on triceps anatomy confirm that the long head's dual joint function requires shoulder positioning to optimize activation, with overhead positions creating pre-stretch that enhances force production capacity.

## Practical Applications

**Setup Protocol:**
- Attach rope or bar handle to low pulley cable
- Stand facing away from machine with feet shoulder-width apart
- Grasp handle and bring overhead with elbows bent 90+ degrees
- Upper arms should be close to head, not flared wide

**Execution Guidelines:**
- Keep upper arms completely stationary throughout movement
- Extend elbows fully while exhaling
- Pause 1-2 seconds at full extension
- Lower slowly (2-3 seconds) while inhaling
- Maintain core engagement throughout

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Beginner: 3 sets of 12-15 reps with moderate weight
- Intermediate: 3-4 sets of 8-12 reps with heavier load
- Advanced: Add tempo variations or single-arm progressions

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Elbow flare:** Allowing elbows to move outward reduces triceps activation and increases shoulder stress
⚠️ **Moving upper arms:** Forward/backward movement of upper arms shifts emphasis away from triceps
⚠️ **Incomplete range:** Not fully extending or achieving full stretch limits muscle development
⚠️ **Lower back arch:** Excessive arching when standing indicates poor core stability or too much weight
⚠️ **Momentum use:** Swinging or bouncing reduces muscle tension and increases injury risk

## Target Muscles and Form Cues

**Primary Movers (Agonists):** Triceps Brachii (long head highly emphasized)
**Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Anconeus
**Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]] (especially standing), Scapular stabilizers, Rotator Cuff

**Form Cues:** [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]
- "Pin your upper arms to your head"
- "Only your forearms should move"
- "Squeeze at the top, stretch at the bottom"
- "Keep your core tight throughout"

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications:**
- Acute elbow or shoulder injuries (impingement) [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]]
- Limited shoulder flexion mobility
- Recent shoulder surgery without clearance

**Safety Requirements:**
- Adequate shoulder mobility assessment before performing [[L2. Mobility and Flexibility Development]]
- Controlled weight selection - ego lifting leads to compensation patterns
- Proper warm-up of shoulders and elbows
- Standing version requires greater core stability than seated
