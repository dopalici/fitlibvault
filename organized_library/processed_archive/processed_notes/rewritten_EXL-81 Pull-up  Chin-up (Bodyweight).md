### **EXL-81: Pull-up / Chin-up (Bodyweight)**
Pull-ups and chin-ups are fundamental bodyweight exercises that build exceptional upper body pulling strength, with chin-ups (underhand grip) being slightly easier for beginners due to increased bicep involvement.

### **EXL-81: Pull-up / Chin-up (Bodyweight)**
- Start with assisted variations or negatives if you can't perform full reps yet
- Pull-ups (overhand) emphasize back muscles more; chin-ups (underhand) engage biceps more
- Focus on full range of motion: dead hang start to chin clearing the bar
- Progress by adding reps, then sets, then external weight once you can do 8-12 clean reps
- Core engagement prevents swinging and improves overall strength transfer

### **EXL-81: Pull-up / Chin-up (Bodyweight)**
Pull-ups and chin-ups represent the gold standard of bodyweight upper body pulling exercises within [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]. The primary difference lies in grip: pull-ups use an overhand (pronated) grip while chin-ups use an underhand (supinated) grip. Both exercises require pulling your bodyweight from a dead hang position until your chin clears the bar.

Research demonstrates that pull-ups activate the latissimus dorsi more significantly than chin-ups, while chin-ups show greater biceps brachii activation (Youdas et al., 2010). This makes pull-ups superior for back development and chin-ups more accessible for beginners due to the stronger bicep contribution.

### **EXL-81: Pull-up / Chin-up (Bodyweight)**
**Primary Movers (Agonists):**
- Latissimus Dorsi (primary back muscle, creates the V-taper)
- Teres Major (assists lat function)
- Biceps Brachii (especially prominent in chin-ups)
- Brachialis (deep arm muscle)

**Secondary Movers (Synergists):**
- Rhomboids and Trapezius (middle/lower portions)
- Posterior Deltoid
- Brachioradialis and forearm muscles

**Stabilizers:**
- Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]] prevents excessive swinging
- Erector Spinae maintains spinal alignment
- Scapular stabilizers control shoulder blade movement

### **EXL-81: Pull-up / Chin-up (Bodyweight)**
Following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] principles:

**Setup Phase:**
- Grip the bar with chosen hand position (shoulder-width typical)
- Begin from complete dead hang with full elbow extension
- Engage core slightly to minimize swinging
- Maintain neutral spine alignment

**Execution Phase:**
- Initiate movement by depressing and retracting shoulder blades
- Pull elbows down toward ribs, not just back
- Drive chest toward bar while keeping core tight
- Continue until chin clearly passes over the bar
- Control the descent back to full dead hang

**Breathing Pattern:**
- Exhale during the pulling (concentric) phase
- Inhale during the lowering (eccentric) phase

### **EXL-81: Pull-up / Chin-up (Bodyweight)**
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times per week for strength gains
- **Volume:** 3-5 sets of submaximal reps (leave 1-2 reps in reserve)
- **Progression:** Add 1 rep per week when possible, or increase sets

### **EXL-81: Pull-up / Chin-up (Bodyweight)**

⚠️ **Incomplete Range of Motion** - Not reaching full dead hang or chin not clearing bar
⚠️ **Excessive Kipping/Swinging** - Using momentum instead of strength (unless training specific kipping technique)
⚠️ **Forward Head Posture** - Craning neck forward instead of pulling chest to bar  
⚠️ **Shoulder Shrugging** - Lifting shoulders toward ears instead of keeping them down and back
⚠️ **Progressing Too Quickly** - Jumping to advanced variations before mastering basics
⚠️ **Ignoring Eccentric Phase** - Dropping down quickly instead of controlling the descent
