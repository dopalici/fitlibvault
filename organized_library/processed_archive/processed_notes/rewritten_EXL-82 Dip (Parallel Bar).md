The parallel bar dip is a challenging bodyweight exercise that builds significant upper body pressing strength, primarily targeting triceps, chest, and shoulders while requiring intermediate to advanced strength levels.

### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**
A fundamental bodyweight upper-body pressing exercise [[B7. Bodyweight Training]] performed between parallel bars, involving lowering the body by bending the elbows and pressing back up. The dip primarily targets the triceps brachii, pectoralis major (sternal portion), and anterior deltoid as primary movers, with secondary activation of serratus anterior, pectoralis minor, rhomboids, latissimus dorsi, and levator scapulae. Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]] and rotator cuff muscles serve as stabilizers throughout the movement.

**Difficulty Level:** Intermediate to Advanced - requires significant upper body strength relative to bodyweight.

### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**

**Setup:**
- Grasp parallel bars with neutral grip, hands slightly wider than shoulder-width
- Jump or push up to support bodyweight with arms fully extended (slight elbow bend acceptable)
- Keep shoulders depressed (down away from ears)
- Torso positioning: upright (more triceps focus) or leaned slightly forward (more chest emphasis)
- Cross legs or keep them straight down for stability

**Execution:**
- Inhale and slowly lower body by bending elbows
- Keep elbows tucked relatively close to body (avoid excessive flare)
- Lower until upper arms are approximately parallel to floor or comfortable stretch is felt
- Maintain shoulder position, avoid rolling forward excessively
- Exhale and press forcefully back up by extending elbows to starting position

**Breathing Pattern:** Inhale during descent (eccentric phase), exhale during press (concentric phase).

### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**

**Advancement Options:**
- Increase repetitions and sets [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Add external load using weight belt or vest [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Implement tempo variations (slow eccentric [[B20. Eccentric Training]], pause at bottom)
- Progress to ring dips for increased instability [[X9. Gymnastics Calisthenics]]
- Forward lean variation to emphasize chest development

**Regression Options:**
- Assisted dip machine [[EXL-65 Assisted Pull-upDip Machine]]

- Negative-only dips (controlled lowering phase only)
- Bench dips with feet supported
- Standard push-ups [[EXL-83 Push-up (Standard & Variations)]]
- Cable triceps pushdowns [[EXL-67 Cable Triceps Pushdown (RopeBar)]]

### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**
Research demonstrates that dips produce high levels of muscle activation in the triceps brachii, with EMG studies showing comparable or superior activation to other pressing movements. The exercise generates significant mechanical tension through the full range of motion, making it effective for strength and hypertrophy development. Studies on bodyweight training indicate that compound movements like dips provide excellent stimulus for upper body strength development when progressed appropriately.

### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Beginners: Start with assisted variations, 3 sets of 5-8 reps
- Intermediate: 3-4 sets of 8-15 bodyweight reps
- Advanced: Add external load or progress to ring dips

**Integration Strategies:**
- Pair with pulling movements for balanced upper body development
- Use as primary pressing movement in bodyweight training programs [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]
- Incorporate into strength circuits or as accessory work for bench press

### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**

⚠️ **Excessive shoulder elevation and forward rolling** - compromises joint position and reduces effectiveness

⚠️ **Insufficient range of motion** - stopping too high limits muscle activation and strength development

⚠️ **Uncontrolled descent** - rapid lowering increases injury risk and reduces training stimulus

⚠️ **Excessive elbow flare** - places unnecessary stress on shoulder joint and reduces triceps activation

⚠️ **Using momentum or swinging** - reduces training effect and increases injury risk

### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**

**Exercise Cautions:**
- Acute shoulder injuries (impingement, AC joint problems)
- Elbow or wrist pain conditions
- Poor shoulder mobility [[L2. Mobility and Flexibility Development]] may require preliminary work

**Safety Protocols:**
- Ensure adequate strength foundation before attempting unassisted dips
- Maintain controlled movement throughout full range
- Avoid excessive depth that causes shoulder pain
- Verify equipment stability before use
