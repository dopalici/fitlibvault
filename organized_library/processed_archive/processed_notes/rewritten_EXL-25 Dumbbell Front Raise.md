### **EXL-25: Dumbbell Front Raise**

**ID:** EXL-25-DB-FRONTRAISE **Created:** 2025-04-29 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
An isolation exercise that targets the front deltoid by raising dumbbells forward to shoulder height - ideal for building anterior shoulder strength and definition.

## Key Points
- Raise dumbbells straight forward to shoulder height, controlling both up and down phases
- Keep slight elbow bend throughout - avoid completely straight or significantly bent arms
- Stop at shoulder height to prevent unnecessary trap involvement
- Use strict form without momentum or back lean to maximize deltoid activation
- Start light (5-15 lbs) as front delts fatigue quickly from compound pressing movements

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The dumbbell front raise is a single-joint isolation exercise that primarily involves shoulder flexion. The anterior deltoid acts as the prime mover, lifting the arm forward and upward against gravity. This movement pattern complements compound pressing exercises by providing targeted anterior deltoid development.

### Muscle Activation Patterns
**Primary Movers (Agonists):** Anterior Deltoid handles the primary lifting motion through shoulder flexion.

**Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Pectoralis Major (clavicular head) assists with shoulder flexion, Medial Deltoid provides stability, Serratus Anterior helps with scapular movement, Biceps Brachii (short head) assists minimally with shoulder flexion.

**Stabilizers:** Core musculature maintains torso position [[L4. Core Stability Training]], Trapezius stabilizes the shoulder girdle, Rotator Cuff muscles provide glenohumeral joint stability.

### Technique Execution
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]:

**Setup:** Stand with feet hip-width apart or sit upright with back supported. Hold dumbbells in front of thighs with overhand or neutral grip, maintaining slight elbow bend (15-20 degrees).

**Execution:** Engage core and raise dumbbells directly forward in an arc motion to shoulder height. Maintain constant elbow angle throughout the movement. Control the descent with same path.

**Breathing:** Exhale during the lifting phase (concentric), inhale during lowering (eccentric).

## Research Evidence
Research on isolation exercises shows that front raises effectively target the anterior deltoid with high EMG activation levels. Studies indicate that maintaining strict form without momentum significantly increases muscle activation compared to using body swing (Doma et al., 2013).

Biomechanical analysis demonstrates that stopping at shoulder height (90 degrees of shoulder flexion) optimizes anterior deltoid activation while minimizing upper trap involvement (Campos et al., 2020).

## Practical Applications

### Programming Guidelines
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps with 5-10 lbs
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 10-12 reps with 10-20 lbs
- **Advanced:** 3-4 sets of 8-12 reps with 15-25+ lbs

### Progression Strategies
- Increase load following progressive overload principles [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Increase volume through reps/sets [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Add tempo variations with pauses or slow eccentrics [[B20. Eccentric Training]]
- Progress to alternating front raises, cable variations [[EXL-72 Cable Front Raise]], or barbell front raises

### Regression Options
- Decrease load or use resistance bands [[B11. Resistance Bands]]
- Focus on compound movements like bench press [[EXL-02 Barbell Bench Press (Flat)]], [[EXL-21 Dumbbell Bench Press (Flat)]], or overhead press [[EXL-05 Barbell Overhead Press (Standing)]], [[EXL-23 Dumbbell Shoulder Press (Seated)]]

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Using momentum or body swing** - Reduces deltoid activation and increases injury risk
⚠️ **Excessive back lean** - Compensates for weak anterior delts and stresses lower back
⚠️ **Raising weights above shoulder height** - Shifts emphasis to upper traps instead of front delts
⚠️ **Uncontrolled eccentric phase** - Misses opportunity for additional muscle building stimulus
⚠️ **Starting too heavy** - Front delts are typically pre-fatigued from compound movements

## Contraindications & Safety
**Avoid with:** Acute shoulder impingement, rotator cuff injuries, biceps tendinopathy, or anterior shoulder pain. Can aggravate these conditions if performed improperly.

**Safety considerations:** Use strict form, control both phases of movement, and avoid excessive shrugging or momentum following exercise contraindication guidelines [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications.md]].
