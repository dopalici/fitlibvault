### **EXL-13: Barbell Skullcrusher (Lying Triceps Extension)**

**ID:** EXL-13-BB-SKULLCRUSHER **Created:** 2025-04-29 (Updated: 2025-01-01)

## Quick Takeaway
A highly effective triceps isolation exercise performed lying down, lowering a barbell toward your forehead and extending back up - requires careful control and proper form to avoid injury.

## Key Points
- Primary triceps builder that emphasizes the long head due to shoulder positioning
- Start with lighter weight and EZ-curl bar to reduce wrist strain
- Keep upper arms stationary throughout - only elbows should move
- Control the descent carefully - never let the bar drop toward your face
- Use a spotter for heavier weights and focus on slow, controlled movement

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Overview
The barbell skullcrusher is an isolation exercise targeting the triceps brachii through elbow extension while lying supine on a bench. This exercise is particularly effective at targeting the long head of the triceps due to the shoulder position and arm angle during execution.

### Target Muscle Groups
**Primary Movers (Agonists):** Triceps Brachii (all three heads, with emphasis on the long head due to shoulder positioning)
**Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Anconeus
**Stabilizers:** Deltoids and rotator cuff muscles (isometric hold), core musculature for stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]], [[G-VII-1. Musculoskeletal Anatomy Overview]]

### Proper Form and Technique
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] is crucial for this exercise:

**Setup:**
- Lie flat on bench with feet planted firmly on floor
- Hold barbell (straight or EZ-curl) with overhand grip, narrower than shoulder-width
- Start with bar extended straight above chest/shoulders
- Arms should be perpendicular to floor
- Keep upper arms stationary throughout entire movement

**Execution:**
- Inhale and slowly lower the bar toward forehead by bending only at elbows
- Maintain vertical upper arms or angle slightly back toward head
- Lower until bar approaches forehead or just behind head (based on comfort/mobility)
- Exhale and extend elbows, pressing bar back to starting position
- Focus on triceps contraction while keeping upper arms stable

**Breathing Pattern:**
- Inhale during lowering phase (eccentric)
- Exhale during extension phase (concentric)

## Research Evidence
Research demonstrates that lying triceps extensions effectively activate all three heads of the triceps brachii, with particular emphasis on the long head due to the overhead arm position (Boehrens & Buskies, 1994). EMG studies show high triceps activation compared to other triceps exercises, making it an effective isolation movement for triceps development.

The exercise's effectiveness for hypertrophy training [[B3. Hypertrophy Training Principles]] is supported by its ability to provide constant tension throughout the range of motion and allow for progressive overload [[B1. Progressive Overload]].

## Practical Applications

### Programming Recommendations
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps with light weight
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 6-10 reps with moderate weight
- **Advanced:** 3-5 sets of 4-8 reps or higher volume protocols

### Progression Strategies
- Increase load following progressive overload principles [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Increase reps/sets within training volume guidelines [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Implement tempo variations (slow eccentric [[B20. Eccentric Training]], pause at extension)
- Progress to decline angle for altered resistance curve
- Advance to close-grip bench press [[EXL-02 Barbell Bench Press (Flat)]] or dips [[EXL-82 Dip (Parallel Bar)]]

### Regression Options
- Reduce load or switch to EZ-curl bar for wrist comfort
- Use dumbbells for more natural wrist positioning
- Try overhead dumbbell triceps extension [[EXL-35 Dumbbell Overhead Triceps Extension (Single & Double Arm)]]
- Perform cable triceps pushdown [[EXL-67 Cable Triceps Pushdown (RopeBar)]]
- Use bodyweight triceps extensions with bench or bar

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Moving upper arms excessively, turning the movement into a pullover motion
⚠️ Flaring elbows too wide, reducing triceps activation and increasing joint stress
⚠️ Using momentum or bouncing the weight, which reduces muscle tension
⚠️ Incomplete range of motion, limiting muscle development
⚠️ Lowering the bar too quickly, losing control and increasing injury risk

## Contraindications and Safety
**Conditions to Consider:** Elbow tendinopathy, previous elbow injuries, or acute elbow pain should be evaluated before performing this exercise [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications.md]]

**Safety Protocols:**
- Always use a spotter, especially with heavier weights
- Never allow the bar to drop uncontrolled toward face
- Start with lighter weight to master proper form
- Consider EZ-curl bar to reduce wrist stress
- Maintain strict upper arm position throughout movement
