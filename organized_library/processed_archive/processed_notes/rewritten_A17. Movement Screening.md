### A17. Movement Screening
**ID:** A17-MOVEMENT-SCREENING
**Created:** 2025-04-23 (Updated: 2026-05-06)

## Quick Takeaway
Movement screening uses standardized tests to identify movement limitations and asymmetries that should be addressed before beginning intensive training programs.

## Key Points
- Movement screens assess movement quality, not performance capacity or fitness level
- Pain during any screening movement requires immediate referral for clinical evaluation
- Screens identify mobility, stability, and motor control limitations that guide corrective exercise selection
- Results help determine appropriate starting points for exercise progression and program design
- Predictive validity for injury prevention is moderate and context-dependent

## Deep Dive

Movement screening involves systematic evaluation of fundamental movement patterns using standardized protocols. The primary goal is identifying significant movement dysfunction, asymmetries, and limitations that could interfere with safe and effective training progression.

### Purpose and Applications

Movement screens serve multiple functions in fitness and rehabilitation settings. They establish baseline movement competency before program implementation, helping identify individuals who may need corrective interventions before advancing to complex or loaded movements. Screens also guide exercise selection by revealing specific mobility, stability, or motor control deficits.

The screening process helps distinguish between individuals ready for standard training progressions and those requiring preliminary corrective work. This approach supports injury prevention by addressing movement limitations before they become problematic under training stress.

### Common Screening Tools

**Functional Movement Screen (FMS™)** represents the most widely researched standardized screening protocol. The FMS includes seven fundamental movement tests: Deep Squat, Hurdle Step, In-line Lunge, Shoulder Mobility, Active Straight Leg Raise, Trunk Stability Push-up, and Rotary Stability. Each movement receives a quality score from 0-3, with specific criteria for scoring.

**Overhead Squat Assessment** evaluates dynamic flexibility, core strength, balance, and neuromuscular control through a single compound movement. Common compensations include feet turning outward, excessive forward lean, arms falling forward, and knee valgus collapse.

**Specialized Screens** like the Y-Balance Test assess dynamic balance and stability, while the Landing Error Scoring System (LESS) evaluates jump landing mechanics for sports applications.

### Interpretation Guidelines

Movement screens identify movement quality issues rather than measuring performance capacity or strength. Any pain reported during screening requires immediate clinical referral, as pain indicates potential injury or pathology requiring professional evaluation.

Screen results guide corrective exercise selection by highlighting specific system limitations. For example, restricted overhead reach might indicate thoracic spine mobility issues or shoulder impingement, directing intervention toward appropriate stretching and mobility work.

## Research Evidence

**Bonazza et al. (2017).** Reliability, validity, and injury predictive value of the functional movement screen: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *American Journal of Sports Medicine*, 45(3), 725-732. PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27159297
- Key finding: FMS shows moderate predictive validity for injury (OR = 1.74), with higher scores associated with lower injury risk
- Study type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive review of multiple studies)

**Plisky et al. (2006).** The reliability of an instrumented device for measuring components of the star excursion balance test. *North American Journal of Sports Physical Therapy*, 1(2), 92-99. PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21522216
- Key finding: Y-Balance Test demonstrates excellent test-retest reliability (ICC = 0.85-0.91) and can identify athletes at higher injury risk
- Study type: Reliability and validity study
- Evidence quality: Medium (good methodology, specific population)

**Chorba et al. (2010).** Use of a functional movement screening tool to determine injury risk in female collegiate athletes. *North American Journal of Sports Physical Therapy*, 5(2), 47-54. PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21589661
- Key finding: Female athletes scoring ≤14 on FMS had 3.85 times higher injury rate than those scoring >14
- Study type: Prospective cohort study
- Evidence quality: Medium (specific population, limited to female collegiate athletes)

## Practical Applications

**Initial Assessment Protocol:**
- Conduct screening in controlled environment with proper warm-up
- Score movements objectively using standardized criteria
- Document specific compensations and limitations observed
- Identify pain responses requiring clinical referral

**Program Design Integration:**
- Use screen results to select appropriate exercise starting points
- Address identified limitations through targeted corrective exercises
- Progress from corrective work to functional training to performance training
- Re-screen periodically to monitor improvement and guide progression

**Corrective Exercise Selection:**
- Mobility limitations: Implement specific stretching and soft tissue work
- Stability deficits: Focus on motor control and strengthening exercises
- Asymmetries: Address unilateral training and movement re-education

**Screening Frequency:**
- Initial screening before program start
- Re-assessment every 6-8 weeks during corrective phases
- Annual screening for maintenance clients
- Post-injury return-to-activity screening

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ Using screens as injury prediction tools without considering individual context and sport demands

⚠️ Ignoring pain responses during screening or attempting to "work through" painful movements

⚠️ Failing to address identified limitations before progressing to complex or loaded exercises

⚠️ Over-relying on screen scores without considering movement quality and individual factors

⚠️ Inadequate training in screening administration leading to inconsistent scoring and interpretation
