### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
A strict barbell rowing variation where the bar starts and stops on the floor each rep, eliminating momentum to build explosive pulling power and upper back strength.

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
- Bar must come to complete dead stop on floor between every rep
- Torso stays parallel to floor throughout - no lifting up during the pull
- Focus on explosive acceleration from static start position
- Requires advanced hip hinge mechanics and spinal stability
- Typically performed for lower reps (3-6) due to explosive nature

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
The Pendlay Row eliminates the stretch reflex and momentum used in standard bent-over rows by requiring a complete dead stop on the floor. This forces the muscles to generate maximum power from a static position, similar to a deadlift but targeting the upper back musculature.

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
**Difficulty Level:** Advanced - requires mastery of hip hinge mechanics [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]], excellent spinal stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]], and explosive power development.

The exercise demands maintaining a torso parallel to the floor while explosively pulling maximum loads, making it significantly more technical than standard rowing variations.

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
- **Primary Movers:** Latissimus Dorsi, Trapezius (Middle & Lower), Rhomboids, Teres Major
- **Secondary Movers:** Posterior Deltoid, Biceps Brachii, Brachialis, Brachioradialis, Erector Spinae (strong isometric hold)
- **Stabilizers:** Hamstrings, Gluteus Maximus, Core musculature, Forearm flexors (grip) [[G-VII-1. Musculoskeletal Anatomy Overview]]

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
Research on explosive pulling movements demonstrates improved rate of force development and power transfer to athletic activities. Studies on rowing variations show the dead-stop position eliminates elastic energy contribution, requiring greater concentric force production (Swinton et al., 2012).

Power development research indicates that training with explosive intent from static positions enhances neuromuscular recruitment patterns and force production capabilities across multiple movement patterns.

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
1. Position feet hip-to-shoulder width, mid-foot under bar
2. Hip hinge to torso parallel position [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]]
3. Grip bar overhand, wider than shoulders
4. Maintain neutral spine throughout

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
- **Pull Phase:** Explosive acceleration toward lower chest/upper abdomen
- **Reset Phase:** Controlled descent to complete floor contact
- **Tempo:** 1-2 second pause on floor, explosive concentric
- **Breathing:** Brace before pull, reset between reps if needed

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
- **Rep Ranges:** 3-6 reps for power, 6-8 for strength-endurance
- **Sets:** 3-5 sets depending on training phase
- **Frequency:** 1-2x per week due to high neuromuscular demand
- **Load:** 80-90% of standard bent-over row weight initially

### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**
⚠️ **Torso Rising:** Lifting chest/torso during pull defeats the purpose - maintain constant angle
⚠️ **Bouncing:** Using floor contact for momentum instead of complete dead stop
⚠️ **Arm Dominant Pulling:** Leading with biceps instead of initiating with back muscles
⚠️ **Spinal Flexion:** Rounding lower back due to inadequate hip hinge mobility or core stability
⚠️ **Inadequate Pause:** Rushing the floor contact without allowing muscles to relax
