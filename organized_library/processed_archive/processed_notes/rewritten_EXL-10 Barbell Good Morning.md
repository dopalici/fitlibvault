### **EXL-10: Barbell Good Morning**

**ID:** EXL-10-BB-GOODMORNING **Created:** 2025-04-29 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The barbell good morning is an advanced hip hinge exercise that primarily strengthens the posterior chain (spinal erectors, glutes, hamstrings) but requires excellent technique and should only be performed by those who have mastered safer hip hinge movements first.

## Key Points
- Hip hinge movement with barbell positioned on upper back, similar to squat bar placement
- Primarily targets spinal erectors (isometric/eccentric), glutes, and hamstrings
- Advanced exercise requiring excellent hip hinge mechanics and hamstring flexibility
- High injury risk if performed with poor form, especially spinal flexion under load
- Safer alternatives like Romanian deadlifts should be mastered first

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The barbell good morning is a hip-dominant movement pattern that involves hinging at the hips while maintaining spinal neutrality. The barbell placement on the upper back creates a longer lever arm compared to exercises like Romanian deadlifts, increasing the challenge to the posterior chain muscles and requiring greater core stability.

### Primary Muscle Activation
**Primary Movers:** The spinal erectors work isometrically and eccentrically to maintain spinal position against the forward-bending moment created by the barbell placement. The hamstrings and gluteus maximus control the hip hinge movement and provide the power for hip extension during the return phase.

**Secondary and Stabilizing Muscles:** The adductor magnus assists with hip extension, while the entire core musculature, upper back (trapezius, rhomboids), and latissimus dorsi work to stabilize the barbell and maintain proper positioning [[G-VII-1. Musculoskeletal Anatomy Overview]].

### Technical Execution
**Setup:** Position the barbell across the upper back using either high bar or low bar placement similar to the [[EXL-01 Barbell Back Squat (High Bar & Low Bar)]]. Stand with feet hip-to-shoulder width apart, maintaining a slight bend in the knees throughout the movement.

**Movement Pattern:** Initiate by pushing the hips backward while maintaining perfect spinal neutrality [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]]. Lower the torso toward parallel with the floor, stopping when hamstring flexibility limits further descent while maintaining neutral spine. The movement should feel similar to an [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]] but with the load positioned higher on the body.

**Return Phase:** Drive the hips forward forcefully to return to the starting position, focusing on glute activation and maintaining core stability throughout [[L4. Core Stability Training]].

## Practical Applications

### Progression Pathway
- Master hip hinge pattern with bodyweight movements
- Progress to [[EXL-86 Back Extension  Hyperextension (Bench)]]
- Advance to [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]]
- Only then consider barbell good mornings with empty barbell
- Extremely conservative load progression following [[B1. Progressive Overload]] principles

### Programming Guidelines
- **Frequency:** 1-2 times per week maximum due to high recovery demands
- **Volume:** Start with 2-3 sets of 8-12 repetitions [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- **Load Progression:** Increase weight by 2.5-5 lbs maximum per week, only after perfect form is maintained
- **Tempo Variations:** Slow eccentric phases (3-4 seconds) can enhance posterior chain strength [[B20. Eccentric Training]]

### Assessment Criteria
- Maintain neutral spine throughout entire range of motion
- Smooth hip hinge initiation without knee bend compensation
- Adequate hamstring flexibility to reach parallel position
- Core stability under load [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]]

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Spinal flexion under load** - The most dangerous error, potentially causing disc injury
⚠️ **Initiating movement with back bend** - Should start with hip hinge, not spinal movement
⚠️ **Excessive knee bend** - Turns the movement into a squat variation rather than hip hinge
⚠️ **Inadequate warm-up** - Posterior chain requires thorough preparation before loading
⚠️ **Progressing load too quickly** - Conservative progression is essential for safety
