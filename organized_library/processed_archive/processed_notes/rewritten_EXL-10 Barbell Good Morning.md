### **EXL-10: Barbell Good Morning**

**ID:** EXL-10-BB-GOODMORNING **Created:** 2025-04-29 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The barbell good morning is an advanced hip hinge exercise that strengthens the posterior chain but carries high injury risk if performed incorrectly - master RDLs first and prioritize perfect spinal alignment over heavy weight.

## Key Points
- Advanced exercise requiring excellent hip hinge mechanics and hamstring flexibility
- Primarily targets spinal erectors, glutes, and hamstrings through hip hinge movement
- High injury potential - maintain neutral spine throughout entire range of motion
- Start with empty barbell and progress weight very conservatively
- Romanian deadlifts are a safer alternative that target similar muscles

## Deep Dive

### Movement Pattern and Execution
The barbell good morning is a hip hinge exercise performed with a barbell positioned across the upper back, similar to a back squat setup. The movement involves hinging at the hips to lower the torso towards parallel while maintaining spinal neutrality and slightly bent knees.

### Primary Muscle Targets
- **Primary Movers:** Erector Spinae (strong isometric & eccentric focus), Hamstrings, Gluteus Maximus
- **Secondary Movers:** Adductor Magnus  
- **Stabilizers:** Core musculature, Upper Back (Traps, Rhomboids), Latissimus Dorsi [[G-VII-1. Musculoskeletal Anatomy Overview]]

### Proper Form and Technique
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]:

**Setup:** Position barbell across upper back similar to squat positioning [[EXL-01 Barbell Back Squat (High Bar & Low Bar)]]. Stand with feet hip-to-shoulder width apart, knees slightly bent. Maintain neutral spine, chest up, shoulders pulled back. Brace core tightly.

**Execution:** Initiate by pushing hips backward through hip hinge pattern [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]], lowering torso towards parallel. Keep back perfectly neutral throughout movement. Maintain slight knee bend similar to RDL technique. Lower only as far as flexibility allows while maintaining spinal alignment. Drive hips forward forcefully to return to upright position.

**Breathing:** Inhale during lowering phase (eccentric), exhale during hip extension (concentric).

## Research Evidence
While specific research on barbell good mornings is limited, studies on hip hinge patterns and posterior chain exercises support the movement's effectiveness:

- Hip hinge exercises effectively target the posterior chain while promoting functional movement patterns (McGill, 2016)
- Exercises placing load higher on the kinetic chain increase spinal compression forces and require greater stabilization (Cholewicki & McGill, 1996)
- Proper hip hinge mechanics are essential for injury prevention during loaded spinal flexion-extension movements (Stuart & McGill, 1995)

## Practical Applications

### Progression Strategies
- Increase load through progressive overload [[B1. Progressive Overload]] - progress very cautiously
- Increase volume with reps/sets [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Add tempo variations like slow eccentric phases [[B20. Eccentric Training]] or pauses
- Try seated good mornings for different loading angle

### Programming Recommendations
- Start with empty barbell (45 lbs) for multiple sessions
- Focus on 6-10 reps to maintain form quality
- Progress load by 5-10 lbs maximum per week
- Perform 2-3 sets maximum until movement is mastered

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Spinal rounding** - Most dangerous error; can cause serious back injury under load
⚠️ **Excessive knee bending** - Turns movement into squat rather than hip hinge
⚠️ **Initiating with spinal flexion** - Should start with hip movement, not back bend
⚠️ **Losing core brace** - Compromises spinal stability throughout movement
⚠️ **Progressing weight too quickly** - High injury risk requires conservative progression

## Contraindications and Safety
**Avoid if you have:** Low back pain/injury, disc issues, limited hamstring flexibility, poor hip hinge mechanics [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications.md]]

**Safety priorities:** Master neutral spine position, start extremely light, ensure adequate hamstring flexibility [[L2. Mobility and Flexibility Development]], maintain core stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]]

### Regression Options
- Romanian deadlifts [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]] - safer starting point with load held lower
- Back extensions [[EXL-86 Back Extension  Hyperextension (Bench)]] - bodyweight hip hinge pattern
- Kettlebell swings/deadlifts [[B9. Kettlebell Training]] - dynamic hip hinge development
- Improve movement quality through hip hinge practice [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]]
