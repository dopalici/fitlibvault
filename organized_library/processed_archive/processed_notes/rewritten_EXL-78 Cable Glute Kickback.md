### **EXL-78: Cable Glute Kickback**

**ID:** EXL-78-CEX-GLUTEKICKBACK **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
A beginner-friendly cable machine exercise that isolates the glutes through controlled hip extension, perfect for building glute strength and mind-muscle connection.

## Key Points
- Stand facing cable machine with ankle strap, kick leg straight back while squeezing glute
- Keep torso upright and avoid arching your lower back - movement comes from the hip only
- Use slow, controlled motion with 2-second pause at top for maximum glute activation
- Start with light weight (10-20 lbs) focusing on form before adding load
- Progress by increasing weight, reps, or adding tempo variations once form is mastered

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Mechanics
The cable glute kickback is an isolation exercise using a low pulley cable machine [[B10. Machine Training]] with an ankle strap attachment to target the gluteus maximus through hip extension. The constant tension from the cable provides consistent resistance throughout the range of motion, making it superior to bodyweight variations for progressive overload [[B1. Progressive Overload]].

**Difficulty Level:** Beginner to Intermediate

### Target Muscles
- **Primary Movers (Agonists):** Gluteus Maximus
- **Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Hamstrings  
- **Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]], Erector Spinae, Gluteus Medius/Minimus (of stance leg), Quadriceps (of stance leg)

### Proper Form and Technique
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]:

**Setup:**
- Attach ankle strap to low pulley cable
- Secure cuff around working ankle
- Stand facing machine, holding lightly for balance
- Step back to create cable tension
- Maintain slight bend in support knee, engage core, neutral spine

**Execution:**
- Exhale and extend cuffed leg straight back (hip extension)
- Focus on squeezing glute of working leg
- Keep knee relatively straight or slightly bent
- Avoid excessive lower back arching - movement originates from hip
- Pause briefly at peak contraction
- Inhale and slowly return to start position under control
- Complete all reps on one side before switching

**Breathing Pattern:** Exhale during kickback (concentric phase), inhale during return (eccentric phase)

## Research Evidence

Research on hip extension exercises demonstrates that isolated glute exercises like kickbacks can effectively activate the gluteus maximus while minimizing compensatory movement patterns (Contreras et al., 2013). Studies show that cable-based exercises provide consistent resistance throughout the range of motion, leading to greater muscle activation compared to bodyweight variations (Andersen et al., 2006).

The eccentric component of cable exercises [[B20. Eccentric Training]] contributes significantly to strength gains and muscle development, with the cable providing controlled resistance during the lowering phase (Roig et al., 2009).

## Practical Applications

**Programming Recommendations:**
- **Beginners:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps per leg, 2-3x per week
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 10-15 reps per leg with increased load
- **Volume considerations:** Follow training volume guidelines [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]

**Weight Selection:**
- Start with 10-20 lbs to master form
- Progress by 5-10 lbs when you can complete all sets with perfect form
- Focus on glute contraction over heavy weight

**Tempo Variations:**
- 2-second pause at peak contraction
- 3-second slow eccentric lowering phase
- 1-second explosive concentric with controlled return

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Excessive lower back arching** - Keep core engaged and movement isolated to the hip
⚠️ **Using momentum or swinging the leg** - Control the movement throughout entire range of motion  
⚠️ **Insufficient glute contraction** - Focus on actively squeezing the glute rather than just moving the leg
⚠️ **Bending knee too much** - This turns the exercise into a hamstring curl instead of hip extension
⚠️ **Starting with too much weight** - Master the movement pattern before adding significant load

## Progression and Regression Options

**Progression Paths:**
- Increase load following progressive overload principles [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Increase reps/sets per training volume guidelines [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Add tempo variations with pauses and slow eccentrics [[B20. Eccentric Training]]
- Perform standing without hand support (increases stability demand)
- Progress to compound hip extension exercises like barbell hip thrusts [[EXL-07 Barbell Hip Thrust]] or Romanian deadlifts [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]]

**Regression Paths:**
- Decrease load
- Bodyweight glute kickbacks [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]
- Quadruped hip extensions (Bird Dog) for core stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]]
- Glute bridges for basic hip extension [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]
- Resistance band glute kickbacks [[B11. Resistance Bands]]

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications:** Acute low back or hip injuries. This exercise requires good core control to prevent lumbar hyperextension - see exercise contraindications [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]].

**Safety Guidelines:**
- Maintain neutral spine throughout movement
- Control the weight - never use momentum
- Start with light resistance to establish proper movement pattern
- Stop if you feel strain in lower back

## Assessment and Metrics

Track progress using established fitness measurement protocols [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]]:

- **Qualitative:** Form assessment focusing on hip extension isolation, minimal back arch, and movement control
- **Progression markers:** Ability to increase weight while maintaining perfect form
