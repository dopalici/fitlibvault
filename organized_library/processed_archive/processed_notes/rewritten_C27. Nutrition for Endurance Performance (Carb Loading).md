### C27. Nutrition for Endurance Athletes
**ID:** C27-NUTRITION-ENDURANCE
**Created:** 2025-04-26 (Updated: 2025-01-20)

## Quick Takeaway
Endurance athletes need strategic carbohydrate periodization (5-12g/kg/day based on training load), consistent hydration practices, and practiced fueling strategies to maintain performance during prolonged exercise lasting over 90 minutes.

## Key Points
- Match daily carb intake to training load: 5-7g/kg for moderate training, up to 12g/kg for extreme volume (>4-5h/day)
- Practice race-day fueling: 30-60g carbs/hour for events up to 2.5 hours, up to 90g/hour for ultra-endurance
- Prevent dehydration beyond 2% body weight loss through individualized hydration plans
- Time recovery nutrition within 4 hours: 1.0-1.2g carbs/kg/hour plus 20-40g protein when rapid recovery is needed
- Monitor key micronutrients: iron (especially females), vitamin D, and calcium status

## Deep Dive

Nutrition for endurance athletes focuses on optimizing fuel availability (primarily carbohydrate), maintaining hydration, preventing gastrointestinal distress, and supporting recovery to sustain performance over prolonged durations during [[E. Cardiovascular  Endurance Training]].

### Carbohydrate Periodization Strategy

**Daily Carbohydrate Requirements:** Crucial for moderate-to-high intensity endurance exercise lasting >90 minutes. Daily intake must match training load to optimize glycogen stores and support performance:
- Moderate duration/low intensity: 5-7 g/kg/day
- Moderate-heavy endurance (1-3h/day): 6-10 g/kg/day  
- Extreme exercise (>4-5h/day): 8-12 g/kg/day

This periodized approach ensures adequate muscle glycogen stores while avoiding unnecessary overconsumption during lighter training phases. See comprehensive macronutrient guidelines in [[A3. Macronutrients]].

### Pre-Competition Strategies

**Carbohydrate Loading (Supercompensation):** Strategy to maximize muscle glycogen stores before major competitions lasting >90 minutes. Effective protocols involve consuming **8-12 g CHO/kg/day** for 1-3 days alongside an exercise taper. This supercompensation can increase muscle glycogen stores by 20-40% above normal levels, significantly delaying fatigue onset.

**Pre-Event Meal:** Consume 1-4 g CHO/kg 1-4 hours before the event. Focus on familiar, easily digestible foods low in fat and fiber to minimize gastrointestinal distress. Timing depends on individual tolerance and event start time [[C3. Nutrient Timing]].

### During-Exercise Fueling

**Intra-Event Fueling:** Essential for events >60-90 minutes:
- Target **30-60 g CHO per hour** for events up to ~2.5 hours
- For longer ultra-endurance events, intake can increase up to **90 g CHO per hour**
- Utilize multiple transportable carbohydrates (e.g., glucose/maltodextrin + fructose) for optimal absorption and oxidation rates

Sources include sports drinks, gels, chews, bars, or suitable whole foods. The key principle: practice your exact fueling strategy during training sessions to avoid race-day gastrointestinal issues.

### Hydration and Electrolyte Management

Critical for both performance and health. Develop an individualized hydration plan based on sweat rate testing, event duration, and environmental conditions to prevent >2% body weight loss [[C4. Hydration]]. Replace sodium and other key electrolytes lost in sweat, especially during prolonged events (>2 hours) or in hot conditions [[C5. Electrolytes]].

### Supporting Nutrients

**Protein Requirements:** Supports muscle repair and adaptation. Typically **1.2-1.8 g/kg/day** recommended for endurance athletes, with higher intakes during periods of increased training load or caloric restriction.

**Recovery Nutrition:** Timely intake of carbohydrates (1.0-1.2 g/kg/hour for first ~4h if rapid recovery needed) and protein (0.3-0.5 g/kg or 20-40g) becomes critical with frequent training sessions or multi-day competitions [[C29. Nutrition for Recovery]].

### Energy Availability Considerations

Recent research highlights the importance of adequate energy availability (energy intake minus exercise energy expenditure) for optimal sleep quality and recovery in elite athletes. Low energy availability can impair sleep quality, which subsequently affects performance and adaptation to training.

**Thomas, D.T., et al. (2016).** Position of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians of Canada, and the American College of Sports Medicine: Nutrition and Athletic Performance. *Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics*, 116(3), 501-528. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006
- Key finding: Established evidence-based carbohydrate intake recommendations ranging from 5-12g/kg/day based on training load
- Study type: Systematic review/Position statement
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive review of multiple RCTs and meta-analyses)

**Burke, L.M., et al. (2011).** Carbohydrates and fat for training and recovery. *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 29 Suppl 1, S17-27. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2011.585473
- Key finding: Carbohydrate loading protocols can increase muscle glycogen stores by 20-40% when combined with exercise taper
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (synthesis of controlled trials)

**Jeukendrup, A.E. (2014).** A step towards personalized sports nutrition: carbohydrate intake during exercise. *Sports Medicine*, 44 Suppl 1, S25-33. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-014-0148-z
- Key finding: Multiple transportable carbohydrates can increase oxidation rates up to 90g/hour in ultra-endurance events
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (review of controlled feeding studies)

**Logue, D.M., et al. (2026).** Association between energy availability and sleep quality in elite female and male swimmers: a brief report. *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, 23(1), 2665547. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/15502783.2026.2665547
- Key finding: Low energy availability was significantly associated with poor sleep quality in elite swimmers
- Study type: Observational
- Evidence quality: Medium (cross-sectional design with elite athlete population)

## Practical Applications

**Training Phase Periodization:**
- Base building (moderate intensity): 5-7g carbs/kg/day
- Peak training blocks: 8-10g carbs/kg/day  
- Competition preparation: 8-12g carbs/kg/day for 1-3 days pre-event

**Race Day Timeline Example (Marathon):**
- 3 hours before: Large meal with 2-3g carbs/kg
- 1 hour before: Small snack with 1g carbs/kg
- During race: 30-60g carbs/hour starting at 45-60 minutes
- Post-race: 1.2g carbs/kg within first hour

**Sweat Rate Testing Protocol:**
1. Weigh yourself nude pre-exercise
2. Train for 1 hour at race pace/conditions
3. Weigh yourself nude post-exercise
4. Calculate: (Pre-weight - Post-weight + fluid consumed) = sweat rate/hour

**Energy Availability Monitoring:**
- Track total daily energy intake
- Estimate exercise energy expenditure
- Aim for energy availability >30 kcal/kg fat-free mass/day
- Monitor sleep quality as an indicator of adequate energy availability

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Under-fueling during training:** Not practicing race-day nutrition strategies leads to GI distress on competition day

⚠️ **Static carb intake:** Using the same daily carbohydrate intake regardless of training load leads to either under-fueling or unnecessary overconsumption

⚠️ **Ignoring individual tolerance:** Copying other athletes' strategies without testing personal GI tolerance and preferences

⚠️ **Dehydration misconceptions:** Either over-drinking (hyponatremia risk) or under-drinking based on thirst alone rather than planned intake

⚠️ **Micronutrient neglect:** Focusing only on macronutrients while ignoring iron status, vitamin D, and other key micronutrients

⚠️ **Low energy availability:** Chronically under-eating relative to training demands, which can impair sleep quality and recovery
