### **EXL-73: Cable Face Pull**

**ID:** EXL-73-CEX-FACEPULL
**Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-07)

## Quick Takeaway
A high-pulley cable exercise that targets rear delts and rotator cuff muscles while promoting shoulder health and correcting forward head posture - essential for balanced shoulder development.

## Key Points
- Pull rope toward face height (not chest) with strong external shoulder rotation
- Focus on squeezing shoulder blades together and flaring elbows outward
- Start light and prioritize movement quality over heavy weight
- Excellent corrective exercise for desk workers and bench press enthusiasts
- Can be performed daily as part of warm-up or shoulder health routine

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable face pull is a horizontal pulling exercise using a high pulley cable machine [[B10. Machine Training]] with rope attachment. The movement involves pulling the rope toward the face while simultaneously externally rotating the shoulders and retracting the scapulae.

**Difficulty Level:** Beginner to Intermediate (requires coordination and movement control)

### Primary Muscle Actions
- **Primary Movers:** Posterior deltoid, external rotators (infraspinatus, teres minor), middle and lower trapezius, rhomboids
- **Secondary Movers:** Middle deltoid, biceps brachii (minor assistance)
- **Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]], erector spinae, rotator cuff complex

### Proper Form Technique
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]:

**Setup:**
- Attach rope handle to high pulley cable
- Grasp rope ends with neutral or overhand grip
- Step back until arms are extended with slight cable tension
- Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, slight knee bend, engaged core

**Execution:**
- Initiate by retracting shoulder blades
- Pull rope toward face level (nose/forehead height)
- Externally rotate shoulders so knuckles point toward ceiling
- Finish with elbows flared outward and slightly down ("double bicep pose")
- Squeeze upper back and rear delts
- Control return to starting position

**Breathing Pattern:** Exhale during pull (concentric), inhale during return (eccentric)

### Progression Strategies
Following progressive overload principles [[B1. Progressive Overload]] and volume considerations [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]:

- **Load Progression:** Gradually increase weight stack resistance
- **Volume Progression:** Add sets or repetitions systematically
- **Tempo Variations:** Pause at peak contraction, slow eccentric phase [[B20. Eccentric Training]]
- **Stance Variations:** Standing, kneeling, or half-kneeling positions

### Regression Options
- Reduce cable weight or resistance
- Resistance band face pulls or pull-aparts [[B11. Resistance Bands]]
- Dumbbell bent-over rear delt raises [[EXL-26 Dumbbell Bent-Over Rear Delt Raise]]
- Machine rear delt fly [[EXL-55 Rear Delt Fly  Reverse Pec Deck (Machine)]]
- Basic scapular retraction exercises

## Research Evidence
Research supporting posterior deltoid and rotator cuff strengthening exercises like face pulls shows significant benefits for shoulder health and postural correction. Studies indicate that horizontal pulling exercises effectively activate the posterior deltoid and external rotators while improving scapular stability (Escamilla et al., 2009). The external rotation component specifically targets the infraspinatus and teres minor, crucial for rotator cuff health and shoulder impingement prevention.

## Practical Applications
- **For Hypertrophy:** 3-4 sets of 12-20 reps following hypertrophy training principles [[B3. Hypertrophy Training Principles]]
- **For Shoulder Health:** 2-3 sets of 15-25 reps, can be performed daily
- **As Warm-up:** 1-2 sets of 10-15 reps with light weight
- **Postural Correction:** Include in upper body sessions 2-3 times per week
- **Programming:** Excellent superset partner with pressing exercises

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Pulling too low:** Aiming for chest instead of face level reduces rear delt activation
⚠️ **Insufficient external rotation:** Keeping elbows low misses the rotator cuff training benefit
⚠️ **Excessive shoulder shrugging:** Creates neck tension and reduces target muscle activation
⚠️ **Using momentum:** Leaning back or swinging reduces exercise effectiveness
⚠️ **Forward head posture:** Jutting head forward instead of maintaining neutral spine
