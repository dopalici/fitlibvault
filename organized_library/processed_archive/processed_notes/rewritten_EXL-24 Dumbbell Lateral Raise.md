### **EXL-24: Dumbbell Lateral Raise**

**ID:** EXL-24-DB-LATRAISE **Created:** 2025-04-29 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The dumbbell lateral raise is an isolation exercise that primarily targets the medial deltoid through shoulder abduction, building shoulder width and roundness when performed with controlled form.

## Key Points
- Targets the medial (side) deltoid head as the primary mover, with assistance from anterior deltoid and supraspinatus
- Performed by raising dumbbells out to the sides until arms reach approximately parallel to the floor
- Requires controlled movement with slight elbow bend throughout the range of motion
- Common errors include using momentum, excessive shoulder shrugging, and raising weights too far forward
- Can be modified by adjusting weight, tempo, or performing in the scapular plane for comfort

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The dumbbell lateral raise involves shoulder abduction in the frontal plane, with the medial deltoid serving as the primary mover. The supraspinatus initiates the movement during the first 15-30 degrees of abduction, after which the medial deltoid takes over as the dominant force producer. The exercise creates peak tension on the deltoids when the arms are parallel to the floor.

### Target Muscle Groups
**Primary Movers (Agonists):** Medial Deltoid
**Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Anterior Deltoid, Posterior Deltoid, Supraspinatus (initiates abduction), Trapezius (Upper - assists in final ROM/upward rotation)
**Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]], Rotator Cuff, Muscles controlling scapula

### Proper Form and Technique
Following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] principles:

**Setup:** Stand or sit upright with core engaged, holding dumbbells at sides with palms facing body (neutral grip) or slightly towards back. Maintain slight bend in elbows throughout movement. Keep chest up and shoulders down.

**Execution:** Raise dumbbells out to the sides, leading with the elbows, until arms are approximately parallel to the floor. Maintain slight elbow bend and imagine pushing hands outwards, not just upwards. Control the descent slowly back to starting position.

**Breathing:** Exhale during the raise (concentric), inhale during the lowering (eccentric).

### Progression and Regression Options
**Progression Paths:**
- Increase Load (Dumbbell Weight) following [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Increase Reps/Sets per [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Tempo Variations (pauses at top, slow eccentric [[B20. Eccentric Training]])
- Cable Lateral Raises [[EXL-71 Cable Lateral Raise]]
- Machine Lateral Raises [[EXL-53 Lateral Raise (Machine)]]
- Leaning Lateral Raises (alters resistance curve)

**Regression Paths:**
- Decrease Load
- Resistance Band Lateral Raises [[B11. Resistance Bands]]
- Scapular plane raises (30 degrees forward, often more comfortable)
- Focus on form with lighter weights

## Practical Applications

**Training Implementation:**
- Perform 2-4 sets of 8-15 repetitions for hypertrophy following [[B3. Hypertrophy Training Principles]]
- Use 2-3 second eccentric (lowering) phase for enhanced muscle damage and growth
- Include 1-2 times per week as part of shoulder training routine
- Start with 5-10 lb dumbbells for beginners, progressing based on form quality

**Programming Considerations:**
- Place after compound pressing movements when shoulders are pre-fatigued
- Combine with other deltoid isolation exercises for complete development
- Monitor for shoulder impingement symptoms and adjust plane of movement if needed

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Using excessive momentum or body swing** - negates isolation benefits and increases injury risk
⚠️ **Shrugging shoulders upward** - shifts emphasis to upper trapezius instead of deltoids
⚠️ **Raising dumbbells too far forward** - engages anterior deltoid more than medial head
⚠️ **Insufficient range of motion** - limits muscle activation and development potential
⚠️ **Locking elbows completely** - reduces joint safety and muscle tension

## Contraindications & Safety

**Conditions requiring caution:**
- Acute shoulder injuries (impingement, rotator cuff issues)
- May aggravate impingement if performed with internal rotation at top
- Consider scapular plane variation (30 degrees forward) for those with shoulder discomfort

**Safety guidelines:**
- Use appropriate weight allowing strict form throughout all repetitions
- Control eccentric phase to prevent momentum buildup
- Stop if sharp pain occurs in shoulder joint
- Warm up shoulders adequately before performing
