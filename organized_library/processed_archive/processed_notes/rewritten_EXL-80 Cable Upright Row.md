### **EXL-80: Cable Upright Row**

**ID:** EXL-80-CEX-UPRIGHTROW **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
A cable exercise targeting the side deltoids and upper traps that requires careful technique to avoid shoulder impingement - consider wider grip or alternative exercises if experiencing shoulder discomfort.

## Key Points
- Pull the cable bar vertically toward your chin with elbows leading higher than wrists
- Use a wider grip (6-12 inches apart) and avoid pulling above chest height to reduce impingement risk
- Focus on controlled movement - exhale up, inhale down, no momentum or body swing
- Stop immediately if you feel shoulder pain and choose alternative exercises
- Progress by increasing weight, reps, or adding tempo variations like pauses at the top

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Mechanics and Setup
This intermediate-level exercise uses a low pulley cable machine [[B10. Machine Training]] with a straight bar or EZ-bar attachment. The movement involves vertical pulling toward the chin, primarily targeting the medial deltoids and upper trapezius while engaging secondary muscles including the anterior deltoids, biceps, and core stabilizers [[L4. Core Stability Training]].

### Proper Execution Technique
Following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] principles:
- **Setup**: Attach bar to low pulley, stand close to machine with overhand grip 6-12 inches apart
- **Movement**: Pull bar vertically upward leading with elbows, keeping bar close to body
- **Top Position**: Elbows should be higher than wrists at peak contraction
- **Control**: Slowly lower under control, maintaining tension throughout range of motion
- **Breathing**: Exhale during upward pull (concentric), inhale during lowering (eccentric)

### Target Muscle Groups
- **Primary Movers**: Medial Deltoid, Upper Trapezius
- **Secondary Movers**: Anterior Deltoid, Supraspinatus, Biceps Brachii, Brachialis, Brachioradialis, Levator Scapulae
- **Stabilizers**: Core musculature, Rotator Cuff complex

### Shoulder Impingement Concerns
The combination of shoulder abduction and internal rotation inherent in upright rows can compress soft tissues in the subacromial space, potentially leading to impingement syndrome. Research indicates that narrower grips and higher pulling angles increase impingement risk (Boettcher et al., 2008).

## Research Evidence
Boettcher, C. E., Ginn, K. A., & Cathers, I. (2008). Standard maximum isometric voluntary contraction tests for normalizing shoulder muscle EMG. Journal of Orthopaedic Research, 26(12), 1591-1597.

Evidence suggests that upright rows can effectively activate the deltoids and upper trapezius, but biomechanical analysis shows increased impingement risk with narrow grips and high pulling angles. The cable variation allows for smoother resistance curve compared to free weights.

## Practical Applications

### Progression Strategies
- **Load Progression**: Increase weight stack following [[B1. Progressive Overload]] principles
- **Volume Progression**: Increase reps/sets per [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]] guidelines
- **Tempo Variations**: Add pauses at top position or slow [[B20. Eccentric Training]] phases
- **Equipment Progression**: Advance to dumbbell or barbell variations for increased load capacity

### Regression Options
- Decrease load and focus on form mastery
- Use wider grip to reduce internal rotation
- Limit pulling height to chest level instead of chin height
- Substitute with [[EXL-24 Dumbbell Lateral Raise]], [[EXL-53 Lateral Raise (Machine)]], or [[EXL-71 Cable Lateral Raise]]
- Use [[B11. Resistance Bands]] for lighter resistance
- Focus on isolated movements like [[EXL-16 Barbell Shrug]] or [[EXL-41 Dumbbell Shrug]]

## Practical Applications
- Incorporate 2-3 sets of 8-15 reps depending on training goals
- Use as accessory movement after compound pressing exercises
- Ideal for shoulder width development and upper trap strengthening
- Can be performed 2-3 times per week with adequate recovery

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Pulling primarily with arms/biceps** instead of leading with shoulders and elbows
⚠️ **Allowing wrists to rise higher than elbows** at top position
⚠️ **Using excessive momentum or body swing** instead of controlled movement
⚠️ **Pulling bar away from body** rather than keeping it close throughout range of motion
⚠️ **Ignoring shoulder pain** and continuing exercise despite discomfort
