### **Q2. Core Nutrition Principles Overview**
Six evidence-based nutrition principles form the foundation of effective dietary strategies: energy balance, adequate macronutrients, sufficient micronutrients, proper hydration, quality food choices, and sustainable practices.

### **Q2. Core Nutrition Principles Overview**
- Energy balance (calories in vs. out) is the primary driver of weight change - create deficits, surpluses, or maintenance based on your goals
- Meet minimum protein needs (0.8-1.6g/kg bodyweight), fuel activity with carbs, and include healthy fats for hormone production
- Prioritize whole, minimally processed foods for better nutrient density, satiety, and long-term health outcomes
- Maintain consistent hydration (35ml/kg bodyweight daily) and replace electrolytes lost through sweat during exercise
- Choose sustainable approaches you can follow long-term rather than extreme short-term restrictions

**1. Energy Balance - The Master Controller**
Energy balance determines body weight changes through the relationship between calories consumed and calories burned [[A1. Energy Balance]]. Your total daily energy expenditure [[A2. Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)]] includes metabolic processes, physical activity, and food digestion. Goals dictate your target balance:
- Deficit for fat loss [[C26. Nutrition for Fat Loss]]
- Surplus for muscle gain [[C25. Nutrition for Muscle Gain (Hypertrophy)]]  
- Maintenance for body composition recomposition [[C1. Caloric Targets for Different Goals]]

Research consistently demonstrates that energy balance is the primary determinant of weight change, regardless of macronutrient composition (Hall et al., 2012).

**2. Macronutrient Adequacy - Meeting Physiological Needs**
Each macronutrient serves distinct physiological functions [[A3. Macronutrients]], [[C2. Macronutrient Distribution]]:

**Protein:** Essential for muscle protein synthesis, enzyme production, and immune function [[B2. Protein Requirements for Muscle Growth]]. Requirements range from 0.8g/kg (sedentary) to 1.6-2.2g/kg (active individuals) bodyweight daily.

**Carbohydrates:** Primary fuel for moderate-to-high intensity exercise. Muscle glycogen stores become depleted after 60-90 minutes of sustained activity, making adequate intake crucial for performance.

**Fats:** Required for hormone production, cell membrane integrity, and absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. Minimum intake should be 20-35% of total calories.

**3. Micronutrient Sufficiency - The Metabolic Foundation**
Vitamins and minerals function as cofactors in enzymatic reactions, energy production, and cellular repair [[A4. Micronutrients]], [[C6. Micronutrients (Detailed Roles & Considerations)]]. Deficiencies in iron, vitamin D, B-vitamins, or magnesium can significantly impair performance and recovery capacity.

**4. Hydration and Electrolyte Balance**
Fluid balance affects every physiological system, from cardiovascular function to temperature regulation [[C4. Hydration]], [[C5. Electrolytes]]. Even 2% dehydration can reduce performance by 10-15% and impair cognitive function.

**5. Food Quality - Nutrient Density Matters**
Whole, minimally processed foods provide superior nutrient density, fiber content, and satiety signals compared to ultra-processed alternatives [[C30. Food Quality & Health (Whole vs Processed)]], [[C32. Gut Health and Microbiome]]. Higher fiber intake supports gut health and helps regulate appetite hormones.

**6. Sustainability and Adherence - The Long Game**
The most effective diet is one you can follow consistently over time [[D2. Diet Adherence and Behavior Change]], [[N3. Building Sustainable Fitness Habits]]. Extreme restrictions often lead to psychological stress and eventual abandonment of healthy practices.

**Nutrient Timing** can enhance performance and recovery when basic nutrition principles are already established [[C3. Nutrient Timing]], [[C29. Nutrition for Recovery]]. Post-exercise protein intake (20-40g) within 2 hours optimizes muscle protein synthesis rates.

**Strategic Supplementation** should address specific deficiencies or performance goals based on scientific evidence [[C20. Supplements (Overview & Framework)]], [[C21. Evidence-Based Supplements (Group A)]]. Most nutrients are best obtained through whole foods first.

**Daily Implementation:**
- Calculate your TDEE and adjust calories by ±300-500 for body composition goals
- Include protein at each meal (20-30g servings throughout the day)
- Fill half your plate with vegetables, one-quarter with lean protein, one-quarter with complex carbohydrates
- Drink 35ml per kg bodyweight daily, plus 500-750ml per hour of exercise
- Choose whole foods 80-90% of the time, allowing flexibility for social situations

**Weekly Planning:**
- Prepare 3-4 balanced meals in advance to ensure consistency
- Shop the perimeter of grocery stores where whole foods are typically located
- Track intake for 1-2 weeks to establish baseline awareness of current habits

### **Q2. Core Nutrition Principles Overview**

⚠️ **Focusing on single nutrients instead of overall diet quality** - No single food or nutrient determines health outcomes

⚠️ **Extreme caloric restrictions** - Deficits greater than 25% of TDEE often lead to muscle loss and metabolic adaptations

⚠️ **Neglecting hydration until feeling thirsty** - Thirst is a late indicator of dehydration

⚠️ **All-or-nothing mentality** - Perfectionism leads to abandonment when lapses occur

⚠️ **Ignoring individual preferences and lifestyle** - Sustainable changes must fit your actual life circumstances
