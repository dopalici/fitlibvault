### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
The plank is a foundational isometric exercise that builds core stability by holding a straight-body position, with progressions from 20-second holds for beginners to advanced variations with movement and instability.

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
- Hold a straight line from head to heels, avoiding hip sag or pike
- Start with 20-30 second holds, progress to 60+ seconds before adding variations
- Breathe steadily throughout - don't hold your breath
- Quality over duration - stop when form breaks down
- Progress through knee planks → standard plank → movement variations → unstable surfaces

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
An isometric core stability exercise [[B19. Isometrics]] involving holding a rigid, straight line from head to heels, typically supported on forearms/hands and toes. Primarily targets the anterior core muscles [[L4. Core Stability Training]] and emphasizes resisting spinal extension while engaging multiple stabilizing muscle groups throughout the body.

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
**Forearm Plank Setup:**
- Lie prone, place forearms on floor with elbows directly under shoulders
- Palms flat or clasped, toes on floor, feet hip-width apart
- Lift hips off floor, creating straight line from head to heels

**High Plank Setup:**
- Hands directly under shoulders, arms straight (like top of push-up [[EXL-83 Push-up (Standard & Variations)]])
- Maintain same body alignment as forearm variation

**Execution:**
- Engage core by bracing abdominals and squeezing glutes
- Avoid hips sagging (lumbar hyperextension) or rising too high (piking)
- Keep head/neck aligned with spine, looking down or slightly forward
- Hold position isometrically with steady, controlled breathing

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
- Hips sagging below neutral spine
- Hips too high (creating inverted V shape)
- Head dropping or looking up
- Holding breath or excessive Valsalva
- Shoulders creeping toward ears
- Feet too close together (reduces stability base)

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
Research demonstrates the plank's effectiveness for core strengthening and stability. Studies show significant activation of deep core muscles including transverse abdominis and multifidus during plank holds (Ekstrom et al., 2007). EMG analysis reveals the plank produces moderate to high activation of rectus abdominis (comparable to crunches) while providing superior activation of stabilizing muscles (Schoenfeld et al., 2014).

Evidence supports isometric training's transfer to dynamic core stability tasks, with plank training improving performance in functional movement assessments (Hibbs et al., 2008).

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
- Start with knee plank: 3 sets × 15-30 seconds
- Progress to incline plank (hands elevated): 3 sets × 20-40 seconds
- Advance to standard plank: 3 sets × 20-60 seconds

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
- Standard plank: 3-4 sets × 45-90 seconds
- Add single limb raises during hold
- Introduce unstable surface variations

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
- **RKC Plank:** Maximum tension throughout entire body
- **Long Lever Plank:** Elbows positioned further forward
- **Body Saw:** Using sliders or stability ball for anterior-posterior movement
- **Plank with limb movements:** Alternating arm/leg raises, shoulder taps
- **Unstable surface planks:** Using stability ball or suspension trainer [[B12. Suspension Training (TRX)]]

### **EXL-84: Plank (Standard & Variations)**
⚠️ Prioritizing hold duration over proper form alignment
⚠️ Allowing hips to sag due to weak glutes or poor hip flexor mobility
⚠️ Holding breath instead of maintaining steady breathing pattern
⚠️ Progressing too quickly to advanced variations without mastering basics
⚠️ Ignoring shoulder positioning and allowing forward head posture
