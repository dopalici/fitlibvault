### IX9. Load Management in Rehabilitation

Load management in rehabilitation involves the systematic application and progression of mechanical stress to promote optimal tissue adaptation while avoiding overload that could delay healing or cause re-injury. This approach balances the need for sufficient stimulus to drive adaptation with appropriate recovery to prevent tissue breakdown.

**Core Components:**
- **Tissue-Specific Loading Parameters:** Understanding load tolerance and adaptation timelines for different tissues using [[../foundation/A29. Tissue Adaptation to Exercise (Molecular & Cellular)]] and [[../foundation/A42. Connective Tissue Adaptations to Training]] principles
- **Progressive Overload Application:** Systematic increase in load magnitude, frequency, and complexity following [[../resistance_training/B1. Progressive Overload]] and [[../foundation/A19. Principle of Overload]] frameworks
- **Load Monitoring and Assessment:** Objective tracking of external and internal load responses using [[../foundation/A48. Training Load Monitoring]] and [[../foundation/A49. Recovery Monitoring and Assessment]] methodologies
- **Recovery Integration:** Balancing loading with adequate recovery periods following [[../foundation/A26. Principle of Recovery]] and [[../recovery/M1. Recovery Modalities Evidence and Application]] guidelines
- **Individual Response Adaptation:** Modifying load progression based on individual healing rates and [[../foundation/A23. Principle of Individuality]] considerations
