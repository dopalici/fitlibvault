### X16. American Football Training

American football training emphasizes maximal strength, explosive power, and position-specific conditioning to meet the demands of high-impact collisions, short bursts of maximal effort, and varied positional requirements. Training is highly periodized around competitive seasons with emphasis on injury prevention and recovery.

**Core Components:**
- **Maximal Strength Development:** Heavy compound movements ([[../exercise_library/EXL-01 Barbell Back Squat (High Bar & Low Bar)]], [[../exercise_library/EXL-03 Barbell Conventional Deadlift]], [[../exercise_library/EXL-02 Barbell Bench Press (Flat)]]) form the foundation for power development and injury prevention in contact situations ([[../resistance_training/B4. Strength Training Principles]]).
- **Explosive Power Training:** Olympic lifting variations ([[../resistance_training/B15. Olympic Weightlifting]]) and plyometric exercises ([[X11.5 Plyometric Training for Sprinters]]) to develop rate of force development for blocking, tackling, and acceleration ([[../foundation/A32. Power Profiling and Testing]]).
- **Position-Specific Conditioning:** Linemen focus on anaerobic power and strength endurance, while skill position players emphasize speed and agility training ([[../cardiovascular_training/E4. Interval Training (General)]]) with appropriate work-to-rest ratios ([[../foundation/A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]]).
- **Collision Preparation and Injury Prevention:** Eccentric strength training ([[../resistance_training/B20. Eccentric Training]]), neck strengthening, and movement screening ([[../foundation/A17. Movement Screening]]) to prepare for high-impact collisions and reduce concussion risk.
- **Speed and Acceleration Development:** Sprint mechanics training ([[X11. Sprinting]]), resisted sprint methods ([[X11.6 Resisted & Assisted Sprint Training Methods]]), and first-step quickness drills for position-specific movement demands.
