### X11.8 Hamstring Injury Prevention in Sprinters

## Quick Takeaway
Nordic hamstring exercises and proper training load management can reduce hamstring injury risk in sprinters by up to 50%, with prevention focusing on eccentric strength and addressing muscle imbalances.

## Key Points
- Nordic hamstring exercises are the most evidence-backed prevention method, reducing injury rates by ~50%
- Previous hamstring injury is the strongest predictor of future injury - prevention is critical
- Eccentric strength training must be progressive and consistent (2-3x/week minimum)
- Address quad-hamstring strength imbalances and between-limb asymmetries early
- Gradual sprint volume progression and adequate recovery prevent overload injuries

## Deep Dive

Hamstring strain injuries (HSIs) are the most common acute injury in sprinting and many team sports involving sprinting. They typically occur during high-speed running, often during the late swing phase (terminal swing) or early stance phase, involving rapid eccentric loading as the hamstrings decelerate knee extension and initiate hip extension.

### Primary Risk Factors

**Previous HSI History**
Previous hamstring strain injury represents the strongest predictor of future HSI, with re-injury rates ranging from 12-33% in athletic populations (Heiderscheit et al., 2010). This emphasizes the critical importance of proper rehabilitation and ongoing prevention strategies.

**Strength-Related Factors**
- **Eccentric Ham:Quad Ratio:** Weak hamstrings relative to quadriceps during eccentric actions increase injury risk
- **Between-limb Asymmetries:** >10-15% strength differences between legs elevate risk
- **Biceps Femoris Vulnerability:** The long head is most commonly injured due to its dual-joint function
- **Synergistic Weakness:** Poor gluteal strength can increase hamstring compensation and strain

**Neuromuscular Control Issues**
Poor lumbopelvic control and inadequate [[../cross_training/L4. Core Stability Training]] can lead to altered pelvic positioning (excessive anterior tilt), increasing hamstring strain during high-speed movements.

**Training Load Errors**
Rapid increases in sprint volume or intensity without adequate adaptation represent a major modifiable risk factor, particularly early in training phases.

### Evidence-Based Prevention Strategies

**Eccentric Hamstring Strengthening**
The Nordic Hamstring Exercise (NHE) shows the strongest evidence base, with multiple systematic reviews demonstrating 50+ percent reductions in HSI incidence (van Dyk et al., 2019). Other effective eccentric exercises include razor curls and single-leg RDLs with controlled eccentric phases.

**Comprehensive Strength Training**
Beyond hamstring-specific work, address the entire posterior chain through exercises targeting glutes, erectors, and core musculature. Integration with [[X11.4 Strength Training Programming for Sprinters]] ensures systematic development.

**Training Load Management**
Implement gradual progression protocols and monitor athlete readiness through appropriate [[../integrated_approaches/O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]] methods to prevent overload-related injuries.

## Practical Applications

**Nordic Hamstring Exercise Protocol:**
- Week 1-2: 2x5 reps (assisted eccentric, partner catch)
- Week 3-4: 2x8 reps (less assistance)
- Week 5-8: 2x10 reps (minimal assistance)
- Week 9+: 3x10 reps (full bodyweight control)
- Frequency: 2-3 sessions per week during preparation phases, 1-2 during competition

**Strength Assessment Benchmarks:**
- Eccentric Ham:Quad ratio >60%
- Between-limb strength asymmetry <10%
- Nordic hamstring: Control bodyweight eccentric for 3+ seconds

**Training Load Progression:**
- Increase sprint volume by no more than 10% weekly
- Build high-speed running exposure gradually over 4-6 weeks
- Monitor session RPE and next-day readiness markers

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Focusing only on static stretching** - Excessive static stretching pre-activity may increase injury risk rather than reduce it

⚠️ **Neglecting eccentric strength** - Concentric hamstring exercises alone provide insufficient protection against high-speed running demands

⚠️ **Inconsistent Nordic exercise progression** - Performing Nordics only occasionally or without proper progression limits effectiveness

⚠️ **Ignoring previous injury history** - Athletes with prior HSI need intensified prevention strategies, not standard protocols

⚠️ **Inadequate warm-up specificity** - Generic warm-ups don't prepare tissue for sprint-specific eccentric demands
