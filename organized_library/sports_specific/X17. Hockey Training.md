### X17. Hockey Training

Hockey training integrates anaerobic power, core stability, and multidirectional movement patterns to optimize performance for high-intensity shifts, skating mechanics, and puck handling while maintaining balance and power on ice. Training emphasizes lateral movement patterns and rotational core strength unique to skating sports.

**Core Components:**
- **Anaerobic Power and Lactate Tolerance:** High-intensity interval training ([[../cardiovascular_training/E3. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)]]) with work-to-rest ratios mimicking hockey shifts (45-90 seconds work, 2-4 minutes rest) to improve repeated sprint ability ([[../foundation/A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]]).
- **Lateral Movement and Skating-Specific Strength:** Single-leg exercises ([[../exercise_library/EXL-38 Dumbbell Split Squat]]), lateral lunges, and hip abduction/adduction strength ([[../exercise_library/EXL-63 Hip Abduction (Machine)]]) to support skating stride mechanics and injury prevention.
- **Rotational Core Power:** Medicine ball throws, cable woodchops ([[EXL-76 Cable Woodchop (High-to-Low & Low-to-High)]]), and anti-rotation exercises ([[../exercise_library/EXL-77 Cable Pallof Press (Anti-Rotation)]]) for shooting power and maintaining balance during contact ([[../cross_training/L4. Core Stability Training]]).
- **Upper Body Power for Shooting and Checking:** Plyometric upper body exercises, Olympic pulling variations ([[../resistance_training/B15. Olympic Weightlifting]]), and unilateral strength training to develop asymmetrical power for stick handling and body checking ([[../resistance_training/B4. Strength Training Principles]]).
- **Balance and Proprioception:** Single-leg stability work ([[../cross_training/L3. Balance and Coordination Training]]) and unstable surface training to improve on-ice balance, edge control, and injury prevention in the unstable skating environment.
