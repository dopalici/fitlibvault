### X10. CrossFit
**ID:** X10-CROSSFIT
**Created:** 2025-04-24 (Updated: 2025-01-11)

## Quick Takeaway
CrossFit combines Olympic lifting, powerlifting, gymnastics, and cardio in constantly varied, high-intensity workouts that can improve general fitness but requires quality coaching to minimize injury risk.

## Key Points
- Develops broad fitness across 10 physical domains (strength, endurance, power, speed, etc.)
- Daily varied workouts (WODs) prevent adaptation plateaus but may hinder specific goal progress
- High injury risk if coaching is poor or technique is sacrificed for intensity
- Choose affiliates with qualified coaches who emphasize proper movement patterns
- Prioritize technique mastery over workout speed, especially when learning complex movements

## Deep Dive

CrossFit is a branded fitness regimen characterized by constantly varied, high-intensity, functional movements. Workouts (WODs - Workout of the Day) typically combine elements of Olympic weightlifting, powerlifting, gymnastics/calisthenics, running, rowing, and other modalities.

### Core Methodology and Training Principles

**Constantly Varied:** Workouts change daily, aiming to develop broad, general fitness across multiple domains rather than specialization. This approach prevents adaptation plateaus but may not optimize progress toward specific performance goals.

**High Intensity:** Emphasis on performing workouts at high relative intensity, often measured by time to completion or total reps within a time limit. Research shows high-intensity training can improve both aerobic and anaerobic capacity (Laursen & Jenkins, 2002).

**Functional Movements:** Utilizes multi-joint movements mimicking natural patterns (squats, deadlifts, presses, pulls, Olympic lifts, basic gymnastics) - [[../cross_training/L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]].

### Physical Demands and Fitness Domains

CrossFit targets ten recognized fitness domains, requiring broad capacity across multiple areas:

- **Cardiovascular/Respiratory Endurance:** Needed for longer "metcon" (metabolic conditioning) workouts
- **Stamina (Muscular Endurance):** Ability to sustain repeated movements under fatigue
- **Strength:** Foundational for lifting components and overall performance
- **Power:** Required for Olympic lifts, jumping, and explosive movements
- **Speed:** Developed through sprinting, cycling, and rowing intervals
- **Flexibility & Mobility:** Essential for safe execution of lifts (overhead squat, snatch) and gymnastics movements
- **Coordination, Agility, Balance, Accuracy:** Developed through varied movement practice

### Training Components

**WODs (Workouts of the Day):** Daily workouts combining multiple modalities with formats including:
- AMRAP (As Many Reps/Rounds As Possible)
- For Time workouts
- EMOM (Every Minute On the Minute)
- Tabata protocols

**Strength Work:** Dedicated sessions focusing on powerlifting and weightlifting movements, often following linear or conjugate progression models.

**Gymnastics Skill Practice:** Developing proficiency in bodyweight movements (pull-ups, handstands, muscle-ups) requiring significant strength-to-weight ratios.

**Metabolic Conditioning:** Workouts designed to challenge cardiovascular and energy system endurance through varied time domains and intensities.

## Research Evidence

**Fitness Improvements:**
- Claudino JG, et al. (2018). CrossFit Overview: Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Sports Medicine - Open, 4(1), 11. DOI: 10.1186/s40798-018-0124-5
  - Found significant improvements in VO2max, body composition, and strength measures
  - Effect sizes ranged from moderate to large across fitness parameters

**Injury Risk:**
- Hak PT, Hodzovic E, Hickey B. (2013). The nature and prevalence of injury during CrossFit training. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 27(11), 3295-3312.
  - Reported injury rates of 3.1 injuries per 1000 hours of training
  - Most common injuries: shoulders (25%), back (14%), knees (13%)

- Weisenthal BM, et al. (2014). Injury rate and patterns among CrossFit athletes. Orthopaedic Journal of Sports Medicine, 2(4), 2325967114531177. DOI: 10.1177/2325967114531177
  - Found 19.4% injury rate among surveyed athletes
  - Higher rates associated with competitive participation

**Training Adaptations:**
- Meyer J, et al. (2017). The effects of high intensity interval training on skeletal muscle hypertrophy in resistance-trained men: A pilot study. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 14, 22. DOI: 10.1186/s12970-017-0180-4

## Practical Applications

**Getting Started:**
- Begin with 2-3 sessions per week, allowing adequate recovery
- Focus on learning fundamental movements before adding intensity
- Scale all workouts to match current fitness level and technical ability
- Prioritize consistency over intensity in first 3-6 months

**Workout Scaling:**
- Reduce weight by 20-50% when learning new movements
- Modify complex movements (knee push-ups instead of regular, ring rows instead of pull-ups)
- Adjust time domains and rep schemes based on fitness level

**Progressive Development:**
- Master air squat before adding weight
- Develop strict pull-ups before attempting kipping variations
- Learn proper deadlift form before progressing to Olympic lifts

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Sacrificing form for speed/intensity** - Leads to injury and poor movement patterns
⚠️ **Ignoring scaling options** - Attempting advanced movements without prerequisite strength/skill
⚠️ **Inadequate recovery** - Training through fatigue increases injury risk significantly
⚠️ **Choosing affiliate based on price alone** - Coaching quality varies dramatically between locations
⚠️ **Comparing beginner performance to experienced athletes** - Creates unrealistic expectations and overreaching
