### X11. Sprinting

## Quick Takeaway
Sprinting is the most demanding form of human locomotion, requiring explosive power, precise technique across distinct phases, and specific training targeting the ATP-PCr and glycolytic energy systems for maximum speed development.

## Key Points
- Sprinting relies heavily on anaerobic energy systems - ATP-PCr for first 5-8 seconds, then glycolysis
- Three distinct phases require different techniques: acceleration (0-30m), maximum velocity (30-80m), and speed endurance (>60m)
- Training must include technique work, strength/power development, and specific speed work with full recovery
- Hamstring strains are the most common injury due to extreme eccentric forces during high-speed running
- Creatine supplementation (3-5g daily) significantly enhances sprint performance by boosting ATP-PCr capacity

## Deep Dive

Sprinting represents maximal velocity human locomotion over short distances, typically ranging from 60m dashes to 400m events. It demands extraordinary integration of neuromuscular power, metabolic capacity, refined technique, and specific physical preparation.

### Physiological Demands

#### Energy Systems
Sprint performance is fundamentally anaerobic, relying on immediate energy systems detailed in understanding energy pathways [[../foundation/A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]]:

**ATP-PCr System:** Dominates the first 5-8 seconds, crucial for overcoming inertia during acceleration. Limited by intramuscular phosphocreatine stores, making creatine supplementation highly effective [[../nutrition_diet/C21. Evidence-Based Supplements (Group A)]].

**Glycolytic System:** Becomes primary after 8-15 seconds, especially in 200m-400m events. Provides rapid ATP but accumulates metabolic byproducts (lactate, H+) causing fatigue and acidosis. Training enhances glycolytic enzyme activity and buffering capacity.

**Oxidative System:** Minimal contribution during maximal sprints but critical for phosphocreatine resynthesis and lactate clearance between efforts, supporting repeated sprint ability.

#### Neuromuscular Factors
Maximal speed requires exceptional neural drive and coordination as outlined in movement neurophysiology [[../documentation/G-VII-3. Neurophysiology of Movement]]:

- **Motor Unit Recruitment:** Maximal activation of high-threshold motor units, particularly those innervating Type IIx and IIa fibers [[../foundation/A7. Muscle Physiology Fundamentals]]
- **Rate Coding:** High motor neuron firing frequencies for maximal force production
- **Stretch-Shortening Cycle:** Efficient elastic energy storage and recoil during brief ground contacts (<0.1s at max velocity)
- **Rate of Force Development:** Ability to produce high forces rapidly, especially critical during short ground contacts

### Biomechanical Phases and Technique

#### Start & Acceleration Phase (0-30m)
**Objective:** Maximize horizontal velocity gain by overcoming inertia through high net horizontal ground reaction forces.

**Key Technique Elements:**
- Deep forward body lean with center of gravity ahead of support foot
- Powerful triple extension (hip-knee-ankle) driving backward against ground
- Low heel recovery with gradual transition to upright posture
- Forceful, coordinated arm swing supporting leg drive

#### Maximum Velocity Phase (30-80m)
**Objective:** Achieve and maintain highest possible speed through effective vertical force application and stretch-shortening cycle utilization.

**Key Technique Elements:**
- Near-upright posture with slight forward lean
- Cyclical leg action: rapid hip flexion bringing thigh toward parallel, followed by forceful downward "pawing" action
- Minimal ground contact time (<0.1s)
- High stride frequency with stiff ankle complex upon contact
- Relaxed upper body with coordinated arm swing

#### Speed Endurance Phase (>60m)
**Objective:** Minimize deceleration due to neuromuscular fatigue and metabolic byproduct accumulation.

**Key Focus:** Maintaining technique under fatigue, emphasizing front-side mechanics and efficient arm swing despite declining neural drive.

### Training Components

#### Technique Development
Essential foundation requiring consistent practice through specific drills:
- Block starts and acceleration mechanics
- Maximum velocity drills (A-skips, B-skips, wicket runs)
- Video analysis for technique refinement

#### Acceleration Training
- Short sprints (10-40m) with full recovery
- Resisted sprints using sleds (10-20% bodyweight), hills, or bands
- Focus on powerful horizontal force production

#### Maximum Velocity Development
- Flying sprints (30-80m) with build-up runs
- Full recovery between efforts (1 minute per 10m sprinted)
- Occasional assisted sprints (slight downhill <3°) for overspeed training

#### Speed Endurance Training
- **Short Speed Endurance:** 150-300m at 90-95% intensity with long recovery
- **Lactate Tolerance:** Repeated 200m efforts with incomplete recovery (2-3 min)
- **Extensive Tempo:** 300-600m at 70-80% intensity for aerobic recovery enhancement

#### Strength and Power Development
Foundation strength training principles [[../resistance_training/B4. Strength Training Principles]] must support sprint-specific adaptations:

**Strength Training:** Heavy compound movements (squats, deadlifts, hip thrusts) targeting key sprint muscles. Upper body and core work for force transfer [[../cross_training/L4. Core Stability Training]].

**Power Training:** Critical for rate of force development [[../resistance_training/B13. Strength Training Goals (Max Strength, Power, Hypertrophy, Endurance)]]:
- Plyometrics: Jumps, bounds, hops emphasizing minimal ground contact time
- Olympic lift variations developing triple extension power [[../resistance_training/B15. Olympic Weightlifting]]  
- Medicine ball throws for rotational and upper body power

#### Mobility and Flexibility
Essential for optimal movement patterns [[../cross_training/L2. Mobility and Flexibility Development]], particularly hip flexors, hamstrings, glutes, ankles, and thoracic spine. Dynamic mobility in warm-ups, static stretching post-training.

## Practical Applications

**Weekly Training Structure:**
- 2-3 speed sessions per week with 48-72 hours between high-intensity efforts
- Technique work in every speed session when nervous system is fresh
- Strength training 2-3x weekly, avoiding high-volume leg work before speed sessions
- Recovery/tempo work on alternate days

**Session Guidelines:**
- Complete warm-up including dynamic mobility and activation drills
- Speed work first in session when nervous system is fresh
- Full recovery between sprints (walk back to start, feeling fully recovered)
- Volume: 150-300m total sprint distance for pure speed work
- Quality over quantity - stop when technique deteriorates

**Periodization:**
- Base phase: Emphasize strength, technique, and general preparation
- Build phase: Increase sprint-specific training volume and intensity
- Peak phase: Reduce volume, maintain intensity, perfect technique
- Recovery phase: Active recovery with technique maintenance

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Insufficient Recovery:** Training speed when fatigued leads to poor technique reinforcement and increased injury risk

⚠️ **Overstriding:** Landing foot too far ahead of center of gravity creates braking forces and reduces efficiency

⚠️ **Premature Upright Posture:** Standing up too quickly during acceleration reduces horizontal force production

⚠️ **Excessive Volume:** Too much high-intensity work without adequate recovery overwhelms the nervous system

⚠️ **Neglecting Strength Training:** Inadequate force production capacity limits sprint potential and increases injury risk

⚠️ **Poor Technique Under Fatigue:** Continuing to sprint when form breaks down reinforces poor movement patterns

**Fueling Strategy:**
- High carbohydrate availability for glycogen replenishment [[../foundation/A3. Macronutrients]]
- Strategic nutrient timing around training sessions [[../nutrition_diet/C5. Pre, During, and Post-Exercise Nutrition]]
- Adequate protein intake for muscle repair [[../resistance_training/B2. Protein Requirements for Muscle Growth]]

**Evidence-Based Supplementation:**
- Creatine monohydrate 3-5g daily significantly enhances ATP-PCr capacity
- Beta-alanine may benefit 200-400m performance through improved buffering
- Caffeine can reduce perceived exertion and enhance power output

**Recovery Priorities:**
Quality sleep for nervous system recovery [[../performance_factors/F1. Sleep Quality and Quantity]], stress management [[../recovery/M3. Stress Management and Performance]], and comprehensive recovery strategies [[../recovery/M. Recovery Strategies]] due to high CNS demands.

## Injury Prevention

**Primary Risk - Hamstring Strains:**
Most common acute sprint injury, typically occurring during late swing phase eccentric loading or at maximum velocity. Prevention strategies detailed in injury prevention protocols [[../health_conditions/IX-2. Injury Prevention Strategies]].

**Risk Factors:**
- Inadequate warm-up preparation
- Poor sprint mechanics, especially overstriding
- Muscle imbalances (quadriceps:hamstring strength ratios)
- Insufficient eccentric strength
- Training load errors and fatigue accumulation

**Other Common Issues:**
Hip flexor strains, adductor strains, Achilles tendon problems, and stress fractures from high ground reaction forces [[../health_conditions/IX-1. Common Musculoskeletal Injuries]].
