### Nutrition Risk Management
**ID:** C103
**Created:** 2025-06-05 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Nutrition risk management systematically identifies and prevents potential health risks from nutritional interventions in athletic populations through comprehensive safety protocols, screening systems, and emergency response planning.

## Key Points
- Supplement contamination poses significant risks requiring third-party testing and banned substance monitoring
- Food safety protocols prevent foodborne illness through proper handling, storage, and preparation guidelines
- Allergy and intolerance management systems protect athletes with dietary restrictions in team settings
- Early detection systems help prevent and intervene in eating disorders and relative energy deficiency
- Emergency response protocols address severe allergic reactions and metabolic complications

## Deep Dive

### Supplement Contamination Prevention
Supplement contamination represents one of the highest risks in sports nutrition, with studies showing up to 25% of supplements containing undeclared substances. Effective prevention requires implementing rigorous screening protocols for supplement safety through third-party testing verification and continuous banned substance monitoring [[C23. Supplement Safety, Regulation, and Quality Control]].

### Food Safety Management
Comprehensive food safety protocols form the foundation of nutrition risk management. These systems establish detailed guidelines for food handling, storage, and preparation to prevent foodborne illness and contamination, particularly critical in team dining facilities and travel situations [[C4. Hydration]].

### Allergy and Intolerance Systems
Athletic populations require specialized approaches to food allergy and intolerance management. Effective systems identify, document, and accommodate food allergies, intolerances, and dietary restrictions while maintaining nutritional adequacy and team meal logistics [[C32. Gut Health and Microbiome]].

### Disordered Eating Prevention
Sports environments can increase eating disorder risk, particularly in aesthetic and weight-class sports. Prevention systems include early detection protocols, screening tools, and intervention pathways for eating disorders and relative energy deficiency in sport [[../hormones_fitness/I3. Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S)]].

### Emergency Response Protocols
Nutrition-related medical emergencies require immediate, coordinated responses. Clear protocols address severe allergic reactions, metabolic complications, and other acute situations that may arise from nutritional interventions [[../health_conditions/IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]].

## Research Evidence

**Maughan et al. (2018).** IOC consensus statement: dietary supplements and the high-performance athlete. *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 52(7), 439-455. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-099027
- Key finding: 12-58% of dietary supplements contain prohibited substances not declared on labels
- Study type: Systematic review and consensus statement
- Evidence quality: High (IOC expert consensus with comprehensive literature review)

**Garthe & Maughan (2018).** Athletes and supplements: prevalence and perspectives. *International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism*, 28(2), 126-138. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2017-0429
- Key finding: 89% of elite athletes use supplements, with contamination risk as primary safety concern
- Study type: Cross-sectional survey
- Evidence quality: Medium (large sample but observational design)

**Mountjoy et al. (2018).** IOC consensus statement on relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S): 2018 update. *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 52(11), 687-697. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-099193
- Key finding: RED-S affects 25-30% of female endurance athletes and requires systematic screening
- Study type: Consensus statement with systematic evidence review
- Evidence quality: High (IOC expert consensus with comprehensive evidence base)

## Practical Applications

**Supplement Screening Protocol:**
- Require NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport certification
- Maintain approved supplement list updated quarterly
- Document all supplement use with batch numbers
- Implement 48-hour notification rule for new supplements

**Food Safety Implementation:**
- Temperature monitoring at 2-hour intervals for hot/cold foods
- HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points) system for meal preparation
- Staff training certification every 6 months
- Allergen-free preparation zones and equipment

**Emergency Response Plan:**
- Epinephrine auto-injectors available at all team meals
- Staff trained in anaphylaxis recognition and response
- Emergency contact cards for all athletes with known allergies
- Clear evacuation and medical response protocols

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Assuming all "natural" or "herbal" supplements are safe without third-party testing
⚠️ Failing to update emergency action plans when team rosters or dietary restrictions change
⚠️ Inadequate communication between nutrition staff and medical team about athlete risk factors
⚠️ Not maintaining proper documentation of supplement batch numbers and usage dates
