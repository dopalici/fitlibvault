### C27. Nutrition for Endurance Athletes

## Quick Takeaway
Endurance athletes need strategic carbohydrate periodization (5-12g/kg/day based on training load), consistent hydration practices, and practiced fueling strategies to maintain performance during prolonged exercise lasting over 90 minutes.

## Key Points
- Match daily carb intake to training load: 5-7g/kg for moderate training, up to 12g/kg for extreme volume (>4-5h/day)
- Practice race-day fueling: 30-60g carbs/hour for events up to 2.5 hours, up to 90g/hour for ultra-endurance
- Prevent dehydration beyond 2% body weight loss through individualized hydration plans
- Time recovery nutrition within 4 hours: 1.0-1.2g carbs/kg/hour plus 20-40g protein when rapid recovery is needed
- Monitor key micronutrients: iron (especially females), vitamin D, and calcium status

## Deep Dive

Nutrition for endurance athletes focuses on optimizing fuel availability (primarily carbohydrate), maintaining hydration, preventing gastrointestinal distress, and supporting recovery to sustain performance over prolonged durations during [[../cardiovascular_training/E. Cardiovascular  Endurance Training]].

### Carbohydrate Periodization Strategy

**Daily Carbohydrate Requirements:** Crucial for moderate-to-high intensity endurance exercise lasting >90 minutes. Daily intake must match training load (Thomas et al., 2016):
- Moderate duration/low intensity: 5-7 g/kg/day
- Moderate-heavy endurance (1-3h/day): 6-10 g/kg/day  
- Extreme exercise (>4-5h/day): 8-12 g/kg/day

This periodized approach ensures adequate muscle glycogen stores while avoiding unnecessary overconsumption during lighter training phases. See comprehensive macronutrient guidelines in [[../foundation/A3. Macronutrients]].

### Pre-Competition Strategies

**Carbohydrate Loading (Supercompensation):** Strategy to maximize muscle glycogen stores before major competitions lasting >90 minutes. Effective protocols involve consuming **8-12 g CHO/kg/day** for 1-3 days alongside an exercise taper (Burke et al., 2011). This supercompensation can increase muscle glycogen stores by 20-40% above normal levels, significantly delaying fatigue onset.

**Pre-Event Meal:** Consume 1-4 g CHO/kg 1-4 hours before the event. Focus on familiar, easily digestible foods low in fat and fiber to minimize gastrointestinal distress. Timing depends on individual tolerance and event start time [[C3. Nutrient Timing]].

### During-Exercise Fueling

**Intra-Event Fueling:** Essential for events >60-90 minutes (Jeukendrup, 2014):
- Target **30-60 g CHO per hour** for events up to ~2.5 hours
- For longer ultra-endurance events, intake can increase up to **90 g CHO per hour**
- Utilize multiple transportable carbohydrates (e.g., glucose/maltodextrin + fructose) for optimal absorption and oxidation rates

Sources include sports drinks, gels, chews, bars, or suitable whole foods. The key principle: practice your exact fueling strategy during training sessions to avoid race-day gastrointestinal issues.

### Hydration and Electrolyte Management

Critical for both performance and health. Develop an individualized hydration plan based on sweat rate testing, event duration, and environmental conditions to prevent >2% body weight loss [[C4. Hydration]]. Replace sodium and other key electrolytes lost in sweat, especially during prolonged events (>2 hours) or in hot conditions [[C5. Electrolytes]].

### Supporting Nutrients

**Protein Requirements:** Supports muscle repair and adaptation. Typically **1.2-1.8 g/kg/day** recommended for endurance athletes, with higher intakes during periods of increased training load or caloric restriction (Thomas et al., 2016).

**Recovery Nutrition:** Timely intake of carbohydrates (1.0-1.2 g/kg/hour for first ~4h if rapid recovery needed) and protein (0.3-0.5 g/kg or 20-40g) becomes critical with frequent training sessions or multi-day competitions [[C29. Nutrition for Recovery]].

## Practical Applications

**Training Phase Periodization:**
- Base building (moderate intensity): 5-7g carbs/kg/day
- Peak training blocks: 8-10g carbs/kg/day  
- Competition preparation: 8-12g carbs/kg/day for 1-3 days pre-event

**Race Day Timeline Example (Marathon):**
- 3 hours before: Large meal with 2-3g carbs/kg
- 1 hour before: Small snack with 1g carbs/kg
- During race: 30-60g carbs/hour starting at 45-60 minutes
- Post-race: 1.2g carbs/kg within first hour

**Sweat Rate Testing Protocol:**
1. Weigh yourself nude pre-exercise
2. Train for 1 hour at race pace/conditions
3. Weigh yourself nude post-exercise
4. Calculate: (Pre-weight - Post-weight + fluid consumed) = sweat rate/hour

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Under-fueling during training:** Not practicing race-day nutrition strategies leads to GI distress on competition day

⚠️ **Static carb intake:** Using the same daily carbohydrate intake regardless of training load leads to either under-fueling or unnecessary overconsumption

⚠️ **Ignoring individual tolerance:** Copying other athletes' strategies without testing personal GI tolerance and preferences

⚠️ **Dehydration misconceptions:** Either over-drinking (hyponatremia risk) or under-drinking based on thirst alone rather than planned intake

⚠️ **Micronutrient neglect:** Focusing only on macronutrients while ignoring iron status, vitamin D, and other key micronutrients
