### **EXL-85: Side Plank (Standard & Variations)**

**ID:** EXL-85-BW-SIDEPLANK **Created:** 2025-05-01 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
The side plank is an isometric core exercise that targets lateral stabilizers, particularly the obliques, by maintaining a straight body line while supported on one forearm and feet.

## Key Points
- Primarily activates obliques, quadratus lumborum, and hip abductors on the supporting side
- Isometric hold develops lateral core stability and resistance to spinal lateral flexion
- Can be progressed through duration increases, reduced base of support, or added movements
- Requires maintaining neutral spine alignment while resisting gravity's lateral pull
- Essential exercise for developing functional core strength in the frontal plane

## Deep Dive

### Exercise Mechanics and Muscle Activation
The side plank creates an anti-lateral flexion challenge, forcing the core musculature to work isometrically [[B19. Isometrics]] to maintain spinal alignment. The exercise primarily targets the lateral stabilization system, including the obliques, quadratus lumborum, and hip abductors. This positioning creates a unique training stimulus compared to sagittal plane exercises like the standard plank [[EXL-84 Plank (Standard & Variations)]].

The supporting shoulder experiences significant stabilization demands, requiring coordinated activation of the deltoids, rotator cuff, and serratus anterior. The exercise also challenges proprioception and balance, making it valuable for [[L4. Core Stability Training]] programs.

### Target Muscle Groups
**Primary Stabilizers:**
- Obliques (Internal & External - especially bottom side)
- Quadratus Lumborum (bottom side) 
- Gluteus Medius/Minimus (bottom leg)

**Secondary Stabilizers:**
- Transverse Abdominis
- Rectus Abdominis
- Erector Spinae
- Latissimus Dorsi
- Deltoids (supporting shoulder)
- Adductors (top leg)

### Proper Form and Execution
Following [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]] principles:

**Setup:**
- Lie on side with legs straight and stacked
- Place bottom forearm on floor, elbow directly under shoulder
- Position top hand on hip or extend toward ceiling
- Engage core before lifting

**Execution:**
- Lift hips off floor until body forms straight line from head to feet
- Maintain neutral spine without allowing hips to sag or pike
- Keep head and neck aligned with spine
- Hold position while breathing steadily
- Complete full duration before switching sides

**Common Form Errors:**
- Hip sagging toward floor
- Hip rotation forward or backward
- Shoulder elevation toward ear
- Neck hyperextension or flexion
- Holding breath during the hold

## Research Evidence

Due to the specific nature of side plank research, most core stability studies examine planking variations as part of broader core training protocols rather than isolating the side plank specifically. The available evidence supports isometric training benefits for core stability and functional movement patterns.

## Practical Applications

### Programming Guidelines
- **Beginner:** 15-30 second holds, 2-3 sets per side
- **Intermediate:** 30-60 second holds, 2-4 sets per side  
- **Advanced:** 60+ second holds or advanced variations, 3-4 sets per side
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times per week as part of [[L4. Core Stability Training]]

### Progression Strategies
1. **Duration Progression:** Increase hold time by 5-10 seconds weekly
2. **Stability Reduction:** Stack feet precisely, lift top leg
3. **Dynamic Elements:** Add arm reaches, hip dips, or rotations
4. **Lever Length:** Progress to straight-arm side plank
5. **External Load:** Add weight to top hip or hold dumbbells
6. **Advanced Variations:** Star side plank, side plank with leg lifts

### Regression Options
- Knee side plank (support on bottom knee at 90 degrees)
- Shorter hold durations (10-15 seconds)
- Wall-supported side plank for beginners
- Focus on basic plank mastery first [[EXL-84 Plank (Standard & Variations)]]

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Elbow placement too far from shoulder** - Creates unstable base and shoulder stress
⚠️ **Allowing hips to sag** - Reduces core activation and increases spinal stress
⚠️ **Rotating hips forward or backward** - Compromises exercise specificity
⚠️ **Holding breath** - Limits exercise duration and functional carryover
⚠️ **Ignoring pain signals** - Shoulder or back discomfort should prompt form assessment

## Contraindications and Safety

### Medical Considerations
Following [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]]:
- Acute shoulder impingement or injury (supporting side)
- Recent abdominal or back surgery
- Severe osteoporosis in spine
- Acute low back pain episodes

### Safety Guidelines
- Ensure proper warm-up before attempting holds
- Progress duration gradually to avoid overuse
- Stop immediately if sharp pain occurs
- Modify based on individual limitations and pain responses

## Assessment and Metrics

Using [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]] principles:
- **Duration:** Maximum hold time with proper form
- **Form Quality:** Ability to maintain straight body line
- **Progression:** Successful advancement through variations
- **Bilateral Comparison:** Time differences between left and right sides
- **Functional Integration:** Performance in dynamic movements requiring lateral stability
