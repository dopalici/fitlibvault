### C28-NUTRITION-STRENGTH-POWER
**ID:** C28-NUTRITION-STRENGTH-POWER
**Created:** 2025-04-26 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
Strength and power athletes need 1.6-2.2g/kg protein daily, 3-6g/kg carbohydrates, adequate calories to fuel intense training, and strategic supplementation with creatine and caffeine for optimal performance and adaptation.

## Key Points
- Maintain adequate energy intake to fuel high-intensity training and prevent performance decline
- Consume 1.6-2.2g/kg protein daily, distributed evenly across 4-5 meals for optimal muscle protein synthesis
- Include 3-6g/kg carbohydrates daily to fuel anaerobic training and maintain muscle glycogen stores
- Supplement with creatine monohydrate (3-5g/day) and caffeine (3-6mg/kg pre-training) for proven performance benefits
- Time protein and carbohydrate intake around training sessions to maximize recovery and adaptation

## Deep Dive

Nutrition strategies for strength and power athletes (powerlifters, weightlifters, sprinters, throwers, field athletes) aim to optimize high-intensity training performance, maximize adaptations in strength, power, and hypertrophy, facilitate recovery, and achieve optimal body composition for performance through targeted [[B. Resistance Training]] and explosive movement patterns [[X11. Sprinting]].

### Energy Requirements and Availability

Adequate energy intake must meet the demands of intense training while supporting recovery and adaptation through proper [[A1. Energy Balance]]. Caloric needs depend heavily on training volume, intensity, body size, and specific body composition goals - whether maintaining weight, pursuing muscle gain [[C25. Nutrition for Muscle Gain (Hypertrophy)]], or achieving fat loss [[C26. Nutrition for Fat Loss]].

Maintaining adequate Energy Availability is critical to prevent negative hormonal consequences and performance decrements associated with [[I3. Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S)]]. This is particularly important for athletes in weight-class sports who may be tempted to severely restrict calories.

### Protein Requirements and Distribution

Sufficient protein intake is paramount for repairing exercise-induced muscle damage and synthesizing new muscle tissue. Research supports consuming **1.6-2.2 g/kg body weight per day** as outlined in [[B2. Protein Requirements for Muscle Growth]].

Optimal protein distribution involves consuming 0.3-0.5 g/kg per meal across 4-5 eating occasions throughout the day to maximize muscle protein synthesis rates. This approach to [[C3. Nutrient Timing]] ensures consistent amino acid availability for ongoing muscle repair and growth processes.

### Carbohydrate Fueling Strategy

Carbohydrates serve as the primary fuel for anaerobic glycolysis, the dominant [[A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]] during high-intensity strength and power training sessions. Adequate carbohydrate intake ensures sufficient muscle glycogen stores to fuel performance and maintain training quality across multiple sessions.

Intake typically ranges from **3-6+ g/kg/day** depending on training volume and overall caloric needs, as detailed in [[C2. Macronutrient Distribution]]. Higher intakes may be necessary during particularly intense training phases or for larger athletes with greater energy demands.

### Strategic Nutrient Timing

**Peri-Workout Nutrition:** Consuming carbohydrates and protein around training sessions supports both performance and recovery [[C3. Nutrient Timing]]. Pre-workout intake (1-4 hours prior) ensures fuel availability, while post-workout consumption (within 2 hours) facilitates recovery and muscle protein synthesis.

**Daily Distribution:** Consistent protein intake throughout the day proves more effective than consuming large amounts in fewer meals, supporting continuous muscle protein synthesis rates.

## Research Evidence

**Helms, E.R., et al. (2014).** Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: nutrition and supplementation. *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, 11, 20. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20
- Key finding: Protein intake of 1.6-2.2g/kg body weight optimizes muscle protein synthesis and body composition in strength athletes
- Study type: Systematic review
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive analysis of multiple studies)

**Kerksick, C.M., et al. (2018).** International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: nutrient timing. *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, 15, 38. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-018-0243-y
- Key finding: Protein distribution across 4-5 meals maximizes muscle protein synthesis compared to fewer, larger meals
- Study type: Position stand/Meta-analysis
- Evidence quality: High (expert consensus based on extensive research)

**Kreider, R.B., et al. (2017).** International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation in exercise, sport, and medicine. *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, 14, 18. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z
- Key finding: Creatine monohydrate supplementation (3-5g/day) consistently improves high-intensity exercise performance and training adaptations
- Study type: Position stand/Meta-analysis
- Evidence quality: High (extensive safety and efficacy data)

**Thomas, D.T., et al. ## Practical Applications

**Daily Meal Planning:**
- 180lb (82kg) athlete needs: 131-180g protein, 246-492g carbohydrates daily
- Distribute protein across 4-5 meals: ~25-36g per meal
- Include carbohydrate-rich foods at each meal: rice, oats, fruits, vegetables

**Pre-Training (1-4 hours before):**
- 30-60g carbohydrates from easily digestible sources
- 15-25g protein if meal timing allows
- Adequate hydration throughout the day [[C4. Hydration]]

**Post-Training (within 2 hours):**
- 30-40g high-quality protein
- 1-1.5g/kg carbohydrates to replenish glycogen
- Continue hydration to replace training losses

**Evidence-Based Supplementation:**
- Creatine monohydrate: 3-5g daily, timing irrelevant [[C21. Evidence-Based Supplements (Group A)]]
- Caffeine: 3-6mg/kg body weight 30-60 minutes pre-training
- Beta-alanine: 3-5g daily for repeated high-intensity efforts
- Protein powder: Convenient tool to meet daily protein targets

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Inadequate total energy intake** leading to poor recovery, strength plateaus, and increased injury risk

⚠️ **Uneven protein distribution** such as consuming most protein at dinner instead of spreading throughout the day

⚠️ **Neglecting carbohydrates** in favor of excessive protein, leading to poor training quality and glycogen depletion

⚠️ **Poor hydration practices** including relying on thirst as an indicator or inadequate pre-training hydration

⚠️ **Supplement over-reliance** while ignoring fundamental nutrition principles and whole food intake
