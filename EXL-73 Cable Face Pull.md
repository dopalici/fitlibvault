### **EXL-73: Cable Face Pull**

## Quick Takeaway
Cable face pulls are a high-pulley cable exercise that targets rear deltoids and rotator cuff muscles through horizontal pulling with external rotation, making them essential for shoulder health and posture correction.

## Key Points
- Targets posterior deltoids, rotator cuff external rotators, and upper back scapular retractors
- Requires simultaneous horizontal pulling and external shoulder rotation for proper execution
- External rotation component differentiates it from standard rowing movements
- Can be performed from various stances (standing, kneeling, half-kneeling) for stability variations
- Emphasizes movement quality and control over heavy resistance

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable face pull combines two primary movement patterns: horizontal pulling and external rotation of the shoulders. The exercise begins with the arms extended forward, holding a rope attachment from a high pulley. The movement involves retracting the scapulae while pulling the rope toward the face, with simultaneous external rotation of the shoulders so the knuckles point toward the ceiling at the end position.

### Primary Target Muscles
**Primary Movers:** Posterior deltoid, infraspinatus, teres minor (external rotators), middle and lower trapezius, and rhomboids work as the main drivers of the movement.

**Secondary Contributors:** The medial deltoid assists with arm positioning, while the biceps provide minor assistance during the pulling phase.

**Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]], erector spinae, and the entire rotator cuff complex maintain stability throughout the movement.

### Execution Technique
Proper setup involves attaching a rope handle to a high pulley cable [[B10. Machine Training]] and grasping the rope ends with an overhand or neutral grip. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, maintaining a slight knee bend and engaged core. Alternative stances include kneeling or half-kneeling positions for enhanced stability.

The movement begins by initiating scapular retraction, then pulling the rope toward face level while simultaneously externally rotating the shoulders. The finish position resembles a "double bicep pose" with elbows flared outward and knuckles pointing toward the ceiling. Control the return to starting position under tension.

### Progression and Regression Strategies
**Progression paths** include increasing load through the weight stack [[B1. Progressive Overload]], increasing training volume [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]], implementing tempo variations with pauses at peak contraction, or utilizing slow eccentric phases [[B20. Eccentric Training]].

**Regression options** involve decreasing resistance, using resistance bands for face pulls [[B11. Resistance Bands]], or substituting with simpler rear deltoid exercises like the [[EXL-26 Dumbbell Bent-Over Rear Delt Raise]] or [[EXL-55 Rear Delt Fly  Reverse Pec Deck (Machine)]].

## Research Evidence

While specific research on cable face pulls is limited, the movement patterns and muscle groups involved are well-supported by shoulder rehabilitation and strengthening literature. The external rotation component and posterior deltoid strengthening are fundamental aspects of shoulder health programs.

## Practical Applications

**Programming Guidelines:**
- Sets: 2-4 sets
- Repetitions: 12-20 reps for muscle endurance and shoulder health
- Frequency: 2-3 times per week as part of upper body or shoulder-focused sessions
- Load: Start with light resistance, focusing on movement quality

**Integration Strategies:**
- Use as a warm-up exercise before pressing movements
- Include in shoulder rehabilitation protocols
- Incorporate into upper body pulling sessions
- Utilize for posture correction programs

**Form Assessment Checklist:**
- Rope pulled to face level (not chest level)
- Clear external rotation with knuckles pointing up
- Scapular retraction without excessive shoulder elevation
- Controlled eccentric phase
- Minimal momentum or body lean

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Pulling too low:** Drawing the rope toward the chest like a row eliminates the external rotation component and reduces rear deltoid activation

⚠️ **Insufficient external rotation:** Keeping elbows low without rotating the shoulders externally misses the primary benefit for rotator cuff strengthening

⚠️ **Using excessive momentum:** Leaning back or using body weight reduces muscle activation and control

⚠️ **Forward head posture:** Allowing the head to jut forward during the pull can stress the cervical spine
