### Testing Reliability and Validity

## Quick Takeaway
Reliability measures test consistency across repeated measurements, while validity ensures tests actually measure what they claim to measure - both are essential for trustworthy performance assessments.

## Key Points
- Reliability refers to consistency of test results when repeated under similar conditions
- Validity indicates whether a test accurately measures the intended construct or ability
- Three main validity types: construct (measures the concept), criterion (predicts outcomes), and content (covers all relevant aspects)
- Measurement error includes both systematic bias and random variability that affects test interpretation
- Standardized protocols and quality control procedures are essential for maintaining test reliability and validity over time

## Deep Dive

### Understanding Reliability
Reliability quantifies the consistency and reproducibility of test measurements. A reliable test produces similar results when administered repeatedly under consistent conditions. Key reliability types include:

**Test-Retest Reliability:** Consistency of results when the same test is administered to the same individuals on different occasions. This is particularly important for [[A50. Performance Testing and Evaluation]] protocols where athletes may be tested multiple times throughout a training cycle.

**Inter-Rater Reliability:** Agreement between different evaluators scoring the same performance. Critical when multiple assessors are involved in [[A31. Force-Velocity Profiling]] or subjective movement assessments.

**Internal Consistency:** The degree to which test items measure the same construct, typically assessed using Cronbach's alpha coefficient.

### Validity Framework
Validity ensures that tests measure what they purport to measure, making it arguably more important than reliability for meaningful assessments.

**Construct Validity:** Whether the test measures the theoretical construct it claims to assess. For example, does a vertical jump test truly reflect lower body power as intended in [[A32. Power Profiling and Testing]]?

**Criterion Validity:** How well test results correlate with established gold standard measures or predict future performance outcomes. This includes both concurrent validity (correlation with existing measures) and predictive validity (ability to forecast future performance).

**Content Validity:** Whether the test adequately covers all aspects of the construct being measured, ensuring comprehensive assessment without important gaps.

### Measurement Error and Quality Control
All measurements contain error that affects interpretation of results and progress tracking in [[Q4. Interpreting Biometric & Progress Data]].

**Systematic Error (Bias):** Consistent deviation from true values in one direction, often caused by equipment calibration issues or procedural inconsistencies.

**Random Error:** Unpredictable variability in measurements due to factors like environmental conditions, tester variability, or participant state fluctuations.

Quality control procedures should include regular equipment calibration, standardized testing protocols, and ongoing monitoring to detect measurement drift over time.

## Practical Applications

**Establishing Reliability Standards:**
- Aim for intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) > 0.90 for individual assessments
- Calculate typical error as percentage of mean to determine meaningful change thresholds
- Conduct pilot testing with 15-20 participants over 2-3 sessions before implementing new protocols

**Validity Assessment Protocol:**
- Compare new tests against established gold standards in your population
- Ensure test conditions match intended use scenarios
- Validate tests separately for different populations (athletes vs. general population)

**Quality Control Implementation:**
- Calibrate equipment before each testing session
- Use standardized verbal instructions and demonstration procedures
- Track test-retest reliability monthly with subset of participants
- Establish control charts to monitor measurement drift over time

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Assuming face validity equals construct validity** - tests that look appropriate may not actually measure the intended construct

⚠️ **Ignoring population-specific validation** - reliability and validity established in one group may not transfer to different populations

⚠️ **Focusing only on reliability while neglecting validity** - consistent measurements are meaningless if they don't measure what matters

⚠️ **Using inappropriate statistical methods** - applying Pearson correlations instead of ICC for reliability assessment

⚠️ **Failing to account for learning effects** - not providing adequate familiarization sessions before reliability testing
