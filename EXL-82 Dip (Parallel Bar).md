### **EXL-82: Dip (Parallel Bar)**

**ID:** EXL-82-BW-DIP **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
Parallel bar dips are an intermediate-to-advanced bodyweight exercise that primarily targets triceps, chest, and front shoulders through a compound pressing movement between parallel bars.

## Key Points
- Primary movement pattern is vertical pressing using bodyweight resistance
- Targets triceps brachii as primary mover, with significant chest and anterior deltoid involvement
- Requires adequate shoulder mobility and upper body strength for safe execution
- Body position (upright vs. forward lean) shifts emphasis between triceps and chest
- Progression involves adding external load, tempo changes, or advanced variations like ring dips

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics and Muscle Activation
The parallel bar dip is a closed-chain, compound exercise that involves shoulder adduction and elbow extension against bodyweight resistance. The exercise primarily recruits the triceps brachii, pectoralis major (sternal portion), and anterior deltoid as prime movers, while secondary muscles including serratus anterior, pectoralis minor, and core stabilizers provide support and stability.

Body positioning significantly affects muscle emphasis. An upright torso position increases triceps activation, while a forward-leaning posture shifts more work to the pectoralis major. The exercise requires coordinated movement at both shoulder and elbow joints, making it an effective compound movement for upper body development.

### Proper Execution Technique
**Setup:** Grasp parallel bars with a neutral grip, hands positioned slightly wider than shoulder-width. Support bodyweight with arms fully extended (maintaining slight elbow bend to protect joints). Keep shoulders depressed and away from ears. Torso position can be upright for triceps emphasis or slightly forward-leaning for chest emphasis.

**Execution:** Initiate the movement by slowly lowering the body through controlled elbow flexion. Maintain elbows relatively close to the torso to optimize muscle recruitment and joint safety. Descend until upper arms reach approximately parallel to the floor or until a comfortable stretch is felt across the chest and shoulders. Press forcefully back to the starting position through coordinated elbow extension.

**Breathing Pattern:** Inhale during the eccentric (lowering) phase, exhale during the concentric (pressing) phase.

### Progression and Regression Strategies
**Progressions:**
- Increase training volume through additional repetitions or sets [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Add external resistance via weight belts or weighted vests [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Implement tempo variations such as slow eccentrics [[B20. Eccentric Training]] or paused repetitions
- Progress to ring dips for increased instability demands [[X9. Gymnastics  Calisthenics]]

**Regressions:**
- Utilize assisted dip machines [[EXL-65 Assisted Pull-upDip Machine]] or resistance band assistance [[B11. Resistance Bands]]
- Practice negative-only repetitions focusing on controlled eccentric movement
- Perform bench dips with feet supported
- Build foundational strength through push-ups [[EXL-83 Push-up (Standard & Variations)]] and triceps-specific exercises [[EXL-67 Cable Triceps Pushdown (RopeBar)]]

## Practical Applications
- **Beginner Implementation:** Start with assisted variations using 20-40% body weight assistance, performing 3 sets of 5-8 repetitions
- **Intermediate Training:** Aim for 3-4 sets of 8-12 bodyweight repetitions with controlled 2-3 second eccentric phases
- **Advanced Progression:** Add 10-25% bodyweight as external load, or progress to single-arm variations and ring dips
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times per week with at least 48 hours recovery between sessions targeting the same muscle groups

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Excessive shoulder elevation:** Allowing shoulders to rise toward ears reduces stability and increases injury risk
⚠️ **Inadequate range of motion:** Stopping short of parallel arm position limits muscle development and mobility benefits
⚠️ **Excessive elbow flare:** Wide elbow positioning increases shoulder stress and reduces triceps activation
⚠️ **Using momentum:** Bouncing at the bottom or swinging legs reduces exercise effectiveness and control
