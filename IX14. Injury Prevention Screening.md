### IX14. Injury Prevention Screening
**ID:** IX14-INJURY-PREVENTION-SCREENING
**Created:** 2025-06-05 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
Injury prevention screening systematically identifies movement dysfunctions, physical limitations, and risk factors before injuries occur, allowing for targeted interventions to reduce injury risk.

## Key Points
- Movement quality assessment reveals dysfunctional patterns that predispose to injury
- Physical performance testing identifies strength, flexibility, and balance deficits
- Risk factor identification includes both intrinsic factors (previous injuries, biomechanics) and extrinsic factors (training load, equipment)
- Sport-specific screening protocols target common injury patterns for each activity
- Screening results guide targeted corrective interventions and training modifications

## Deep Dive

### Movement Quality Assessment
Comprehensive evaluation of fundamental movement patterns forms the foundation of injury prevention screening. Using standardized tools like [[A17. Movement Screening]] and [[A28. Advanced Movement Assessment Methodologies]], practitioners assess how individuals perform basic movements such as squatting, lunging, and overhead reaching. These assessments reveal compensatory patterns, asymmetries, and dysfunctions that may predispose to injury.

Movement screening identifies specific limitations in mobility, stability, and motor control that can be addressed through targeted interventions. The assessment process evaluates movement quality rather than quantity, focusing on how well someone moves rather than how much they can lift or how fast they can run.

### Physical Performance Testing
Systematic evaluation of physical capabilities provides objective data about an individual's readiness for activity demands. This includes assessment of strength, power, flexibility, and balance using protocols from [[A32. Power Profiling and Testing]] and [[L3. Balance and Coordination Training]]. Testing reveals specific physical deficits that may increase injury risk when activity demands exceed current capabilities.

Performance testing should be sport-specific when possible, evaluating the physical qualities most relevant to the individual's activities. For example, runners might undergo different assessments than team sport athletes, reflecting the distinct demands of each activity type.

### Risk Factor Identification
Comprehensive screening evaluates both intrinsic and extrinsic risk factors following principles from [[IX-2. Injury Prevention Strategies]]. Intrinsic factors include previous injury history, biomechanical limitations, muscle imbalances, and individual characteristics. Extrinsic factors encompass training variables, equipment, environmental conditions, and psychosocial elements.

Previous injury history represents one of the strongest predictors of future injury, making thorough injury assessment a critical component of screening. Understanding how previous injuries may have altered movement patterns or created compensations helps guide intervention strategies.

### Sport-Specific Screening Protocols
Different activities present distinct injury patterns and risk factors, requiring tailored screening approaches. Activities like [[X1. Running (Track, Road, Trail)]] emphasize lower extremity function and repetitive stress tolerance, while [[X4. Team Sports - General Considerations]] require assessment of change-of-direction capabilities, contact tolerance, and multidirectional movement quality.

Sport-specific screening considers the unique demands, common injury patterns, and performance requirements of each activity. This targeted approach ensures screening protocols are relevant and actionable for the individual's specific needs.

### Intervention Strategy Development
Screening results guide the development of targeted interventions to address identified deficits. This may include corrective exercises, [[L2. Mobility and Flexibility Development]] programs, [[L4. Core Stability Training]], or modifications to training load and technique. The intervention strategy should be specific to the identified risk factors and practical for the individual to implement.

Effective intervention requires ongoing monitoring and adjustment based on progress and changing needs. Regular re-screening helps track improvement and identify new risk factors as they emerge.

## Research Evidence

**Whittaker et al. (2023).** Evidence-Based Hamstring Injury Prevention and Risk Factor Management: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials. *The American Journal of Sports Medicine*, 51(8), 2128-2151. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465221083998
- Key finding: Eccentric strengthening programs reduced hamstring injury risk by 51% when implemented based on screening results
- Study type: Meta-analysis
- Evidence quality: High (comprehensive analysis of RCTs with low heterogeneity)

**Schneider et al. (2023).** Prevention strategies and modifiable risk factors for sport-related concussions and head impacts: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 57(12), 749-761. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106656
- Key finding: Multi-component prevention programs including screening and education reduced concussion risk by 29%
- Study type: Meta-analysis
- Evidence quality: High (rigorous methodology with comprehensive search strategy)

**Winters et al. (2024).** Ankle Injury Prevention Programs for Youth Sports: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. *Sports Health*, 16(3), 412-425. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/19417381241231588
- Key finding: Screening-based prevention programs reduced ankle injury incidence by 42% in youth athletes
- Study type: Meta-analysis
- Evidence quality: Medium (moderate quality studies with some heterogeneity in protocols)

## Practical Applications

**Initial Screening Protocol:**
1. Complete movement assessment using standardized tools
2. Evaluate strength, flexibility, and balance capabilities
3. Review injury history and training background
4. Assess sport-specific movement patterns
5. Identify priority risk factors for intervention

**Intervention Development:**
- Address mobility limitations before stability training
- Progress from basic movement patterns to sport-specific demands
- Include both corrective exercises and performance enhancement
- Establish clear progression criteria and timelines
- Plan regular re-assessment intervals

**Implementation Timeline:**
- Week 1-2: Complete comprehensive screening
- Week 3-4: Begin targeted interventions for highest priority deficits
- Week 6-8: Re-assess progress and adjust program
- Ongoing: Monitor for new risk factors and seasonal demands

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Focusing only on obvious deficits while missing subtle movement compensations
⚠️ Using generic screening protocols instead of sport-specific assessments
⚠️ Failing to address underlying mobility restrictions before stability training
⚠️ Not considering psychological and social factors that influence injury risk
⚠️ Implementing interventions without establishing baseline measurements for progress tracking
