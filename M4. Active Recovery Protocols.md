### M4. Active Recovery Protocols
**ID:** M4-ACTIVE-RECOVERY **Created:** 2025-06-05

Active recovery protocols involve low-intensity movement and exercise designed to promote blood flow, enhance metabolite clearance, and facilitate the recovery process between training sessions or during rest days. These protocols offer both physiological and psychological benefits compared to complete passive rest, though effects are typically modest and should complement fundamental recovery strategies.

**Core Components:**
- **Low-Intensity Aerobic Exercise:** Walking, light cycling, swimming at 30-50% VO2 max to promote blood flow without inducing additional training stress ([[E2. Steady-State Cardio (LISS - Low-Intensity Steady State)]], [[A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]])
- **Dynamic Movement Patterns:** Gentle mobility work, light bodyweight movements, and movement preparation exercises to maintain tissue quality ([[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]], [[L2. Mobility and Flexibility Development]])
- **Parasympathetic Activation:** Yoga, tai chi, and breathing exercises that shift the autonomic nervous system toward recovery ([[M3. Stress Management and Performance]], [[L3. Balance and Coordination Training]])
- **Blood Flow Enhancement:** Light resistance exercises with minimal load to promote circulation without causing muscle damage ([[B7. Bodyweight Training]], [[M1. Recovery Modalities Evidence and Application]])
- **Session Structure and Timing:** Strategic placement within training programs and appropriate duration (20-45 minutes) to maximize benefits ([[A25. Principle of Periodization]], [[A26. Principle of Recovery]])
