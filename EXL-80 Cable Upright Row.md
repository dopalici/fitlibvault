### **EXL-80: Cable Upright Row**

**ID:** EXL-80-CEX-UPRIGHTROW **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
Cable upright rows target the side deltoids and upper traps but carry significant shoulder impingement risk - use wider grip and stop at chest height for safer execution.

## Key Points
- Primarily targets medial deltoids and upper trapezius through vertical pulling motion
- High risk exercise for shoulder impingement due to combined abduction and internal rotation
- Cable system provides constant tension throughout range of motion
- Leading with elbows rather than hands ensures proper muscle activation
- Wider grip and reduced range of motion significantly decrease injury risk

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable upright row involves pulling a bar attachment vertically from a low pulley position toward the chin. The exercise combines shoulder abduction (lifting arms to sides) with elbow flexion, creating a compound movement pattern that primarily targets the medial deltoids and upper trapezius.

The cable system provides several advantages over free weight variations, including constant tension throughout the range of motion and the ability to adjust resistance incrementally. However, the fundamental biomechanical concerns remain the same regardless of equipment used.

### Target Muscle Activation
**Primary Movers:** Medial deltoid and upper trapezius handle the majority of the workload, with the medial deltoid responsible for shoulder abduction and the upper trapezius elevating the shoulder girdle.

**Secondary Movers:** Anterior deltoid assists with shoulder flexion, while the supraspinatus initiates abduction. The biceps brachii, brachialis, and brachioradialis contribute to elbow flexion, and the levator scapulae assists with scapular elevation.

**Stabilizers:** [[L4. Core Stability Training]] muscles maintain upright posture, while the rotator cuff complex stabilizes the glenohumeral joint throughout the movement.

### Shoulder Impingement Concerns
The upright row places the shoulder in a compromised position combining abduction and internal rotation, which narrows the subacromial space and can compress soft tissues including the supraspinatus tendon and subacromial bursa. This position is particularly problematic for individuals with pre-existing shoulder impingement syndrome or rotator cuff pathology.

### Form Optimization for Safety
Proper execution requires standing close to the cable machine with core engaged [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]. The grip should be overhand with hands positioned wider than traditionally taught - approximately shoulder-width apart rather than close together. This wider grip reduces internal rotation and decreases impingement risk.

The pulling motion should be initiated by lifting the elbows, not the hands, with the bar staying close to the body throughout. The movement should stop when elbows reach shoulder height or the bar reaches mid-chest level, avoiding the traditional "pull to chin" instruction that maximizes impingement risk.

## Research Evidence

**Note:** Limited high-quality research exists specifically on cable upright rows due to the exercise's controversial nature and injury risk profile. Most shoulder impingement research focuses on overhead movements and rehabilitation protocols rather than specific exercises that may contribute to the condition.

## Practical Applications

### Programming Guidelines
- **Beginner:** Avoid this exercise entirely; focus on [[EXL-24 Dumbbell Lateral Raise]] and [[EXL-41 Dumbbell Shrug]] instead
- **Intermediate:** 3 sets of 12-15 reps with moderate weight, emphasizing form over load
- **Advanced:** Can incorporate as accessory movement with strict technique monitoring

### Load Progression
Follow standard [[B1. Progressive Overload]] principles but prioritize technique over weight increases. Begin with light resistance to establish proper movement pattern, then progress load gradually while maintaining pain-free range of motion.

### Alternative Exercises
Consider these safer alternatives for similar muscle activation:
- [[EXL-71 Cable Lateral Raise]] for medial deltoid development
- [[EXL-53 Lateral Raise (Machine)]] for controlled resistance
- Face pulls for posterior deltoid and upper trap activation
- [[EXL-16 Barbell Shrug]] for isolated upper trapezius work

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Narrow grip positioning** - Increases internal rotation and impingement risk
⚠️ **Pulling to chin height** - Maximizes shoulder compression in vulnerable position  
⚠️ **Leading with hands instead of elbows** - Reduces target muscle activation
⚠️ **Using excessive momentum** - Compromises form and increases injury risk
⚠️ **Ignoring shoulder discomfort** - Early warning sign of impingement development
