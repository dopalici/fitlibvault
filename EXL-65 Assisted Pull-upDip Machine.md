### **EXL-65: Assisted Pull-up/Dip Machine**

**ID:** EXL-65-MEX-ASSISTPULLDIP **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
A counterweight-assisted machine that reduces effective bodyweight during pull-ups and dips, providing a progressive pathway from assisted to unassisted bodyweight movements.

## Key Points
- Provides adjustable counterweight assistance to make pull-ups and dips accessible to beginners
- Allows progressive reduction of assistance as strength improves following [[B1. Progressive Overload]] principles
- Targets the same muscle groups as unassisted versions but with reduced loading
- Enables full range of motion practice with proper form development
- Serves as an effective bridge to unassisted bodyweight exercises

## Deep Dive

### Machine Mechanics and Function
The assisted pull-up/dip machine uses a counterweight system connected to a knee or foot platform. The selected weight provides upward assistance, effectively reducing the amount of bodyweight lifted. This [[B10. Machine Training]] approach allows individuals to perform the movement pattern with proper form while building the necessary strength to progress toward unassisted variations.

### Target Musculature

**Pull-up Assist Mode:**
- **Primary Movers:** Latissimus dorsi, teres major, biceps brachii
- **Secondary:** Rhomboids, middle trapezius, posterior deltoid
- **Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]], scapular stabilizers

**Dip Assist Mode:**
- **Primary Movers:** Pectoralis major (lower fibers), triceps brachii, anterior deltoid
- **Secondary:** Serratus anterior, lower trapezius
- **Stabilizers:** Core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]]

### Progressive Training Approach
The machine excels at implementing [[B1. Progressive Overload]] through systematic assistance reduction. As strength improves, users decrease counterweight assistance while maintaining consistent [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]] patterns. This approach allows for measurable progression tracking and prevents the common plateau experienced when attempting unassisted movements too early.

## Practical Applications

### Setup and Execution
1. **Weight Selection:** Start with assistance equal to 50-70% of bodyweight for beginners
2. **Platform Positioning:** Secure knees or feet firmly on assistance pad
3. **Grip Selection:** Use appropriate grip width and style for training goals
4. **Range of Motion:** Perform full range movements from complete extension to full contraction

### Progression Protocol
- Begin with 8-12 repetitions at comfortable assistance level
- Reduce assistance by 5-10 pounds when completing 3 sets of 12 with good form
- Track assistance weight and repetitions completed for [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]]
- Transition to unassisted attempts when using minimal assistance (10-20 pounds)

### Integration with Training Programs
Incorporate 2-3 times per week with adequate recovery between sessions. Combine with complementary exercises like lat pulldowns [[EXL-56 Lat Pulldown (Machine - Wide Grip)]] for pull-up development or triceps work for dip progression.

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Over-reliance on assistance:** Using too much counterweight prevents strength development
⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion:** Stopping short of full extension or complete pull-up reduces training effectiveness
⚠️ **Poor scapular control:** Allowing shoulders to elevate or protract during movement
⚠️ **Rapid assistance reduction:** Decreasing counterweight too quickly compromises form and increases injury risk
⚠️ **Neglecting eccentric phase:** Rushing the lowering portion reduces training stimulus
