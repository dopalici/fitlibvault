### Mediterranean Diet
**ID:** C10-MEDITERRANEAN-DIET **Created:** 2025-04-26 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The Mediterranean Diet is a research-backed eating pattern emphasizing olive oil, fish, vegetables, and whole grains that significantly reduces cardiovascular disease risk and supports overall health and longevity.

## Key Points
- Prioritize olive oil as your main cooking fat, eat fish twice weekly, and fill half your plate with vegetables and fruits
- Choose whole grains over refined grains and include legumes, nuts, and seeds daily
- Limit red meat to 2-3 times per month and minimize processed foods and added sugars
- This pattern reduces heart disease risk by 30% and supports cognitive health and longevity
- Provides excellent nutrition for active individuals with ample carbs for fuel and anti-inflammatory compounds for recovery

## Deep Dive

The Mediterranean Diet (MedDiet) reflects the traditional dietary patterns of populations in Mediterranean regions (e.g., Greece, Southern Italy, Spain). It is not a single prescribed diet but rather a pattern characterized by high consumption of plant-based foods and healthy fats. It is one of the most researched dietary patterns and consistently linked to significant health benefits, particularly cardiovascular health.

### Core Components

**High Intake Foods (Daily):**
- Fruits and Vegetables (diverse, abundant - aim for 5-9 servings daily)
- Whole Grains (breads, pasta, rice - 3-6 servings daily)
- Legumes (beans, lentils, peas - 3+ servings weekly)
- Nuts and Seeds (1 ounce daily)
- Olive Oil (especially Extra Virgin) as the principal source of added fat for balanced [[A3. Macronutrients]]
- Fish and Seafood (especially fatty fish rich in Omega-3s, consumed ≥2 times/week)

**Moderate Intake Foods (Weekly):**
- Poultry (2-3 servings)
- Dairy products (primarily yogurt and cheese - 1-2 servings daily)
- Eggs (2-4 per week)
- Wine (moderate consumption, typically with meals - optional, 1 glass for women, 2 for men)

**Low Intake Foods (Monthly or less):**
- Red Meat and Processed Meats (2-3 servings monthly)
- Sweets, sugary drinks, pastries
- Refined Grains
- Butter and other saturated fats

### Health Benefits and Mechanisms

**Cardiovascular Health:** The Mediterranean Diet shows strong evidence for reducing heart attack risk by 30%, stroke risk by 13%, and overall cardiovascular mortality. It improves blood pressure (average reduction of 2.35 mmHg systolic), lipid profiles (lowers LDL by 7.4 mg/dL, increases HDL), endothelial function, and reduces inflammation markers like hs-CRP through mechanisms related to [[C33. Anti-Inflammatory FoodsDiets]]. The landmark PREDIMED trial provided definitive evidence with over 7,400 participants.

**Metabolic Health:** Associated with 23% reduced risk of Type 2 Diabetes and improved glycemic control through high fiber intake and stable blood sugar patterns from whole food carbohydrates.

**Cognitive Function:** Linked to 35% slower rate of cognitive decline and 40% reduced risk of neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer's, likely due to anti-inflammatory compounds and omega-3 fatty acids.

**Weight Management:** Supports healthy weight management through high fiber (25-35g daily), nutrient density, and satiety value, though not primarily designed as a weight-loss diet.

## Research Evidence

**Primary Research:**
- Estruch, R., et al. (2018). Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease with a Mediterranean Diet Supplemented with Extra-Virgin Olive Oil or Nuts. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 378(25), e34. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1800389 (PREDIMED Study - RCT with 7,447 participants)

- Martínez-González, M.A., et al. (2019). The Mediterranean Diet and Cardiovascular Health. *Circulation Research*, 124(5), 779-798. DOI: 10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.118.313348

- Sofi, F., et al. (2010). Adherence to Mediterranean diet and health status: meta-analysis. *BMJ*, 341, c4671. DOI: 10.1136/bmj.c4671 (Meta-analysis of 1.5+ million participants)

**Evidence Quality:** High - Multiple large-scale RCTs and meta-analyses with consistent findings across populations.

## Practical Applications

**Daily Implementation:**
- Use 2-3 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil for cooking and dressing
- Eat 2-3 pieces of fruit and 3-4 servings of vegetables daily
- Choose whole grain bread, brown rice, or whole wheat pasta over refined versions
- Snack on 1 ounce (small handful) of unsalted nuts

**Weekly Planning:**
- Include fish/seafood in 2-3 meals (salmon, sardines, mackerel, tuna)
- Prepare 2-3 legume-based meals (lentil soup, chickpea salad, bean stew)
- Limit red meat to 1-2 small servings

**Fitness & Performance Considerations:**
- Provides 45-65% calories from carbohydrates to fuel training through [[C27. Nutrition for Endurance Performance (Carb Loading)]]
- Rich in essential [[C6. Micronutrients (Detailed Roles & Considerations)]] and [[C34. Antioxidants]] supporting recovery
- Anti-inflammatory components from [[C33. Anti-Inflammatory FoodsDiets]] may enhance recovery between sessions
- For maximal muscle gain goals [[C25. Nutrition for Muscle Gain (Hypertrophy)]], add extra fish, Greek yogurt, or legumes to meet higher protein targets

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Using regular olive oil instead of extra virgin** - Extra virgin contains more beneficial polyphenols and antioxidants

⚠️ **Focusing only on individual foods** - The pattern's benefits come from the overall combination, not single "superfoods"

⚠️ **Assuming all fats are equal** - The diet specifically emphasizes monounsaturated fats from olive oil and omega-3s from fish, not all fats indiscriminately

⚠️ **Ignoring portion control** - While emphasizing healthy foods, the diet still requires appropriate portions for weight management
