### IX-6. Exercise Contraindications
**ID:** IX-6-CONTRAINDICATIONS
**Created:** 2025-04-24 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Exercise contraindications are medical conditions where exercise risks outweigh benefits - absolute contraindications require medical clearance before any exercise, while relative contraindications allow modified exercise with proper supervision.

## Key Points
- Always screen clients before exercise to identify potential contraindications
- Absolute contraindications (like unstable angina, recent heart attack) require complete exercise cessation until medically cleared
- Relative contraindications (like controlled high blood pressure) may allow modified exercise with medical supervision
- When in doubt, refer to healthcare providers - fitness professionals must stay within their scope of practice
- Documentation of medical clearance protects both client safety and professional liability

## Deep Dive

Exercise contraindications represent clinical situations where the cardiovascular, metabolic, or physiological stress of exercise could potentially cause harm that outweighs therapeutic benefits. These are classified into two categories based on risk severity and the potential for safe exercise modification.

### Absolute Contraindications

These conditions require complete exercise cessation until the underlying condition is stabilized or treated by medical professionals:

**Acute Cardiovascular Events:**
- Recent significant change in resting ECG suggesting ischemia or recent myocardial infarction (within 2 days)
- Unstable angina pectoris
- Uncontrolled cardiac dysrhythmias causing symptoms or hemodynamic compromise
- Symptomatic severe aortic stenosis

**Acute Cardiac Conditions:**
- Uncontrolled symptomatic heart failure
- Acute myocarditis or pericarditis
- Suspected or known dissecting aneurysm

**Other Acute Conditions:**
- Acute pulmonary embolus or pulmonary infarction
- Acute systemic infection with fever, body aches, or swollen lymph glands

### Relative Contraindications

These conditions may allow for carefully modified exercise programs under appropriate medical supervision:

**Cardiovascular Conditions:**
- Left main coronary artery stenosis
- Moderate stenotic valvular heart disease
- Severe arterial hypertension (systolic BP >200 mmHg or diastolic BP >110 mmHg at rest)
- Tachyarrhythmias or bradyarrhythmias
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and other outflow tract obstructions
- High-degree atrioventricular block
- Ventricular aneurysm

**Metabolic and Systemic Conditions:**
- Electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia)
- Uncontrolled metabolic diseases (diabetes, thyrotoxicosis)
- Chronic infectious diseases (HIV)
- Mental or physical impairments affecting exercise capacity
- Advanced pregnancy (requires specialized guidance)

## Research Evidence

The contraindications framework is primarily established through consensus guidelines from major professional organizations:

American College of Sports Medicine. (2021). ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription (11th ed.). Wolters Kluwer. [Primary evidence-based source for contraindications classification]

Fletcher GF, et al. (2013). AHA/ACCF Scientific Statement: Exercise standards for testing and training: a scientific statement from the American Heart Association. Circulation, 128(8), 873-934. DOI: 10.1161/CIR.0b013e31829b5b44

Riebe D, et al. (Eds.). (2018). ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription (10th ed.). Wolters Kluwer. [Additional validation of contraindications framework]

## Practical Applications

**Pre-Exercise Screening Protocol:**
1. Use standardized screening tools (PAR-Q+, ACSM risk stratification)
2. Document all medical conditions, medications, and symptoms
3. Obtain written medical clearance for any identified contraindications
4. Establish emergency action plans for facilities

**For Relative Contraindications:**
- Require physician evaluation and exercise prescription guidelines
- Implement continuous monitoring (heart rate, blood pressure, symptoms)
- Start with low-intensity activities and progress gradually
- Maintain direct supervision by qualified exercise professionals
- Establish clear stop-exercise criteria

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Keep records of all screening results and medical clearances
- Document any modifications made to exercise programs
- Maintain emergency contact information and medical history updates

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Assuming stable conditions don't require ongoing monitoring** - conditions can change, requiring regular re-assessment

⚠️ **Exercising clients with absolute contraindications** - "light exercise won't hurt" thinking can be dangerous with acute cardiac conditions

⚠️ **Inadequate screening** - relying only on verbal questions without proper documentation or follow-up

⚠️ **Exceeding scope of practice** - making medical decisions about exercise safety instead of referring to healthcare providers

⚠️ **Ignoring medication effects** - not considering how medications might affect exercise response and contraindication status
