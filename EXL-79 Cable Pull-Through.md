### **EXL-79: Cable Pull-Through**

**ID:** EXL-79-CEX-PULLTHROUGH **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
The cable pull-through is a beginner-friendly hip hinge exercise that teaches proper hip movement patterns while strengthening the glutes and hamstrings with controlled resistance.

## Key Points
- Hip hinge movement pattern using cable resistance to target glutes and hamstrings
- Arms act as levers while hips drive the movement - not an upper body exercise
- Excellent teaching tool for learning proper hip hinge mechanics before progressing to deadlifts
- Provides constant tension throughout the movement due to cable resistance
- Requires neutral spine maintenance and strong glute contraction at the top position

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable pull-through is fundamentally a hip hinge exercise [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]] that uses a low pulley cable system [[B10. Machine Training]] to provide resistance. The movement involves standing facing away from the cable machine, reaching between the legs to grasp a rope attachment, then driving the hips forward to extend against the cable's resistance.

The exercise primarily targets the posterior chain - specifically the gluteus maximus and hamstrings - while requiring isometric stabilization from the erector spinae and core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]]. Unlike free weight hip hinge movements, the cable provides constant tension throughout the entire range of motion, making it particularly effective for teaching proper movement patterns.

### Target Muscles and Activation
**Primary Movers (Agonists):** Gluteus Maximus, Hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus)
**Secondary Movers (Synergists):** Adductor Magnus, Erector Spinae (isometric stabilization)
**Stabilizers:** Core musculature, feet and ankle stabilizers

The glutes are the primary driver of hip extension, while the hamstrings assist in the movement and control the eccentric (lowering) phase. The erector spinae work isometrically to maintain spinal neutrality throughout the movement.

### Setup and Execution Technique
**Setup:** Attach a rope handle to the lowest setting on a cable pulley system. Stand facing away from the machine with feet shoulder-width apart, straddling the cable. Reach down between your legs with both hands to grasp the rope ends using a neutral grip. Step forward until there is tension in the cable, maintaining a slight bend in the knees.

**Execution:** Begin the movement by hinging at the hips, pushing them back while maintaining a neutral spine. Allow the rope to travel back between your legs as you feel a stretch in your hamstrings. Initiate the return movement by driving your hips forward forcefully, extending through the hips and knees to return to the starting position. Squeeze your glutes hard at the top of the movement for maximum activation.

**Breathing Pattern:** Exhale during the forward hip drive (concentric phase), inhale during the backward hip hinge (eccentric phase).

### Form Cues and Common Errors
Critical form cues include maintaining a neutral spine throughout the movement, initiating the movement with the hips rather than the arms, and achieving full hip extension with strong glute contraction at the top. The arms should remain relatively straight and act merely as levers transferring force from the cable to the body.

Common errors include rounding the lower back, pulling with the arms instead of driving with the hips, squatting instead of hinging, and failing to achieve complete hip extension at the top of the movement. These errors reduce the exercise's effectiveness and may increase injury risk.

## Practical Applications

### Programming Guidelines
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 repetitions with light weight focusing on movement quality
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 10-12 repetitions with moderate weight
- **Advanced:** Can be used as warm-up, accessory work, or high-repetition finisher

### Progression Strategies
Following [[B1. Progressive Overload]] principles:
- Increase load (weight stack resistance)
- Increase volume through additional sets or repetitions [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Implement tempo variations (pause at top for glute squeeze, slow eccentric phase [[B20. Eccentric Training]])
- Progress to more complex hip hinge movements like kettlebell swings [[B9. Kettlebell Training]] or Romanian deadlifts [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]] / [[EXL-37 Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]]

### Regression Options
For those unable to perform the standard cable pull-through:
- Reduce the weight/resistance
- Practice bodyweight glute bridges [[B7. Bodyweight Training]]
- Use resistance band pull-throughs [[B11. Resistance Bands]] for lighter resistance
- Focus on bodyweight hip hinge pattern practice [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]]

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Arm-dominant movement:** Pulling with the arms instead of driving with the hips reduces glute activation and defeats the exercise's purpose

⚠️ **Spinal flexion:** Rounding the lower back increases injury risk and reduces power transfer through the kinetic chain

⚠️ **Incomplete hip extension:** Failing to fully extend the hips and squeeze the glutes at the top reduces the exercise's effectiveness

⚠️ **Squatting instead of hinging:** Excessive knee bend changes the movement pattern and reduces posterior chain emphasis

⚠️ **Poor cable positioning:** Standing too close or too far from the cable machine affects the resistance curve and movement quality

## Related Concepts

This exercise serves as an excellent introduction to hip hinge movement patterns [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]] and proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]]. It connects well with machine-based training principles [[B10. Machine Training]] and can be integrated into hypertrophy-focused programs [[B3. Hypertrophy Training Principles]].

The movement prepares individuals for more advanced exercises including the barbell hip thrust [[EXL-07 Barbell Hip Thrust]], Romanian deadlifts [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]], and kettlebell training [[B9. Kettlebell Training]]. It also supports core stability development [[L4. Core Stability Training]] and can be modified using alternative resistance methods [[B11. Resistance Bands]] or bodyweight variations [[B7. Bodyweight Training]].

Progress can be tracked using standard fitness assessment methods [[O2. Measuring Fitness Progress]], and the exercise should be evaluated for contraindications [[IX-6. Exercise Contraindications]] based on individual limitations and injury history.
