### **EXL-71: Cable Lateral Raise**

**ID:** EXL-71-CEX-LATRAISE **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
Cable lateral raises provide constant tension throughout the movement to isolate the medial deltoids, offering a controlled alternative to free weight lateral raises with consistent resistance at all joint angles.

## Key Points
- Uses low pulley cable system to create constant tension on medial deltoids throughout full range of motion
- Performed unilaterally (one arm at a time) with cable crossing body for optimal resistance angle
- Requires core stabilization to resist lateral pull of cable, especially from contralateral obliques
- Cable tension provides controlled eccentric loading that dumbbells cannot match
- Progression focuses on load increases, tempo variations, and range of motion refinements

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics and Muscle Activation
The cable lateral raise is an isolation exercise that primarily targets the medial deltoid through shoulder abduction. Unlike dumbbell variations, the cable system provides constant tension throughout the entire range of motion, including the typically weaker bottom portion of the movement where gravity provides minimal resistance with free weights.

The exercise involves standing perpendicular to a low pulley cable machine, grasping the handle with the arm furthest from the machine. This setup allows the cable to cross the body (either in front or behind), creating an optimal resistance vector for medial deltoid activation. The movement pattern follows a wide arc from the side of the body to approximately shoulder height.

### Primary and Secondary Muscle Recruitment
**Primary Movers:** The medial deltoid serves as the primary mover, responsible for shoulder abduction against the cable's resistance.

**Secondary Movers:** The anterior and posterior deltoids provide assistance, particularly at different portions of the range of motion. The supraspinatus initiates the movement and works throughout, while the upper trapezius becomes more active in the final portion of the range of motion above 90 degrees of abduction.

**Stabilizers:** The exercise demands significant core stabilization [[L4. Core Stability Training]], particularly from the contralateral obliques to resist the lateral pull of the cable. Scapular stabilizers maintain proper shoulder blade position, while the rotator cuff muscles provide glenohumeral joint stability throughout the movement.

### Resistance Profile and Training Benefits
The constant tension provided by the cable system creates a unique resistance profile compared to free weights. This continuous loading challenges the muscle throughout the entire range of motion and provides controlled eccentric resistance during the lowering phase, which is beneficial for muscle development and strength gains.

## Practical Applications

### Setup and Execution
1. **Machine Setup:** Attach D-handle to low pulley cable at lowest setting
2. **Body Position:** Stand sideways to machine, feet shoulder-width apart, core engaged
3. **Grip:** Grasp handle with hand furthest from machine, allowing cable to cross body
4. **Movement:** Raise arm in wide arc to shoulder height, leading with elbow
5. **Control:** Pause briefly at top, then lower slowly against cable tension

### Programming Guidelines
- **Beginner:** 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps per arm
- **Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 8-12 reps per arm with tempo variations
- **Advanced:** 4-5 sets with varying rep ranges and intensity techniques

### Load Progression
Start with lighter weight than dumbbell equivalent due to constant tension. Progress by:
- Increasing weight stack selection [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- Adding sets or reps [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- Implementing tempo modifications (2-second pause at top, 3-second eccentric [[B20. Eccentric Training]])

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ **Using momentum or excessive body lean** - defeats the purpose of constant tension
⚠️ **Excessive upper trap involvement** - creates shrugging motion instead of pure abduction
⚠️ **Incomplete range of motion** - stopping short of parallel or going too high above shoulder level
⚠️ **Uncontrolled eccentric phase** - allowing cable to pull arm down rapidly
⚠️ **Excessive elbow bend** - transforms movement into a rowing pattern

## Progression and Regression Options

### Progressions
- **Load Progression:** Increase weight stack resistance [[B1. Progressive Overload]]
- **Volume Progression:** Add sets/reps [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]
- **Tempo Variations:** Pause reps, slow eccentrics [[B20. Eccentric Training]]
- **Leaning Cable Lateral Raise:** Lean away from machine to alter resistance curve
