### G2. Age-Specific Exercise Recommendations
**ID:** G2-AGE-SPECIFIC-RECOMMENDATIONS
**Created:** 2025-04-24 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
Exercise recommendations must be tailored to age-specific physiological needs and capabilities, with children focusing on movement skills and play, adults on health maintenance, and older adults on functional independence and fall prevention.

## Key Points
- Children and teens need 60+ minutes daily of moderate-to-vigorous activity with emphasis on fun and variety
- Adults require 150-300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly plus 2+ days of strength training
- Older adults need the same base requirements as adults plus 3+ days of balance and functional training
- Resistance training is beneficial at all ages but requires age-appropriate progression and supervision
- Nutritional needs, especially protein, increase with age to preserve muscle mass

## Deep Dive

### Children (5-12 years)
The primary focus during childhood should be developing fundamental movement skills, motor coordination, and bone health while fostering lifelong enjoyment of physical activity. This developmental stage is critical for establishing movement patterns and positive associations with exercise.

**Physiological Considerations:**
- Rapid bone development makes weight-bearing activities essential
- Immature thermoregulatory system requires careful hydration and heat management
- High energy turnover supports multiple daily activity sessions
- Neural plasticity allows rapid skill acquisition

**Training Approach:**
- Emphasize play-based activities and games over structured exercise
- Focus on basic movement patterns: running, jumping, throwing, catching
- Introduce bodyweight exercises and light resistance with perfect technique
- Avoid early sport specialization to prevent overuse injuries and burnout

### Adolescents (13-17 years)
Adolescence brings unique challenges including growth spurts, hormonal changes, and increased social pressures. This period offers opportunities for skill refinement and building a strong fitness foundation.

**Physiological Considerations:**
- Growth spurts can temporarily affect coordination and increase injury risk
- Hormonal changes support muscle and bone development
- Peak height velocity affects training responses
- Increased capacity for structured training protocols

**Training Modifications:**
- Monitor growth patterns and adjust training loads accordingly
- Introduce progressive resistance training with proper supervision
- Address body image concerns with focus on performance over appearance
- Support high nutritional needs for growth and activity

### Adults (18-64 years)
Adult exercise programming centers on maintaining health-related fitness components while preventing chronic diseases. This broad age range requires consideration of varying life stresses and physical capabilities.

**Primary Goals:**
- Cardiovascular disease prevention
- Maintenance of muscle mass and bone density
- Weight management and metabolic health
- Stress reduction and mental health benefits

**Evidence-Based Benefits:**
Research consistently demonstrates that meeting adult activity guidelines reduces risk of cardiovascular disease by 30-35%, type 2 diabetes by 25-30%, and all-cause mortality by 20-25%.

### Older Adults (65+ years)
Exercise programming for older adults must prioritize functional independence while managing age-related physiological changes and potential chronic conditions.

**Age-Related Changes:**
- Sarcopenia: 3-8% muscle mass loss per decade after age 30
- Decreased bone density increasing fracture risk
- Reduced balance and proprioception
- Slower recovery from exercise stress

**Specialized Programming Needs:**
- Multicomponent training combining aerobic, strength, balance, and flexibility
- Power training to maintain functional capacity
- Fall prevention through balance and coordination work
- Modified intensity and longer recovery periods

## Practical Applications

**For Children:**
- Schedule 3-4 different activities per week (swimming, playground time, martial arts, dance)
- Use games like tag, relay races, and obstacle courses
- Limit screen time to encourage active play
- Family activities: hiking, bike rides, active games

**For Adults:**
- Break 150 minutes into manageable chunks: 30 minutes × 5 days or 50 minutes × 3 days
- Combine activities: walk to work, take stairs, weekend sports
- Schedule strength training on non-consecutive days
- Include both planned exercise and lifestyle activities

**For Older Adults:**
- Start with 10-15 minute sessions and gradually increase
- Use stable chairs or walls for balance support initially
- Focus on functional movements: sit-to-stand, carrying groceries, climbing stairs
- Consider group classes for social interaction and motivation

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Over-specializing young athletes** - Can lead to overuse injuries and early burnout instead of well-rounded development

⚠️ **Ignoring age-related recovery needs** - Older adults need longer recovery periods between intense sessions

⚠️ **One-size-fits-all programming** - Failing to adjust exercise prescription for developmental stage and individual capabilities

⚠️ **Neglecting balance training in older adults** - Falls are a leading cause of injury; balance work is not optional

⚠️ **Assuming children need less structure** - While emphasizing fun, children still benefit from progressive skill development
