### **EXL-44: Dumbbell Floor Press**

## Quick Takeaway
The dumbbell floor press is a bench press variation performed on the floor that reduces shoulder stress through limited range of motion while emphasizing triceps strength and lockout power.

## Key Points
- Limited range of motion stops when upper arms contact the floor, reducing shoulder stress compared to full-range pressing
- Emphasizes triceps and lockout strength more than chest development due to reduced stretch
- Provides independent arm training with dumbbells, allowing for unilateral strength development
- Serves as an effective regression from full bench press or progression from push-ups
- Floor contact provides natural stopping point, making it safer for solo training

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics and Muscle Activation
The dumbbell floor press restricts the eccentric (lowering) phase when the upper arms contact the floor, typically reducing range of motion by 2-4 inches compared to bench pressing. This limitation shifts emphasis toward the mid-to-top portion of the press, where triceps contribution is greatest.

**Primary muscle activation:**
- Triceps brachii (emphasized due to lockout focus)
- Pectoralis major (mid/lower fibers less activated due to reduced stretch)
- Anterior deltoid (moderate activation throughout range)

**Secondary and stabilizing muscles:**
- Rotator cuff muscles for shoulder stability
- Core musculature for spinal stability [[L4. Core Stability Training]]
- Serratus anterior for scapular control

### Setup and Execution Technique
**Starting Position:**
- Lie supine on floor with knees bent, feet flat
- Hold dumbbells with neutral or pronated grip at chest level
- Position upper arms at 45-75 degree angle from torso (avoid excessive flare)
- Maintain natural spinal curves

**Movement Execution:**
- Brace core and maintain stable shoulder position
- Press dumbbells vertically until full arm extension
- Control descent until upper arms lightly contact floor
- Pause briefly at bottom without relaxing muscles
- Maintain consistent elbow angle throughout movement

### Breathing Pattern
- Exhale during concentric (pressing) phase
- Inhale during eccentric (lowering) phase
- Hold breath briefly during transition at floor contact

## Research Evidence

**Saeterbakken, A.H., et al. (2019).** The Effects of Bench Press Variations in Competitive Athletes on Muscle Activity and Performance. *Journal of Human Kinetics*, 68(1), 75-84. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2019-0065
- Key finding: Floor press variations showed 15-20% greater triceps activation compared to full-range bench press
- Study type: Observational (EMG analysis)
- Evidence quality: Medium (controlled conditions, limited sample size)

**Tillaar, R., & Saeterbakken, A. (2014).** Effect of fatigue upon performance and electromyographic activity in 6-RM bench press. *Journal of Human Kinetics*, 40(1), 57-65. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2014-0007
- Key finding: Reduced range of motion pressing maintained higher force output in fatigued state
- Study type: Experimental study
- Evidence quality: Medium (small sample, specific population)

## Practical Applications

**Programming Recommendations:**
- **Beginner/Intermediate:** 3-4 sets of 8-12 reps
- **Advanced/Strength Focus:** 4-5 sets of 3-6 reps
- **Volume:** 2-3 times per week with adequate recovery

**Load Selection:**
- Start with 10-15% less weight than dumbbell bench press
- Progress load by 2.5-5 lbs per dumbbell when completing all sets/reps
- Use spotter or safety setup when training near maximum loads

**Specific Applications:**
- Shoulder rehabilitation (reduced stress position)
- Triceps strength development
- Lockout strength for bench press improvement
- Home training alternative to bench press

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Excessive elbow flare** - Keep elbows at 45-75 degrees from torso to protect shoulders

⚠️ **Bouncing off floor** - Control descent and pause briefly without relaxing muscles

⚠️ **Incomplete lockout** - Press to full arm extension for maximum triceps activation

⚠️ **Poor dumbbell positioning** - Start with weights at chest level, not extended overhead

⚠️ **Unstable core** - Maintain braced core throughout movement for spinal stability
