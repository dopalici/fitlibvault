### **EXL-78: Cable Glute Kickback**

**ID:** EXL-78-CEX-GLUTEKICKBACK **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
An isolation exercise using cable resistance to target the gluteus maximus through controlled hip extension while maintaining core stability.

## Key Points
- Primarily targets gluteus maximus through hip extension movement pattern
- Requires ankle strap attachment and low pulley cable setup for resistance
- Standing position challenges core stability while isolating glute activation
- Movement should originate from hip joint, not lower back hyperextension
- Suitable for beginners to intermediate trainees with proper form coaching

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics
The cable glute kickback is a unilateral hip extension exercise performed in a standing position. The cable provides constant tension throughout the range of motion, requiring the gluteus maximus to work against resistance in both concentric (kickback) and eccentric (return) phases. The standing position adds a stability component, engaging core musculature and the supporting leg's stabilizers.

### Setup and Equipment
Position yourself facing the cable machine with an ankle strap attached to the low pulley. The working leg should have enough clearance to extend backward without hitting the machine or weight stack. Maintain a slight forward lean while holding the machine lightly for balance, creating optimal positioning for hip extension.

### Execution Technique
Begin with the working leg in a neutral position, core engaged, and slight bend in the supporting knee. Initiate movement by extending the hip backward, keeping the knee relatively straight and focusing on glute contraction. The movement should be controlled and deliberate, avoiding momentum or excessive lower back involvement. Peak contraction occurs when the leg reaches maximum hip extension without compensatory lumbar hyperextension.

### Common Technical Errors
Excessive lumbar hyperextension is the most frequent error, often resulting from inadequate core engagement or attempting to achieve greater range of motion. Other common mistakes include using momentum to swing the leg, insufficient glute activation, and converting the movement into a hamstring curl by excessive knee flexion.

## Practical Applications

**Programming Guidelines:**
- Beginner: 2-3 sets of 12-15 reps per leg with light resistance
- Intermediate: 3-4 sets of 10-12 reps with moderate resistance
- Advanced: 3-4 sets of 8-10 reps with heavier resistance or tempo variations

**Load Progression:**
- Start with minimal weight to master movement pattern
- Increase resistance by 2.5-5 lbs when able to complete all sets with perfect form
- Focus on quality of contraction over absolute load lifted

**Tempo Variations:**
- Standard: 2-1-2-1 (2 seconds up, 1 second pause, 2 seconds down, 1 second pause)
- Emphasis: 1-3-3-1 (quick up, 3-second hold, slow return)

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Arching the lower back excessively to achieve greater range of motion
⚠️ Using momentum or swinging the leg instead of controlled muscle contraction
⚠️ Bending the knee too much, converting the exercise into a hamstring curl
⚠️ Insufficient glute activation due to poor mind-muscle connection
⚠️ Starting with too much weight before mastering proper form
