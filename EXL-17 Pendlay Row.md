### **EXL-17: Pendlay Row**

**ID:** EXL-17-BB-PENDLAYROW **Created:** 2025-04-29 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
The Pendlay Row is an explosive barbell rowing variation that starts from a dead stop on the floor each rep, eliminating momentum to maximize upper back muscle activation and power development.

## Key Points
- Barbell returns to complete dead stop on floor between each repetition, removing stretch reflex
- Requires explosive concentric contraction from static position, developing power and strength
- Torso remains parallel to floor throughout movement with strict spinal neutrality
- Primarily targets latissimus dorsi, rhomboids, and middle/lower trapezius with high stabilizer demands
- Advanced exercise requiring mastery of hip hinge mechanics and excellent spinal control

## Deep Dive

### Movement Mechanics and Muscle Activation
The Pendlay Row distinguishes itself from standard bent-over rows through its dead-stop methodology. Each repetition begins with the barbell resting completely on the floor, requiring the lifter to generate force from zero velocity. This eliminates the stretch-shortening cycle present in continuous rowing motions, placing greater emphasis on concentric strength and explosive power development [[B4. Strength Training Principles]].

The exercise demands exceptional isometric strength from the posterior chain. The hamstrings and glutes maintain the hip hinge position while the erector spinae provides rigid spinal stability throughout the movement [[L1. Movement Patterns and Functional Training]]. Core musculature must work intensively to prevent spinal flexion under load [[L4. Core Stability Training]].

### Primary Movement Pattern
The Pendlay Row follows a strict pulling pattern with the torso maintaining a near-parallel angle to the floor. The barbell travels vertically from floor to lower chest/upper abdomen, with elbows driving the movement rather than the arms. This positioning optimizes recruitment of the latissimus dorsi, rhomboids, and middle/lower trapezius while engaging the posterior deltoids as synergists [[G-VII-1. Musculoskeletal Anatomy Overview]].

### Technical Execution Standards
Proper execution requires maintaining torso angle throughout the lift - any significant lifting of the chest indicates compensation and reduces target muscle activation. The explosive nature of the movement demands maximum acceleration on each concentric phase, followed by controlled eccentric lowering to the floor [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]].

## Practical Applications

**Programming Guidelines:**
- Perform for 3-6 repetitions per set due to explosive nature
- Rest 2-3 minutes between sets to allow full recovery for power output
- Use 70-85% of standard bent-over row weight initially
- Focus on maximum acceleration rather than absolute load

**Technical Progression:**
1. Master standard bent-over row with strict form
2. Practice dead-stop position holds for 3-5 seconds
3. Perform slow eccentric Pendlay rows (3-second lower)
4. Progress to explosive concentric with controlled eccentric
5. Advance to competition-style explosive execution

**Load Selection:**
- Begin with 60-70% of bent-over row 1RM
- Prioritize bar speed over absolute weight
- Increase load only when maintaining explosive velocity

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ **Torso Rising:** Lifting chest during pull reduces target muscle activation and indicates excessive load
⚠️ **Bouncing Bar:** Allowing bar to bounce off floor negates dead-stop benefits and increases injury risk  
⚠️ **Rounded Spine:** Loss of neutral spine position under load creates dangerous shear forces
⚠️ **Arm-Dominant Pull:** Using biceps instead of back muscles reduces exercise effectiveness
⚠️ **Inadequate Hip Hinge:** Insufficient hip flexibility limits proper torso positioning
