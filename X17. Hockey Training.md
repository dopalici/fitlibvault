### X17. Hockey Training
**ID:** X17-HOCKEY **Created:** 2025-06-05

Hockey training integrates anaerobic power, core stability, and multidirectional movement patterns to optimize performance for high-intensity shifts, skating mechanics, and puck handling while maintaining balance and power on ice. Training emphasizes lateral movement patterns and rotational core strength unique to skating sports.

**Core Components:**
- **Anaerobic Power and Lactate Tolerance:** High-intensity interval training ([[E3. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)]]) with work-to-rest ratios mimicking hockey shifts (45-90 seconds work, 2-4 minutes rest) to improve repeated sprint ability ([[A6. Energy Systems (Detailed)]]).
- **Lateral Movement and Skating-Specific Strength:** Single-leg exercises ([[EXL-38 Dumbbell Split Squat]]), lateral lunges, and hip abduction/adduction strength ([[EXL-63 Hip Abduction (Machine)]]) to support skating stride mechanics and injury prevention.
- **Rotational Core Power:** Medicine ball throws, cable woodchops ([[EXL-76 Cable Woodchop (High-to-Low & Low-to-High)]]), and anti-rotation exercises ([[EXL-77 Cable Pallof Press (Anti-Rotation)]]) for shooting power and maintaining balance during contact ([[L4. Core Stability Training]]).
- **Upper Body Power for Shooting and Checking:** Plyometric upper body exercises, Olympic pulling variations ([[B15. Olympic Weightlifting]]), and unilateral strength training to develop asymmetrical power for stick handling and body checking ([[B4. Strength Training Principles]]).
- **Balance and Proprioception:** Single-leg stability work ([[L3. Balance and Coordination Training]]) and unstable surface training to improve on-ice balance, edge control, and injury prevention in the unstable skating environment.
