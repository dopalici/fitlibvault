### **EXL-48: Seated Leg Curl (Machine)**

**ID:** EXL-48-MEX-LEGCURL-SEATED **Created:** 2025-04-30 (Updated: 2026-05-08)

## Quick Takeaway
A beginner-friendly machine isolation exercise that targets the hamstrings through knee flexion while seated, providing stable support and controlled resistance.

## Key Points
- Isolates the hamstring muscles (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus) through knee flexion
- Machine provides stability and controlled resistance path, making it ideal for beginners
- Allows for precise load progression and unilateral training to address imbalances
- Requires proper machine setup with knee joint aligned to machine's axis of rotation
- Can be progressed through load, volume, tempo variations, or advancement to free weight hamstring exercises

## Deep Dive

### Target Muscles and Movement Pattern
The seated leg curl is an isolation exercise performed on a machine [[B10. Machine Training]] that specifically targets the hamstring muscle group through knee flexion. The primary movers include all three hamstring muscles: biceps femoris (both short and long heads), semitendinosus, and semimembranosus. Secondary assistance comes from the gastrocnemius, popliteus, sartorius, and gracilis muscles. The core musculature [[L4. Core Stability Training]] and hip flexors act as stabilizers to maintain proper seated position throughout the movement.

### Proper Setup and Execution
Following proper lifting mechanics [[A18. Proper Lifting Mechanics]], the exercise begins with careful machine adjustment. The knee joint must align with the machine's axis of rotation, and the ankle pad should rest comfortably just above the ankles. The back should remain flat against the pad with hands gripping the handles for stability.

During execution, exhale while flexing the knees to curl the ankle pad toward the glutes in a smooth, controlled motion. Focus on contracting the hamstrings through the full available range of motion, pausing briefly at peak contraction. The eccentric phase involves slowly returning the pad to the starting position while inhaling, maintaining control and avoiding letting the weight stack touch down between repetitions to preserve muscle tension.

### Progression and Regression Options
The exercise follows standard progressive overload principles [[B1. Progressive Overload]] through increased load, repetitions, or sets [[B5. Training Volume and Frequency]]. Advanced variations include tempo manipulations with pauses at peak contraction or slow eccentrics [[B20. Eccentric Training]], and single-leg variations to address muscular imbalances.

For progression beyond the machine, practitioners can advance to lying leg curls [[EXL-49 Lying Leg Curl (Machine)]] for different muscle activation patterns, or progress to free weight exercises like Romanian deadlifts [[EXL-09 Barbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]] or [[EXL-37 Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift (RDL)]], and glute-ham raises [[EXL-87 Glute-Ham Raise (GHR)]].

Regression options include decreased load, resistance band hamstring curls [[B11. Resistance Bands]], stability ball hamstring curls, or glute bridges [[B7. Bodyweight Training]] for less direct hamstring isolation.

## Practical Applications
- **Beginner Programs**: Start with 2-3 sets of 12-15 repetitions at a weight allowing full range of motion
- **Hypertrophy Focus**: 3-4 sets of 8-12 repetitions with progressive load increases following hypertrophy training principles [[B3. Hypertrophy Training Principles]]
- **Imbalance Correction**: Perform single-leg variations, starting with the weaker leg
- **Load Progression**: Increase weight by 2.5-5 lbs when all sets can be completed with proper form
- **Tempo Variations**: 3-second eccentric phase or 2-second pause at peak contraction

## Common Mistakes
⚠️ Using excessive momentum instead of controlled muscle contraction
⚠️ Lifting hips off the seat pad, reducing exercise effectiveness
⚠️ Incomplete range of motion, not fully contracting or extending
⚠️ Allowing the weight stack to touch down between reps, losing muscle tension
⚠️ Improper machine setup with misaligned knee joint and rotation axis
