### C5. Pre, During, and Post-Exercise Nutrition
**ID:** C5-PRE-DURING-POST-EXERCISE
**Created:** 2025-04-24 (Updated: 2025-01-27)

## Quick Takeaway
Strategic timing of carbs, protein, and fluids around workouts can enhance performance and accelerate recovery, with timing becoming more critical for longer sessions and shorter recovery windows.

## Key Points
- Pre-workout: Eat carbs (1-4 g/kg) and protein (0.3-0.5 g/kg) 1-4 hours before training
- During workout: Only needed for sessions >60-90 minutes; consume 30-90g carbs per hour with electrolytes
- Post-workout: Combine carbs (0.8-1.2 g/kg) with protein (20-40g) within 2 hours, prioritizing faster intake when recovery time is short
- Focus on easily digestible foods pre-workout and avoid high fat/fiber close to training
- Hydration and electrolyte replacement are critical during all phases

## Deep Dive

Strategic nutrient intake immediately before, during, and after exercise sessions can influence fuel availability, performance during the session, and the rate/effectiveness of recovery afterwards. This approach builds on general [[C3. Nutrient Timing]] principles with specific focus on the exercise window.

### Pre-Exercise Nutrition (1-4 hours before)

**Primary Goals:**
- Maximize muscle and liver glycogen stores
- Ensure optimal hydration status
- Prevent hunger during exercise
- Provide circulating amino acids for muscle protein synthesis

**Nutritional Strategy:**
The pre-exercise meal should emphasize easily digestible carbohydrates (1-4 g/kg body weight) combined with moderate protein (0.3-0.5 g/kg body weight). Fat and fiber should be minimized to reduce gastrointestinal distress and slow gastric emptying.

Timing considerations: Larger meals require 3-4 hours for digestion, while smaller snacks can be consumed 1-2 hours before exercise. Individual tolerance varies significantly and should be practiced during training.

### Intra-Workout Nutrition (During Exercise)

**When It's Needed:**
Intra-workout nutrition becomes beneficial for exercise sessions lasting longer than 60-90 minutes, particularly for endurance activities or high-intensity intermittent sports.

**Nutritional Strategy:**
- Carbohydrates: 30-90 g/hour depending on exercise duration and intensity
- Multiple carb sources (glucose + fructose) can increase absorption rates up to 90 g/hour
- Electrolytes, especially sodium (200-500 mg/hour), to maintain fluid balance
- Fluid intake: 150-250 ml every 15-20 minutes

For sessions under 60 minutes, water alone is typically sufficient unless training in extreme heat or the athlete has high sweat rates.

### Post-Exercise Nutrition (0-2+ hours after)

**Primary Goals:**
- Replenish muscle and liver glycogen stores
- Stimulate muscle protein synthesis (MPS)
- Rehydrate and restore electrolyte balance
- Support immune function recovery

**Nutritional Strategy:**
The post-exercise window should combine carbohydrates (0.8-1.2 g/kg body weight) with high-quality protein (0.3-0.5 g/kg or 20-40g total). The urgency of nutrient timing increases when recovery time between sessions is limited (<8 hours).

For glycogen replenishment, higher glycemic index carbohydrates are preferred immediately post-exercise, with the addition of protein enhancing both glycogen resynthesis and muscle protein synthesis.

## Research Evidence

**Key Position Statements:**
- Kerksick CM, et al. (2017). International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: nutrient timing. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 14, 33. DOI: 10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4
- Burke LM, et al. (2011). Carbohydrates and fat for training and recovery. Journal of Sports Sciences, 29 Suppl 1, S17-27. DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2011.585473

**Evidence Quality:**
Strong evidence supports carbohydrate intake for sessions >60 minutes and post-exercise carb-protein combinations for glycogen resynthesis. Moderate evidence exists for pre-exercise timing specificity, with high individual variation in tolerance and response.

## Practical Applications

**Pre-Workout Examples:**
- 3-4 hours before: Oatmeal with banana and Greek yogurt
- 1-2 hours before: Toast with honey and small amount of peanut butter
- 30-60 minutes before: Sports drink or easily digestible fruit

**During Workout Examples:**
- Endurance training: Sports drink providing 30-60g carbs/hour
- Team sports: Diluted fruit juice with added sodium
- Ultra-endurance: Combination of liquid and solid carb sources

**Post-Workout Examples:**
- Chocolate milk (optimal carb:protein ratio)
- Protein shake with banana
- Greek yogurt with berries and granola

## Common Mistakes

⚠️ Trying new nutrition strategies on competition day instead of practicing during training
⚠️ Consuming high-fat or high-fiber foods too close to exercise, causing GI distress
⚠️ Neglecting electrolyte replacement during longer sessions, leading to hyponatremia risk
⚠️ Overcomplicating nutrition for short sessions (<60 min) where timing is less critical
⚠️ Ignoring individual tolerance - what works for others may not work for you
